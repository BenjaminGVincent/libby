    Research question¶

    In treatment-naive metastatic high-grade chondrosarcoma with dedifferentiated features, presenting with a pathologic left acetabular fracture and dispersed bilateral pulmonary metastases, what non-standard interventions could target the dedifferentiated histology and the untested IDH1/IDH2 and wider molecular landscape, gated on a comprehensive tumour DNA plus RNA panel that has not yet been ordered? Approved and guideline-carried care for the same presentation is assessed on the Standard-of-care table, a co-equal surface on this same case page.

    Patient profile (scrubbed)¶

    • Woman, age band 40-49. ECOG recorded as 2.
    • The ECOG is an assumption, not a measurement. It was recorded because a pathologic fracture of a weight-bearing joint plausibly limits ambulation and self-care. The true value may be better or worse, and it gates trial eligibility broadly.
    • Chondrosarcoma, high-grade with dedifferentiated features (user-stated; a formal pathology report has not been reviewed). Expert bone-sarcoma pathology review to confirm the WHO diagnosis and quantify the dedifferentiated component is a hardening item, not an open fork.
    • Stage IV, metastatic at diagnosis: large left acetabular mass with pathologic acetabular fracture, plus multiple small pulmonary nodules dispersed through both lungs. Multiple and dispersed rather than oligometastatic.
    • Treatment-naive. No prior therapies, no current therapy.
    • IDH1 mutation — untested (not performed). IDH2 mutation — untested.
    • Comprehensive genomic profile — untested: no NGS, no RNA fusion panel, no COL2A1 / TERT promoter / CDKN2A-B / TP53 status.
    • MSI / dMMR — untested. TMB — untested. PD-L1 — untested.
    • HEY1-NCOA2 fusion — untested; low-yield given the stated histology, which is not mesenchymal chondrosarcoma.
    • Organ function: renal panel, hepatic panel and baseline echo/LVEF all not obtained. Pain and weight-bearing status not documented.

    Target validation paths¶

    Two orders decide most of what this case can become, and both ride the archival diagnostic block: IDH1/IDH2 sequencing, and a comprehensive tumour DNA plus RNA panel read on macrodissected dedifferentiated tissue. A confirmed IDH1 or IDH2 mutation opens an off-label conversation about ivosidenib or olutasidenib rather than a trial seat, because the active chondrosarcoma IDH studies are closed to this histology (the phase 3 ivosidenib trial NCT06127407 enrolls conventional histology only); a wild-type result closes that axis cleanly and leaves the rest of the workup standing. The same panel's TMB, MSI and CDK-pathway readouts decide whether tumour-agnostic pembrolizumab, the abemaciclib study NCT04040205 and TAPUR (NCT02693535) matching are reachable at all. If the panel comes back quiet, which chondrosarcoma genomes often do beyond IDH, the molecular half of this report is exhausted, and the conversation about standard systemic care from there belongs to the treating team, not Libby.

    IDH1/IDH2¶

    Order sequencing, not R132H immunohistochemistry. The R132H antibody was raised for glioma, and roughly 40 percent of IDH1-mutant cartilage tumours carry R132C instead (PMID 21598255), so a stain would read negative in the commonest mutant scenario here. The pre-test probability is high: 71 percent of dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas carried an IDH1/IDH2 mutation in a paired genomic series, against 36 percent of conventional tumours (PMID 36926116). A targeted hotspot assay answers in 3-10 days; on the comprehensive panel the same answer takes 2-3 weeks. Two sampling rules matter as much as the assay. Macrodissect the dedifferentiated component, and require the report to state which lesion was sampled, because mutant IDH1 is recurrently lost during metastatic evolution (PMID 41299743) and a call on the acetabular primary may not describe the lung nodules. Plasma ctDNA (two Streck tubes, 7-10 days) is the fallback when the block is exhausted, and the shedding data are favourable in exactly this population: digital PCR found circulating tumour DNA in all six patients with grade 3 or dedifferentiated disease (PMID 28834325). Whatever comes back, temper the expectations that travel with the word "mutation": the widely quoted ivosidenib response rate belongs to conventional-histology patients, and olutasidenib in dedifferentiated disease read out at a median PFS of 1.5 months.

    Dedifferentiated histology¶

    The histology on file is user-stated, and it is the fact that makes cytotoxic chemotherapy worth giving, so hardening it is essential rather than optional. Expert bone-sarcoma pathology review should report the formal WHO diagnosis and the percentage of the specimen occupied by the dedifferentiated, non-cartilaginous component; it runs 5-10 business days on the existing slides and block, no new biopsy. Ancillary subtyping earns its slot because the differential changes the chemotherapy backbone: HEY1-NCOA2 fusion testing on an RNA panel separates mesenchymal chondrosarcoma, and morphology plus the IDH genotype separates chondroblastic osteosarcoma, which would move treatment toward a MAP-type regimen. Two baseline studies stand between her and an anthracycline: a transthoracic echocardiogram with LVEF plus a 12-lead ECG with QTc (1-3 days), and an organ-function panel with creatinine clearance and urinalysis before any ifosfamide or cisplatin.

    Molecular landscape¶

    Everything molecular in this case runs off one block, and dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma is biphasic by definition, so the pathology block review with macrodissection instructions and a written tissue-allocation plan comes before anything is cut. A core that sampled only the low-grade cartilage returns a falsely reassuring result on every assay downstream of it. The comprehensive DNA plus RNA panel (IDH1/IDH2, TP53, CDKN2A/B, TERT promoter, NTRK1-3, RET, BRAF, TMB, MSI, fusions; 2-3 weeks) is the single order that answers the IDH question and every tumour-agnostic one at once. Around it sit the fast reads: four-antibody mismatch-repair IHC (2-5 days off four unstained slides) as the orthogonal check on the panel's MSI call, which would put tumour-agnostic pembrolizumab or dostarlimab in reach if protein loss appears, though MSI-high showed up in only 0.7 percent of sarcomas in the largest sequenced cohort (PMID 42531431); PD-L1 IHC with the clone and scoring system recorded explicitly, read on the dedifferentiated component, since positivity in this disease is confined to that subtype (9 of 22 dedifferentiated against 0 of 119 conventional, PMID 27312065); and CD3/CD8 density with tumour HLA class I expression as context for any checkpoint or cell-therapy discussion. A germline predisposition panel is a reflex, ordered only if the tumour panel returns a BRCA1, BRCA2, PALB2 or TP53 variant at an allele fraction suggesting germline origin.

    Fitness, fracture and measurable disease¶

    The recorded ECOG of 2 is an intake assumption made from the fracture, not a score, and it decides more than any single molecular result: chemotherapy intensity, and eligibility for every protocol that caps performance status, including the ECOG 0-1 bars on the IACS-6274 study NCT05039801 and the abemaciclib study NCT04040205. Scoring it in clinic is free and same-day; record weight-bearing status, ambulatory aids and analgesic requirement alongside it. Orthopaedic oncology assessment of the periacetabular fracture, with thin-slice bone-algorithm pelvic CT and MRI of the hemipelvis, settles whether fixation precedes or accompanies systemic therapy and starts the surgical-washout clock that trial protocols read. A baseline contrast CT of chest, abdomen and pelvis with thin lung reconstructions establishes RECIST 1.1 measurability, which protocol entry generally requires, and answers whether the bilateral nodule distribution truly forecloses any local pulmonary approach.

    HLA class I and the cancer-testis antigens¶

    One blood draw that spends no tumour tissue: allele-level (4-digit) HLA class I typing (1-2 weeks) decides whether any HLA-restricted TCR-T or ImmTAC programme, afamitresgene autoleucel, uzatresgene autoleucel, IMA203, brenetafusp or letetresgene autoleucel, can be pursued at all. The antigen stains behind it are reflexes, not parallel orders: MAGE-A4, NY-ESO-1 and PRAME expression are worth measuring only after a documented HLA-A*02:01, because an expression result cannot be read for eligibility without the restricting allele. Hold expectations low even then; in the largest cancer-testis antigen survey of bone and soft tissue tumours, 1 of 13 chondrosarcomas stained PRAME-positive (PMID 40152485).

    Biomarkers measured, but not to decision resolution¶

    This case has no under-resolved results, because it has no results: nothing molecular has been measured at all, and the survey behind this report handed four items forward on exactly that basis rather than because an existing number fell short. HLA class I genotype is simply absent, and it is the one that stacks: MAGE-A4, NY-ESO-1 and PRAME are recorded as not assessable rather than negative, since two gaps sit on top of each other for each of them, an unknown restricting allele and an expression level nobody has stained for. Each of the four has a matching workup row above, and the ordering dependency is the whole story: type first, stain second, and read any expression result on the dedifferentiated component.

    Workup considerations¶

    The rank-1 row below is one order out of seven that the target validator marked essential and intervention-gating. The other six are not therapeutic options and do not belong in a ranking, but the treating team needs them on the same page:

    • Expert bone-sarcoma pathology review of the diagnostic material, reporting the WHO diagnosis and the percentage of the specimen occupied by the dedifferentiated, non-cartilaginous component. The stated histology already answers the chemotherapy fork in the affirmative; this hardens it.
    • A documented ECOG scored in clinic, recorded with weight-bearing status, ambulatory aids and analgesic requirement. Performance status of 2 or worse was independently associated with shorter PFS in the French Sarcoma Group cohort (PMID 24099780), so this is prognosis as much as paperwork.
    • Baseline transthoracic echocardiogram with LVEF, plus a 12-lead ECG with QTc, and a baseline organ-function panel with creatinine clearance and urinalysis.
    • Orthopaedic oncology assessment of the periacetabular fracture with thin-slice bone-algorithm pelvic CT and MRI of the hemipelvis, addressing stabilisation and its timing against systemic therapy.
    • Pathology block review for cellularity and dedifferentiated-component representation, with macrodissection instructions and a written tissue-allocation plan across the panel, the IHC set and any reflex stains. Tissue is finite and several pending assays compete for it.
    • Four-antibody mismatch-repair IHC, PD-L1 IHC on the dedifferentiated component, and allele-level HLA class I typing are high-priority reads that inform rather than gate the rank-1 order, but each governs a row further down this table.

    Two sequencing rules carry over from the board and are not negotiable in the dossier's own terms. Fixation of the acetabulum comes before any radiotherapy to that bone (both are assessed on the Standard-of-care table on this page), on the atrophic non-union mechanism (PMID 23606416), co-signed by the conservative and by concensusite.     Top interventions¶

    Rank 1. Comprehensive tumour DNA plus RNA panel on macrodissected dedifferentiated tissue — diagnostic gate¶

    One order that resolves IDH1/IDH2, TMB, MSI and CDK-pathway status; it gates three rows below, the abemaciclib and TAPUR screens, the pembrolizumab label question, and the ivosidenib row on the Standard-of-care table.

    Evidence base¶

    This is an assay, so the evidence is prevalence and eligibility rather than an effect size. IDH1/2 mutations occur in 71% of dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas against 36% of conventional tumours, measured on macrodissected material (PMID 36926116), which makes the pre-test probability the cleanest number in this file. The same paper cautions that the IDH-associated methylation phenotype is partially lost at dedifferentiation. Downstream, the panel is what NCT04040205 screens on for a qualifying CDK-pathway alteration, what NCT02693535 needs to assign a marketed agent, and what would answer the tumour-agnostic TMB and MSI question. Tissue choice is a real variable rather than a formality: mutant IDH1 is recurrently lost during metastatic evolution (PMID 41299743).

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    As a gate it either opens doors or closes them, and the honest expectation is mixed. A positive IDH call is likely on prevalence and buys trial currency plus an off-label conversation, not the targeted rescue the word suggests. A qualifying CDK-pathway alteration or a TMB-high result is a much longer shot; chondrosarcoma genomes are often quiet beyond IDH. What makes the order worth ranking first anyway is that a negative panel closes the IDH axis cleanly, here and on the Standard-of-care table, and closing options honestly is worth more here than keeping them ambiguous.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • No procedural risk. The panel rides archival diagnostic tissue.
    • The only cost is time and tissue. 2-3 weeks for the comprehensive panel, 3-10 days for a targeted IDH hotspot assay, against metastatic medians of 5 to 7 months.
    • Tissue is finite and several assays compete for it, which is why the block review and written allocation plan sit alongside this order.
    • No user toxicity veto to hit; the veto list is empty.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    Four of five personas ranked this and all four gave it their highest confidence, which happened nowhere else on the board. The risktaker declined to rank it, arguing that the glutaminase route does not wait on an IDH result because cell-line sensitivity did not sort by genotype (PMID 29576625); concensusite conceded that point directly in round 2. That objection narrows the claim rather than defeating the order. concensusite also pre-committed against the thing a positive result will create pressure for: outside a trial she would not support off-label ivosidenib here, and she preferred to say so before the report lands rather than after.

    Practical considerations¶

    Send it with the diagnostic tissue, not after the first restaging scan. Require macrodissection of the dedifferentiated component and require the report to state which lesion was sampled. If an operative or metastatic specimen becomes available, test that too. The assay itself is a routine clinical order, which is why its access route reads standard care; the row sits in this table because the gating contract puts the confirmatory workup at rank 1 of the ranking it gates. concensusite could not read NCCN Bone Cancer v2.2025 (PMID 40203873) or the ESMO-EURACAN bone sarcoma guideline (PMID 34500044) beyond their abstracts, and declined to claim either lists IDH testing in the chondrosarcoma workup algorithm. No category is asserted.

    Why this rank¶

    It holds the top slot as this table's diagnostic gate, not as the case's best therapy; that is doxorubicin-based chemotherapy, which sits on the Standard-of-care table with the board's only unanimous agreement score (1.0) behind it. The panel goes first here because it is a same-week order with no safety cost that rides the diagnostic tissue, because a biomarker gate cannot be cleared retroactively, and because neither it nor the chemotherapy waits for the other.

    Rank 2. IACS-6274 (glutaminase-1 inhibitor), phase 1 NCT05039801 — make the screening call¶

    The one open protocol that writes chondrosarcoma into its own treatment-naive population. What is ranked is the phone call and the screening answer, not the drug.

    Evidence base¶

    The whole clinical base is a registration record. NCT05039801 names chondrosarcoma as its own Part A dose-escalation population with no subtype restriction and no prior-therapy requirement for that group, which is rare enough that it is worth a call on its own. Underneath sits one paper: glutaminase expression rose with tumour grade across 120 cartilage tumours (p=0.001, highest in high-grade disease) and all four tested agents had activity in 10 chondrosarcoma cell lines, but sensitivity did not track IDH1/2 status and there is no in-vivo chondrosarcoma arm (PMID 29576625). No glutaminase inhibitor has a reported response in any chondrosarcoma patient anywhere. The null half of that paper is doing real work here: because sensitivity did not sort by genotype, a pending IDH result does not gate this protocol, which concensusite conceded explicitly in round 2.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Low, and the advocate said so in her own row rather than leaving it to the critic. Expected benefit from escalation-phase monotherapy is small, and she could be assigned a dose level nobody involved expects to work. The upside is being the escalation patient the drug turns out to work for. What is likelier to matter is the screening answer itself, which costs a phone call and arrives in days.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • No published grade 3+ profile in any chondrosarcoma patient. Adverse-event incidence is the trial's own primary endpoint.
    • Entry requires ECOG 0-1 with no deterioration over the preceding fortnight. This is where the assumed ECOG of 2 bites: the assumption has to be replaced by a measurement, and the measurement has to come back better, before a screening visit is worth the trip.

    The risktaker ranked the drug at 2; the advocate ranked the call at 4; concensusite endorsed the advocate's version and said plainly that it was the row she should have written herself. The critic dissented against the risktaker's rank and qualified the advocate's, on the same ground both times: one cell-line paper, no in-vivo chondrosarcoma arm, no clinical result for the class in this disease. The critic's phrasing is worth keeping, that ranking the phone call rather than the drug is the defensible version of this. The conservative's broader objection lands here too: everything below chemotherapy on the risktaker's slate carries zero chondrosarcoma clinical results, and this is one of them. Mechanistically the risk is that glutamine dependence in this tumour is inferred from grade-linked expression in dishes and has never been tested in an animal carrying chondrosarcoma.

    Rank 3. Abemaciclib on NCT04040205, pre-positioned for the post-anthracycline line¶

    The cleanest trial design in the screen, sitting behind three untested gates.

    Evidence base¶

    No CDK4/6 inhibitor has a published chondrosarcoma efficacy result. NCT04040205 is a two-step single-arm phase 2, n=44 across a mixed sarcoma population, with dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma named as its own eligible histology and CDKN2A/B loss qualifying as the biomarker. The preclinical case is two papers: CDK4 expression correlated with metastasis and worse prognosis in human samples, and palbociclib cut intraosseous chondrosarcoma burden in vivo with the G1 arrest the mechanism predicts (PMID 30808351); p16 overexpression and CDK4 knockdown both inhibited growth, with pRb-pathway alterations tracking progression from enchondroma (PMID 18624751). The critic's caveat on the first of those is load-bearing: it never reported CDKN2A status of its lines and carries a 2023 figure correction (PMID 36803557), so the biomarker inference does not rest on it.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Low to unknown, and the honest framing is the critic's: this ranks on the quality of the process rather than on any signal, and the two are not the same thing. Even a positive readout from a single-arm n=44 will not separate drug effect from having selected an indolent CDK-altered subset. Against that, the target is one of the recurrent alterations in this subtype and the trial gate is applied before enrolment rather than after the fact.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • Class profile: diarrhoea, neutropenia, fatigue and venous thromboembolism. No chondrosarcoma-specific adverse-event table exists anywhere in this dossier.
    • ECOG 0-1, stricter than most protocols in this screen. The assumed 2 excludes her as written.
    • A 14-day post-surgical window with no significant wound-healing problems, more forgiving than the usual 4 weeks if stabilisation goes ahead.
    • No user toxicity veto to hit.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    Both the risktaker and the critic ranked it and neither dissented, which for this pair is itself informative: they arrived from opposite directions and landed on the same protocol. The risktaker ranked the biology, the critic ranked the design and explicitly refused the biology. Neither argued for delaying first-line treatment to chase the seat. The mechanism-level risk is that CDK4/6 inhibition is cytostatic rather than cytotoxic, so Rb-null or CDK-independent clones keep cycling despite target engagement, which is also why the protocol's central Rb IHC gate exists.

    Practical considerations¶

    Recruiting. Three gates stand between her and a seat: at least one prior anthracycline for the dedifferentiated group, which first-line treatment would satisfy; a qualifying CDK-pathway alteration plus Rb positivity by central IHC, neither ordered; and ECOG 0-1. The move now is the rank-1 panel plus Rb IHC, because a biomarker gate cannot be cleared retroactively.

    Rank 4. Pembrolizumab monotherapy, second line, conditional on TMB-high or MSI-high¶

    Thin but genuine disease-specific signal with the best characterised safety of anything non-cytotoxic here.

    Evidence base¶

    Add up the entire prospective checkpoint experience in chondrosarcoma and it comes to five patients. SARC028 treated 5 evaluable chondrosarcoma patients in its bone cohort and 1 responded, inside a cohort whose primary endpoint failed at 2 of 40, and the primary report never subtypes the responder (PMID 28988646). The retrospective five-centre cohort adds 1 PR among 5 pembrolizumab-treated patients (PMID 37174084), and there is one case report of a CR sustained 24 months (PMID 36465334). The on-label route is different evidence entirely: KEYNOTE-158's TMB-high analysis reported ORR 29% (95% CI 21-39) against 6% (95% CI 5-8) in non-TMB-high patients (PMID 32919526), across ten tumour cohorts, none of them sarcoma. The biomarker context is prevalence and nothing more: PD-L1 positive in 9 of 22 dedifferentiated tumours against 0 of 119 conventional (PMID 27312065).

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Low. The critic put the arithmetic plainly: the 1-of-5 and 1-of-4 numbers everyone cites are subgroups of four and five patients whose confidence intervals run from roughly nothing to roughly everything. The on-label TMB route is a low-prior bet, since TMB at or above 10 mut/Mb appears in about 3.9% of sarcomas and chondrosarcoma is typically TMB-low. It costs nothing extra to test on a panel already going out, and it should not be planned around.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • SARC028 bone cohort, grade 3 or worse: anaemia 14%, lymphocyte count decreased 12%, prolonged aPTT 10%, thrombocytopenia 7%. Serious adverse events in 12% of the bone cohort.
    • Immune-related serious events across the trial included adrenal insufficiency, pneumonitis and nephritis.
    • No user toxicity veto to hit. The conservative ranked it partly on the strength of a decade of post-marketing irAE algorithms every oncology unit already runs.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    Both personas who ranked it called their confidence low, and the critic refused the framing that usually travels with it: the attribution of the SARC028 responder to dedifferentiated histology is secondhand in later sources and traces back to nobody. PD-L1 does no predictive work in either direction, since the one documented responder in the five-centre series was TPS 7%, CPS 8 and microsatellite-stable. The mechanism-level objection is the one that persists regardless of biomarker: macrophages are the dominant immune population in this disease, a high CD68+/CD8+ ratio was independently prognostic at p<0.01 (PMID 31956474), and depleting T cells accelerated growth in the immunocompetent rat model while depleting CD163+ macrophages slowed it (PMID 28344871). The advocate qualified the conservative's row on a different axis, objecting that an assumed ECOG was being used to pre-decide monotherapy over the combination on her behalf. concensusite declined to rank any checkpoint route at all, because MSI, TMB and PD-L1 are untested and the tumour-agnostic label is what would carry it.

    Practical considerations¶

    Off-label unless the panel returns TMB-high or MSI-high, in which case it moves onto the tumour-agnostic label for previously treated disease and the approved route would be re-filed with the standard-of-care options; what is ranked here is the off-label question. MMR IHC is the orthogonal read on the MSI call and runs in 2-5 days off about four unstained slides. PD-L1 should be read on a section containing the dedifferentiated component, with the clone and scoring system recorded explicitly.

    Why this rank¶

    Same agreement score as ranks 2, 3 and 5. It sits below abemaciclib because the CDK protocol has a pre-specified central biomarker gate and a named histology where this has neither, and above TAPUR because it carries an actual, if tiny, disease-specific response signal where TAPUR carries no efficacy estimate at all.

    Rank 6. Sunitinib plus nivolumab, off-label at first progression¶

    The only checkpoint dataset in which a dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma responded, bought with a treatment-related grade 5 pneumonitis.

    Evidence base¶

    IMMUNOSARC is a multicentre single-arm phase 2 in advanced progressing bone sarcomas, n=40, of whom 14 had chondrosarcoma and 4 of those were dedifferentiated (PMID 39540661, NCT03277924). Six-month PFS by central review was 42% (95% CI 27-58) against a >15% threshold, which the trial met. Overall ORR was 5%, median PFS 3.8 months, and one of the two partial responses was a dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma. The registered eligibility names chondrosarcoma and dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma outright, which almost no other checkpoint protocol does. The subgroup is 4 patients and the trial is closed.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Low. The critic's reading is the one to plan against: a 1-of-4 subgroup is not a rank-3 argument, and the 42% six-month figure is largely stable disease in a mixed-histology cohort of four diagnoses. The risktaker's counter is real too, that this is the single dataset in the whole dossier where a patient with her exact histology responded to a checkpoint-containing regimen, and that PD-L1 in this disease is confined to her subtype at 9 of 22 positive against 0 of 119 conventional (PMID 27312065).

    • One treatment-related grade 5 pneumonitis among 40 patients, and 17% discontinued for toxicity.

    • Her toxicity veto list is empty, and the conservative's dissent is about exactly that. An empty inferred list is not consent to a fatal-toxicity risk she has never been shown, in a woman whose ECOG 2 has never been measured by anyone.

    How to read this table

    Toxicity burden is patient-level adverse-event severity, tiered by grade-3+ rates and by any treatment-related death. Counter-productive MoA is a different axis: the mechanism-level risk that the intervention works against the therapeutic goal, such as antigen-loss escape or irradiating a bone that then fails to unite. The access route on each row of the recommendations table separates trial-only from off-label, compassionate-use and not-yet-accessible paths within this experimental table; approved care is on the standard-of-care page. The persona pills under each intervention are the at-a-glance board signal; the full per-persona rationale lives on the board page.

    Caveats¶

    • Evidence-base caveats. There is no randomised comparison in this dossier for any intervention against any disease-directed endpoint; the one randomised trial anywhere in the case file backs the early-palliative-care referral on the Standard-of-care table, and it was run in lung cancer. The ranked trial seats at ranks 2 and 3 have no clinical result in this disease at all. The prospective checkpoint experience in chondrosarcoma is five patients. The B7-H3 prevalence figure everyone cites comes off a 24-specimen tissue microarray with a Merck-employed author. Where per-term toxicity tables sit behind paywalls, the critic said so rather than assuming their contents; the EURO-B.O.S.S. table (PMID 33990016) is one nobody on this board has read.
    • Two numbers the board refused to settle, and this page will not settle either. They belong to chemotherapy, which is filed on the Standard-of-care table, and they travel with it rather than disappearing. The first-line response rate in her histology brackets 9% (modern five-centre cohort, PMID 37174084) to 20.5% (44-patient subset treated 1988-2011 with local response assessment, PMID 24099780), and the p=0.04 attached to the higher figure compares dedifferentiated against conventional histology rather than testing whether chemotherapy helped anybody. The critic and the conservative would plan against the 9%; the advocate holds that she should hear 9 to 20.5 with the reason the ends disagree, because choosing an end on her behalf is a decision she has not been in the room for; concensusite quoted both plus 11.5% for conventional. The second number is EURO-B.O.S.S.'s 29% five-year OS in metastatic patients, which is 23 patients at p=0.12 from an uncontrolled, fitness-selected cohort in which 18 of 57 never completed 6 of 9 planned cycles. The critic's structural point stands: 63% of that cohort reached surgical complete remission and the significant split in the paper is the remission one (49% versus 23%, p=0.004), so the survival tail may track resectability rather than the regimen. Chemotherapy holds its place on the strength of what is measured, and it cannot be sold on the 29%.
    • Compartment and biomarker dependencies. Ranks 13 through 15 assume an IDH1 or IDH2 mutation and foreclose without one, as does the gated ivosidenib row on the Standard-of-care table. Rank 3 assumes a qualifying CDK-pathway alteration plus Rb positivity by central IHC. Rank 4 assumes a TMB-high or MSI-high call. Rank 7 assumes a high B7-H3 H-score from a stain that is not a standard clinical order and has no target-validator row. Ranks 20 and 21 assume an allele-level HLA class I type nobody has drawn. Every one of those is unordered today.
    • The fixation question the board could not resolve travels with the routed row. The advocate argued that stabilising the acetabulum may be what raises her measured ECOG and reopens the doors the assumption is closing — including the ECOG 0-1 bars on ranks 2 and 3 of this table. The critic called that the most useful idea anyone put on the table and also noted that no row in this dossier supports it: nothing measures performance status before and after periacetabular stabilisation, and no cohort here counts surgical morbidity at all. The conservative's counter is that the ECOG can as easily be spent as raised, since recovery runs in weeks against a 5-to-7-month median and a badly picked operative date plus 4-week washouts closes trial doors instead of opening them. The question is unresolved, the fixation row lives on the Standard-of-care table, and every protocol seat here reads through it.
    • What would change the ranking. A measured ECOG of 0 or 1 would open ranks 2 and 3 outright and is the single highest-leverage measurement in the case. A negative IDH1/IDH2 result forecloses ranks 13 through 15 and the gated ivosidenib row on the Standard-of-care table, and leaves everything else standing on both tables — chemotherapy, fixation, palliative care and radiotherapy included. A qualifying CDK-pathway alteration with Rb positivity would move abemaciclib above the IACS-6274 call, since the tie between them broke on availability rather than evidence. A B7-H3 H-score well above the subtype median would move the CAR-T row from flagged to ranked; a median-range score should kill it, which is what the risktaker said himself. An expert-pathology reclassification to conventional histology would rewrite both tables: the chemotherapy fork closes, and pazopanib, filed on the Standard-of-care table, comes alive.
    • Re-scoping caveat. The 0.8 efficacy weight, the empty veto list and the trials-forward flag are all inferred defaults built on one sentence she said. If a goals-of-care conversation returns something other than maximal-options, most of this table reorders and some of it comes off entirely.
    • What this re-rank changed. The contract reverted to two co-equal tables split by regulatory maturity, so nine options ranked here under the unified contract went back to the Standard-of-care table: doxorubicin-based chemotherapy, fixation, early palliative care, palliative radiotherapy, the sarcoma-centre referral, the trial-enrollment strategy, pulmonary metastasectomy, pazopanib and ivosidenib. The scope summary names each one so the board's unanimous first choice cannot vanish the way it did the last time this case was split across two tables. Ivosidenib was the one deliberate adjudication: it sat in both tables, NCCN carries it for IDH-mutant dedifferentiated disease while its FDA label excludes chondrosarcoma, and under this contract guideline carriage decides the filing, so it is filed once, on the Standard-of-care table, gated on the rank-1 panel, with concensusite's pre-commitment against off-label use outside a trial recorded on both surfaces. The trial-enrollment strategy row left on the same logic: the identical option sits on the Standard-of-care table under the same identifier, and one option does not get two rows.
