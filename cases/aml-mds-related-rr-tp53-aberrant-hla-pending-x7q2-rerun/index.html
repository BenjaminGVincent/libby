    Research question¶

    In relapsed/refractory MDS-related AML that has come back six years after a matched-sibling allo-HCT and is now refractory to FLAG-IDA + venetoclax, what interventions could target the patient's HLA-restricted immunotherapy handles (HA-1/HA-2 TCR-T, WT1, PRAME), her CD123- and CD33-positive blasts, a Y220C-contingent TP53 aberration, and a second-transplant platform — gated on retrieving the pending HLA-A*02:01 / HA-1/HA-2 typing that the whole immunotherapy axis hangs on?

    Patient profile (scrubbed)¶

    • Woman, age band 50-59, ECOG 1.
    • AML, myelodysplasia-related (AML-MR), relapsed/refractory; transformed from prior MDS (RCMD, IPSS-R high/very-high, ~2019). Extramedullary right-thigh myeloid sarcoma on MRI (adverse feature).
    • Marrow ~85% blasts (June 2026); peripheral blasts 58->82% (late June 2026). Refractory to FLAG-IDA + venetoclax reinduction.
    • Late relapse ~6 years after matched-sibling PBSC allo-HCT (~2020, CR sustained 72 months); pattern consistent with graft-versus-leukemia escape, not graft failure.
    • TP53 — aberrant, pending confirmation (not resolved in either direction). 2019 diagnostic NGS pathogenic; 2026 relapse NGS negative but on a limited assay (LOD ~2% VAF, no CNV, not MRD-grade). Working call: TP53-aberrant pending. TP53 Y220C — unknown; rezatapopt is contingent on it.
    • HLA-A*02:01 — unknown / pending retrieval (allele-level ~2020 typing not in records on hand). HLA-A*24:02 — unknown / pending. HA-1 / HA-2 minor antigens — unknown / pending for both patient and sibling donor.
    • CD33 — positive by flow (confirmed, qualitative). CD123 — positive by flow (confirmed, qualitative). Quantitative antigen density requested for both.
    • FLT3, IDH1, IDH2 — negative (confirmed, 2019). NPM1 — wild-type on available panels.
    • Cytogenetics — complex, adverse. 2026 relapse: gain of 5' MECOM, del(5q), del(7q), KMT2A amplification (4 copies), del(8cen), del(16q22). Shared del(5q)/del(7q) + TP53 founder across timepoints points to evolution of the original clone (recipient-origin), not a donor-derived or secondary leukemia.
    • No numeric organ-function labs (CrCl, LFTs, cardiac) supplied. Fitness / cardiac assessment is an explicit open item.

    Preferences¶

    • Efficacy/toxicity weight: 0.75 (efficacy-leaning), flagged as provisional — to be revisited once fitness is known.
    • Toxicity vetoes: none.
    • Modality constraints: none. Second allo-HCT (HCT2) and cellular/immunotherapy explicitly acceptable; trials acceptable including travel.
    • Free text: Curative intent. The explicit reason for the run is HLA-restricted immunotherapy eligibility (HA-1/HA-2 TCR-T, WT1, PRAME), gated on HLA-A*02:01 status and HA-1/HA-2 genotyping of patient and sibling donor; surfacing and de-risking that pathway is the priority.
    • Trial preference: prefers trials — yes.

    Target validation paths¶

    Two workups decide whether this case's central question has an answer. Allele-level HLA-A02:01 typing gates the HA-1/HA-2 TCR-T programs (TSC-100/101 via NCT05473910, the Bleakley HA-1 platform via NCT03326921, BSB-1001 via NCT06704152) and the WT1-C4 and PRAME-directed cells that share that allele; HLA-A24:02, pulled in the same run, keeps a WT1-siTCR fallback alive if A02:01 is negative. HA-1/HA-2 genotyping of both the patient and her HLA-identical sibling donor then establishes the recipient-positive / donor-negative mismatch those TCR-T products require. If HLA-A02:01 and A*24:02 both type negative, this report has no within-scope HLA-restricted recommendation, and the next conversation about standard salvage care belongs to the treating team.

    HLA-restricted immunotherapy¶

    Two assays are essential and gate everything the case was submitted to surface. High-resolution HLA class I typing resolves the allele-level HLA-A02:01 status that every HLA-restricted lever hangs on, with A24:02 read in parallel so a negative A*02:01 does not close the door before the WT1-siTCR fallback is checked. Retrieve the allele-level ~2020 transplant typing report first and repeat high-resolution typing only if that record cannot be confirmed; turnaround is 3 to 7 days, with STAT available. HA-1/HA-2 minor-antigen genotyping is the second essential gate: HLA-identical siblings can still differ at these minor loci, so both the patient and the donor must be genotyped to establish the recipient HA-1(H)-positive / donor HA-1-negative mismatch that TSC-100/101 requires. It is an uncommon catalog test that runs 1 to 3 weeks as a specialized send-out and needs a fresh donor sample plus donor consent, so line those up early. Two high-priority reads add context rather than gate: standardized WT1 qPCR confirms the WT1-directed target and sets a quantitative MRD baseline, and a PRAME expression read (flow or RNA) establishes whether the PRAME-directed lever has a target present, since PRAME in AML is variable rather than universal.

    TP53¶

    TP53 is held aberrant pending confirmation, and the workup is built to resolve the 2019-mutant versus 2026-negative discrepancy without collapsing it to wild-type on a 2%-LOD panel that reads no copy number. Two essential steps carry it: retrieving the 2019 diagnostic variant identity and VAF from the original NGS report, and running a copy-number-capable heme NGS panel that reports TP53 at the variant level. A documented Y220C at either step is the specific handle for the Y220C-selective reactivator rezatapopt via NCT06616636; any other TP53 variant, or a true absence, removes that option. Two high-priority orthogonal reads corroborate the call from archival material at no new procedure: p53 protein IHC (an overexpression or complete-null pattern supports a functionally aberrant TP53) and 17p/TP53 deletion FISH (detects the copy loss the limited 2026 panel could not see).

    CD33¶

    Quantitative CD33 antigen-density flow (ABC/MESF) is the essential read here. It converts the qualitative CD33-positive call into the surface-density measure that predicts benefit from CD33-directed ADC therapy, since low density blunts gemtuzumab ozogamicin. Request the density quantification explicitly on the phenotyping specimen, because it is not a default readout. A medium-priority germline genotype of the CD33 rs12459419 splicing SNP refines the prediction: the CC genotype retains the target epitope and predicts better response, while a T allele blunts it. It does not gate on its own but tempers expectations alongside the density read.

    CD123¶

    Quantitative CD123 antigen-density flow (ABC/MESF) is the essential read for selecting among the CD123-directed agents (tagraxofusp, pivekimab sunirine). It can run on the same specimen and draw as the CD33 density request, and a dual CD33/CD123 density supports a combined-targeting strategy. Without it, CD123-directed cytoreduction ahead of a cellular or transplant consolidation is chosen blind to the predictive marker.

    Second-transplant platform¶

    Two high-priority confirmatory reads underpin the transplant plan. STR chimerism on flow-sorted CD34+ blasts confirms that the relapse arises from recipient-origin leukemia rather than a donor-derived or secondary leukemia; the sex-matched sibling donor rules out XY-FISH as a shortcut, and CD34+ sorting keeps normal donor hematopoiesis from masking the blast compartment. A full metaphase karyotype plus MECOM and KMT2A FISH at relapse confirms the adverse cytogenetic features (MECOM rearrangement, KMT2A amplification) that shape prognosis and trial eligibility.

    Cross-cutting caveat (read first)¶

    The pending HLA-A*02:01 typing is the master switch, and the ranking is scoped to the targetable features — not to standard AML care. The primary reason this case was submitted is HLA-restricted immunotherapy, and every lever inside it (HA-1/HA-2 TCR-T, WT1-C4, PRAME-directed cells) is presented on HLA-A*02:01, whose allele-level status is not in the records on hand. An RNA- or EHR-placeholder-level "typed in 2020" is not enough: the therapy gate is the allele call plus a recipient HA-1(H)-positive / donor HA-1-negative mismatch, and HLA-identical siblings can still differ at that minor locus. That is why the rank-1 action here is the typing itself, not a drug.

    • The ranking is targetable-feature-scoped, and it is one of two co-equal tables. This Experimental table carries only feature-targeting investigational options: the workup, the HLA-conditional HA-1 TCR-T rec, and interventions that target the patient's other stated handles (CD123, WT1/PRAME, the HCT2 platform). Approved feature-targeting drugs are routed to the Standard-of-care table — gemtuzumab ozogamicin (approved for R/R CD33+ AML, a population that includes this patient) lives there, not in this ranking. Genuinely non-targeting standard care — including the menin inhibitors an upstream sweep surfaced against her KMT2A amplification (a copy-number gain, not the rearrangement menin inhibitors need) — is out of scope for the feature ranking entirely and is the treating team's separate conversation.
    • If HLA-A02:01 is negative, this case has no within-scope HLA-restricted recommendation; standard-of-care for relapsed/refractory AML lies outside Libby's targetable-feature ranking and should be pursued through the treating team's normal care channel. One door stays ajar: if A02:01 is negative but A24:02 positive, the A24:02-restricted WT1-siTCR (TBI-1301) becomes the fallback lever — which is exactly why both alleles must be pulled in parallel.
    • Practical workup logistics: pull the ~2020 donor-registry / transplant typing report first (HLA typing 3-7 days, STAT available); HA-1/HA-2 genotyping is a specialized send-out (1-3 weeks) that needs a fresh sample from the sibling donor and may route through the ALLOHA (NCT05473910) trial screening pathway. The whole HLA-restricted plan also assumes transplant-grade fitness that no organ-function labs have yet established.

    Rank 1. High-resolution HLA typing (A02:01 / A24:02) + HA-1/HA-2 genotyping of patient and sibling donor — diagnostic gate¶

    The confirmatory test that decides whether the referral's own question has an answer: it resolves the allele status and minor-antigen mismatch that every HLA-restricted lever is gated on.

    Evidence base¶

    This is a records-and-assay action, not a therapy, so the evidence base is the eligibility criteria of the programs it unlocks rather than an effect size. HLA-A02:01 gates the Bleakley HA-1 TCR-T program (NCT03326921, the closest published precedent, PMID 38683966) and the commercial TSC-100/101 platform (NCT05473910); the HA-1/HA-2 genotyping establishes the recipient-positive / donor-negative mismatch those TCR-T products require (PMID 31275297). A24:02, pulled in parallel, is what keeps the WT1-siTCR fallback alive if A*02:01 is negative.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    As a gate it either opens or closes the whole HLA-restricted axis; there is no partial credit. The point of ranking it first is that the referral cannot be answered until it returns, and a negative allele call forecloses the rank-3 rec outright rather than downgrading it.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • None from the test itself — blood draw, or retrieval of the archived 2020 typing report.
    • The only cost is time: HLA typing runs 3-7 days (STAT available); HA-1/HA-2 genotyping is a 1-3 week specialized send-out that needs a fresh sibling-donor sample.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    Board endorsement was unanimous. A non-toxic test that gates every downstream lever is the one thing all five personas agree comes first, and none raised a mechanism or preference objection.

    Practical considerations¶

    Pull the ~2020 donor-registry / transplant typing before re-drawing; allele-level A02:01 from that record is acceptable if it can be confirmed. HA-1/HA-2 genotyping is uncommon as a catalog test — confirm availability at an ASHI immunogenetics lab or route it through the ALLOHA (NCT05473910) screening pathway, and secure donor consent early. Run A02:01 and A*24:02 together, not sequentially.

    Why this rank¶

    Nothing HLA-restricted can be scoped, ranked, or enrolled until this returns, so it precedes even the unanimously endorsed transplant backbone. It carries an agreement score of 1.0 for the same reason the Iomab-B backbone does — no dissent, no veto — but it sits above it because it is the prerequisite, not the platform.

    Rank 2. Iomab-B (131I-apamistamab) conditioning + second allo-HCT¶

    The curative backbone all five personas endorsed — the randomized way to carry an 85%-blast marrow into a second transplant, gated on a fitness workup not yet done.

    Evidence base¶

    SIERRA is the one randomized phase 3 in the dossier that fits the actual problem (PMID 39298738, n=153; NCT02665065). Durable CR at 180 days reached 17.1% (95% CI 9.4-27.5) with the radioconjugate versus 0% (0-4.7) on physician's-choice conventional care, p<0.0001, and EFS favored it at HR 0.23 (95% CI 0.15-0.34). The design carries active disease into allo-HCT rather than demanding a remission chemotherapy can no longer deliver, which is the crux of a curative plan against 85% blasts. Two limits are load-bearing: the endpoint is a surrogate — ITT OS was flat at HR 0.99 (95% CI 0.70-1.41), diluted by 52% crossover to the radioconjugate — and SIERRA enrolled patients heading to a first transplant, so reading it onto a prior-HCT relapse is indirect.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Moderate, and the honesty is in the qualifier: this is the strongest evidence tier in the file, but it was earned in first-transplant patients, so the durable-CR rate is a reasonable expectation for the conditioning mechanism rather than a proven number for a second transplant. Whether it holds depends on fitness that has not been measured and on prior-HCT eligibility the go-forward trial has to confirm.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • Grade 3+ treatment-related AEs occurred in 59.7% versus 59.2% on conventional care — as conditioning the radioconjugate adds essentially no incremental grade 3+ burden.
    • Standard transplant-conditioning cytopenias, infection and mucositis apply on top of that.
    • Second-transplant treatment-related mortality is uncharacterized by SIERRA and is the real unknown here.
    • No cardiac, renal or hepatic labs on file: this conditioning cannot be called deliverable until that clearance lands.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    No persona dissented and none vetoed. The conservative and concensusite led with it on evidence tier and guideline fit; the critic held it at moderate on the surrogate endpoint and indirectness without objecting; the risktaker and advocate ranked it just below their stated-priority immunotherapy because it is the platform that makes cellular consolidation deliverable. The only mechanism-level flag is mild: radioconjugate marrow injury could compromise engraftment if the HCT2 platform is not secured.

    Practical considerations¶

    NCT02665065 is closed and the go-forward conditioning trial NCT07157514 is not yet recruiting, so timing and access are open. ELN 2022 and NCCN AML v3.2026 both keep second allo-HCT + clinical-trial enrollment in play for relapsed disease; Iomab-B enters through the category-2A clinical-trial route, not a drug-level category. The fitness / organ-function workup the preferences flag as open must precede conditioning, and the 0.75 efficacy weight should be revisited once that lands.

    Why this rank¶

    It sits at agreement score 1.0, tied with the workup and ahead of every therapeutic, because all five personas endorsed it and none dissented. It ranks below the workup only because the workup is a prerequisite; it ranks above the HA-1 TCR-T (0.8) because the TCR-T is gated on pending typing and is delivered on top of this platform, not instead of it.

    Rank 3. HA-1-specific CD8+/CD4+ TCR-T (Bleakley platform; TSC-100 / BSB-1001)¶

    Conditional on hla_a0201:positive. Foreclosed if the test is negative.

    Evidence base¶

    Krakow 2024 is the closest published precedent for the patient's primary handle (PMID 38683966, single-arm phase 1, n=9; NCT03326921). Four of nine post-allo-HCT relapses achieved or maintained CR, one past two years, with zero DLTs, zero CRS and zero ICANS. The commercial route is TSC-100 / TSC-101 (NCT05473910) and BSB-1001 (NCT06704152), all recruiting. This is a feasibility cohort, not an efficacy result: n=9, no comparator, no pre-specified efficacy endpoint, so the 4-of-9 CR cannot be separated from the lymphodepletion effect or the natural clonal course.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Assuming the positive branch — A*02:01 confirmed and the HA-1 mismatch present — the likelihood is genuinely uncertain but carries curative intent, because HA-1 sits only on blood-lineage cells, so a durable graft-versus-leukemia remission is the plausible upside and it comes with almost no collateral. The signal is a clean 4-of-9 CR rather than a powered estimate. A negative typing result forecloses this rec entirely; it does not downgrade it.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • Zero CRS and zero ICANS across 9 patients — the opposite of what a T-cell redirection therapy usually risks.
    • Lymphodepletion drove grade 3-4 neutropenia and grade 3-5 infections (Legionella and S. aureus pneumonia reported).
    • Two cases of mild grade 1-2 acute GVHD, attributed to immunosuppression withdrawal rather than the TCR-T.
    • No toxicity veto applies (toxicity_vetoes is empty and the safety read is clean).

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    No persona dissented or vetoed; four ranked it. The critic ranked it but refused to dress up n=9 as efficacy — an evidence-quality caution that is not biomarker-contingent and persists in the positive branch. The conservative declined to rank it on the same evidence-tier grounds but explicitly did not veto, calling the clean safety read the opposite of what they worry about. The risktaker and advocate pushed to rank it higher on the referral's own lens. The mechanism-level risk is modest: TCR-T kinetics may lag an 85%-blast marrow and antigen-loss escape is possible, but lineage-restricted killing limits off-target collateral.

    Practical considerations¶

    Three recruiting trial slots match the preference for trials. Sequencing is real: an 85%-blast marrow needs cytoreduction and the HCT2 platform in place before TCR-T can go in as consolidation, so this is not a standalone. NCCN AML v3.2026's category-2A clinical-trial-enrollment route is the guideline hook; there is no drug-level category, and ELN 2022 offers no consensus on sequencing DLI versus second transplant versus cellular therapy for post-HCT relapse.

    Why this rank¶

    It ranks third rather than first despite being the referral's stated priority for two reasons that survive the positive branch: agreement score 0.8 versus the platform's 1.0 (the conservative's evidence-tier reservation keeps it off five-of-five), and the eligibility gate that means it cannot even be called deliverable until the rank-1 workup returns. The advocate argued it belongs above Iomab-B on the user's lens; the PI ranks it just below, because the platform is what makes this consolidation possible and carries unanimous endorsement.

    Rank 4. Tagraxofusp (SL-401, CD123-directed IL-3/diphtheria-toxin fusion)¶

    A labeled CD123 agent with a real safety algorithm, but its headline efficacy is BPDCN cross-tumor and its capillary-leak deaths demand organ-function labs first.

    Evidence base¶

    The registration study is Pemmaraju 2019 (PMID 31018069, open-label single-arm phase 2). First-line CR/clinical CR was 72% (21/29) at 12 ug/kg — but the disease was BPDCN, not AML, so that number is cross-tumor read-across, not indication data, and an 85%-blast AML marrow is not where it was earned. An AML-specific route exists: tagraxofusp plus low-intensity cladribine/cytarabine in CD123-positive R/R AML (NCT06561152). It targets a confirmed feature (CD123 by flow), so it is not biomarker-gated, but the quantitative CD123 antigen density that predicts benefit is still pending.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Low-to-uncertain for this marrow. The only efficacy number is a first-line BPDCN result, and nothing in the dossier establishes that it transfers to high-burden AML. The CD123 target is confirmed present, which is the argument for it; the density read that would tell you whether the handle is strong enough is not yet in.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • Capillary leak syndrome in 19% (boxed warning), with one death reported in each dose subgroup.
    • Hepatic transaminase elevation (ALT 64%, AST 60% any grade) and hypoalbuminemia (55%).
    • With no baseline albumin or cardiac/hepatic workup on file, this is exactly the toxicity profile that needs fitness data before it can be started safely.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    Only the conservative ranked it, and chose it over pivekimab and flotetuzumab specifically for its worked-out capillary-leak management algorithm — the one CD123 option with an FDA label and years of post-marketing safety data. The critic and concensusite filed qualified cautions rather than dissents: the 72% CR does not transfer from BPDCN, and the agent has no AML guideline standing. Those cautions are why this is carried with caveats despite no formal dissent — presenting an off-label cross-tumor agent as unqualified standard care would misstate its footing. No plausible counter-productive mechanism; the efficacy risk is non-transfer, not antagonism.

    Practical considerations¶

    FDA-approved in BPDCN only; no NCCN AML v3.2026 or ELN 2022 listing, so AML use is off-label. The capillary-leak algorithm covers albumin thresholds, daily weights, blood-pressure monitoring and dose holds — but it needs a baseline albumin and cardiac read to run against. NCT06561152 is the enrollable AML route.

    Why this rank¶

    Agreement score 0.2 places it above the two dissented CD-ADCs below it. It clears gemtuzumab (0.0) because gemtuzumab drew active dissent that its own data show no benefit in this stratum, whereas tagraxofusp drew only qualified cross-tumor cautions. That is a thin margin, and the narrative carries the off-label reality that the score alone does not.

    Rank 5. Multiantigen leukemia-specific donor T cells (PRAME/WT1/Survivin/NY-ESO-1)¶

    The A02:01-independent immunotherapy hedge — targets WT1 and PRAME without the master-switch gate, enrollable now, but modest published activity.*

    Evidence base¶

    Lulla 2021 is the published precedent for this donor T-cell product (PMID 33270816, single-arm phase 1, n=25; NCT02203903). In the active-disease cohort, 1 of 8 reached CR and 1 a PR; across the whole cohort median leukemia-free and overall survival were not reached at 1.9 years. The product primes T cells against PRAME, WT1 and Survivin, so it hits two of the patient's named handles without depending on a single HLA-A*02:01 gate — which is the whole point of carrying it. It is a small early-phase cohort, and the responses in overt disease are modest.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Low-to-modest, but with a strategic value the number alone misses. The 2-of-8 objective-response rate in active disease is not a strong salvage signal, so this is a hedge rather than a lead: it is the one immunotherapy route that stays open if HLA-A*02:01 types negative and forecloses the HA-1 axis. WT1 and PRAME expression on the blasts is not yet confirmed, and that read gates eligibility.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • No grade >2 acute GVHD and no extensive chronic GVHD across 25 recipients — a clean donor-T-cell safety profile.
    • Delivered in the post-transplant setting, so lymphodepletion and infection risks track the transplant rather than the product.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    No persona ranked it, so it carries no agreement score; the advocate flagged it as a live fallback that sidesteps the A*02:01 gate. That is why it is carried with caveats rather than as a lead — the board weighed it as a hedge, not a top-tier choice. The mechanism-level risk is low and cuts the right way: multi-antigen targeting limits the single-antigen escape that a documented clonally-evolving relapse invites.

    Practical considerations¶

    Recruiting at two US sites (Children's National, Johns Hopkins) in the post-HCT setting the patient is heading toward, which matches prefers-trials. Confirm adult enrollment and post-HCT timing, and confirm WT1/PRAME expression first. The product can potentially be generated from the existing sibling donor. Keep it live even if HLA typing forecloses the A*02:01-restricted TCR options.

    How to read this table

    Toxicity burden is patient-level adverse-event severity (Low/Moderate/High by grade-3+ rates and any treatment-related death). Counter-productive MoA is a different axis: the mechanism-level risk that the intervention could work against the therapeutic goal — engraftment failure, antigen-loss escape, a transplant-sinking toxicity — not a patient side effect. The persona pills under each intervention are the at-a-glance board signal; the full per-persona rationale lives on the board page.

    Caveats¶

    • Evidence-base caveats. The lead HLA-restricted lever (HA-1 TCR-T) rests on a single-arm n=9 feasibility cohort with no comparator; the transplant backbone (SIERRA) is randomized but reports a surrogate endpoint with flat ITT OS diluted by 52% crossover, and enrolled first-transplant patients. Tagraxofusp's efficacy is BPDCN cross-tumor; flotetuzumab's TP53 signal is an unadjusted post-hoc correlative subgroup on a discontinued program.
    • Compartment / biomarker dependencies. The rank-3 HA-1 TCR-T assumes HLA-A*02:01 confirmation plus a recipient HA-1(H)-positive / donor HA-1-negative mismatch; without both, it is foreclosed, not downgraded. TP53 status is unresolved in either direction — do not down-call to wild-type on a 2%-LOD/no-CNV panel — and a confirmed Y220C would open rezatapopt as a new axis. CD123 and CD33 are confirmed present but the quantitative antigen density that predicts ADC/immunotoxin benefit is pending for both.
    • What would change the ranking. A negative HLA-A02:01 call moves rank 3 to non-applicable and turns the case to the A24:02 fallback or standard care. Documented organ-function / hepatic labs lift the conservative's pivekimab veto and reinstate it as a rankable debulking bridge. A confirmed TP53 Y220C variant introduces rezatapopt above the current CD123/CD33 tier. A prior-HCT-eligible go-forward Iomab-B slot (NCT07157514 opening) would firm up rank 2's deliverability.
    • Re-scoping caveat. The 0.75 efficacy weight is provisional: if the fitness / goals-of-care assessment returns a frailer picture than curative-intent transplant assumes, the whole ranking shifts toward tolerability and the transplant backbone itself may come off the table.
