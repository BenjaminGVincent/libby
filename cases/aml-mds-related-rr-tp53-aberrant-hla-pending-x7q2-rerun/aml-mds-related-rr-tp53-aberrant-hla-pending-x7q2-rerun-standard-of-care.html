Twelve standard options were screened for this relapsed/refractory AML-MR, and six are worth weighing now. The curative-intent backbone the guidelines carry for AML that has relapsed after an allograft is a second allogeneic transplant, with donor lymphocyte infusion from the same HLA-identical sibling as the other cellular route at that fork. NCCN and the ELN 2022 panel keep both in play for fit patients, and NCCN makes clinical trial enrollment the preferred strategy in this setting. Gemtuzumab ozogamicin earns a row on its own footing: it is FDA-approved for relapsed/refractory CD33-positive AML and these blasts are CD33-positive, so it is standard here by regulatory maturity, though its own tier-1a evidence shows no survival benefit in the adverse-risk cytogenetics this patient carries. High-dose cytarabine salvage and radiotherapy to the thigh myeloid sarcoma fill out the near-term list as a cytoreductive bridge and local control.

Two standard options are already spent: the first sibling allograft, which held remission for about six years, and the FLAG-IDA plus venetoclax reinduction the disease progressed through.

No standard option sits behind an open biomarker gate, but the transplant backbone is conditioned on a fitness and organ-function workup that has not been done. The two approved menin inhibitors, revumenib and ziftomenib, are set aside on target: this disease carries KMT2A amplification, not the KMT2A translocation the revumenib label requires, and is NPM1 wild-type.
