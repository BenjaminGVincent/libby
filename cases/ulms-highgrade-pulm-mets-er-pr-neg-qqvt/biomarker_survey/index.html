    Selected general biomarker report — ulms-highgrade-pulm-mets-er-pr-neg-qqvt¶

    Which biomarkers on the selected panel this patient has been tested for, and which have never been measured. The panel covers targets established in this tumor type, targets relevant across a subset of tumor types, and biomarkers that predict benefit regardless of where the tumor started, such as MSI-H/dMMR and high tumor mutational burden gating PD-1 blockade.

    15 biomarkers surveyed: 15 with no usable result on file (4 essential), 0 measured but not to decision resolution, 0 already established. Panel coverage: 9 tumor-agnostic biomarkers plus 6 of 72 protein targets in scope for this tumor.

    Every one of the fifteen biomarkers surveyed here is a gap, and none is measured to decision grade. No sequencing exists from either specimen; the only stains ever run are ER and PR. Twelve were never measured; three, PRAME, MAGE-A4 and NY-ESO-1, are unassessable rather than negative: no HLA class I type is on file to score them against. Four gaps are essential, all order-now: NTRK fusion, MSI/dMMR (four stains on an existing block, plus Lynch implications for a woman in her thirties), TMB, and HRD/BRCA, the entry this histology most earns: olaparib plus temozolomide gave a 27% response rate and 6.9-month median PFS in pretreated uLMS.

    The list collapses to three orders and no new biopsy; tissue exists from the 2024 hysterectomy and the 2026 lung core. Comprehensive DNA plus RNA profiling on the 2024 resection is the highest-value order: it closes NTRK, TMB and HRD at once and returns TP53, RB1, ATRX and MED12 without a separate test. It must include RNA. NTRK breakpoints sit in introns that DNA-only panels miss, and this spindle-cell tumor was already misread once as benign. Second, one archival block cut for MMR, PD-L1, HER2 and B7-H3 IHC, with PD-L1 read on the lung core. Third, a blood draw for HLA typing and the germline HR panel. All three can run beside the planned LMS-04 induction without delaying it, and since the tumor-agnostic indications require prior therapy anyway, the results land exactly when they can be acted on: at progression.

    Two absences are deliberate. ER and PR <1% are confirmed decision-grade negatives with no survey row because neither reference panel lists them; the validation track is checking which specimen was stained, since at that cutoff a failed stain on the 2024 block reads the same as a true negative. The other 66 of 72 protein targets were set aside as epithelial markers a mesenchymal tumor does not plausibly express. B7-H3 stayed, with the best odds of a positive here: 97% of soft tissue sarcomas stain positive, roughly 69% high, though a positive buys trial screening, not a prescription. Most of what remains will be negative if tested. The argument for testing is the size of the option a positive opens, not the odds of one.

    Reading this report

    This page answers one question per biomarker: has this patient been tested for it, and was the test at a resolution that can carry a treatment decision. Not measured means nothing is on file, which is not the same as a negative result. Measured, not decision-grade means a result exists but cannot be acted on as it stands; those gaps are worked into the Target validation paths report. Priority weighs what is going unassessed against how routine the assay is. Most of these biomarkers will be negative if tested; the case for testing rests on the size of the option that opens if positive, not on how likely a positive is.
