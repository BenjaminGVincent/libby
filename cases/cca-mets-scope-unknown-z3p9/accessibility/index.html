    Idh1 R132 interventions (1)¶

    1. Ivosidenib ivosidenib, AG-120, Tibsovo¶

    Access status: Standard of care Off-label use   Modality: small_molecule   Verified: 2026-06-01

    If NGS returns an IDH1 R132 mutation, ivosidenib is approved second-line therapy the oncologist can prescribe directly. ClarIDHy was positive on PFS (HR 0.37) but the absolute PFS benefit is modest and the OS signal was blunted by heavy crossover, so this is a real option with honest limits, contingent on the hotspot testing positive.

    Regulatory
    FDA-approved (August 2021) for previously treated, locally advanced or metastatic cholangiocarcinoma with a susceptible IDH1 mutation; also approved in IDH1-mutant AML and EMA-approved
    Guidelines
    NCCN-listed for IDH1-mutant biliary tract cancer, 2L+
    Geographic scope
    US (and EU); approved and commercially available

    Next steps

    1. Order tumor DNA NGS covering the IDH1 R132 hotspot and confirm a susceptible mutation.
    2. If positive, prescribe directly and enroll in ServierONE at 1-800-813-5905 for benefits and copay support.
    3. Establish baseline ECG and monitor for QT prolongation on treatment.
    4. Call the same line for medical-information questions; report adverse events to 1-800-807-6124.

    Fgfr2 Fusion interventions (2)¶

    2. Futibatinib futibatinib, TAS-120, Lytgobi¶

    Access status: Standard of care Off-label use   Modality: small_molecule   Verified: 2026-06-01

    Futibatinib is the other approved FGFR inhibitor, and it holds activity against some kinase-domain mutations that drive resistance to pemigatinib, which makes it useful for sequencing rather than just as an alternative first FGFR drug. Same gate as pemigatinib: a confirmed FGFR2 fusion or rearrangement, and the on-label indication is specific to intrahepatic disease.

    Regulatory
    FDA-approved (accelerated, September 2022) for previously treated, unresectable locally advanced or metastatic intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma with an FGFR2 fusion or rearrangement; also EMA-approved
    Guidelines
    NCCN-listed for FGFR2 fusion/rearrangement-positive intrahepatic biliary tract cancer, 2L+
    Geographic scope
    US (and EU); approved and commercially available

    Next steps

    1. Confirm an FGFR2 fusion or rearrangement and document intrahepatic subsite, since the label is intrahepatic-specific.
    2. If positive, prescribe directly and enroll in Taiho Oncology Patient Support at 1-844-824-4648.
    3. Consider futibatinib specifically in the post-pemigatinib setting given its activity against some acquired resistance mutations.
    4. Monitor phosphate and schedule ophthalmologic exams per the FGFR class profile.

    5. Trastuzumab deruxtecan (T-DXd) trastuzumab deruxtecan, T-DXd, DS-8201, fam-trastuzumab deruxtecan-nxki, Enhertu¶

    Access status: Off-label use Clinical trial   Modality: ADC   Verified: 2026-06-01

    Because the HER2 IHC 3+ label is tumor-agnostic, T-DXd is effectively on-label for an IHC 3+ biliary tumor once profiling confirms it; below IHC 3+ it shifts to a genuine off-label call. A first-line randomized trial, DESTINY-BTC01 (NCT06467357), is recruiting across US sites for treatment-naive HER2-expressing patients. ILD risk should weigh in the decision even though no toxicity veto was set.

    Regulatory
    FDA-approved (accelerated, April 2024) tumor-agnostic for previously treated, unresectable or metastatic HER2-positive (IHC 3+) solid tumors; biliary tract cancer was part of the supporting DESTINY-PanTumor02 data
    Guidelines
    NCCN-listed for HER2 IHC 3+ biliary tract cancer under the tumor-agnostic HER2 indication
    Geographic scope
    US; first-line trial NCT06467357 recruiting at multiple US sites

    Next steps

    1. Run HER2 IHC with reflex ISH and document the exact IHC score, since 3+ is the on-label threshold and drives expected benefit.
    2. If treatment-naive, call the AstraZeneca center at 1-877-240-9479 to check DESTINY-BTC01 (NCT06467357) slots before defaulting to off-label use.
    3. For IHC 3+ use, the oncologist can prescribe on-label; for IHC 2+ expect a prior-authorization step.
    4. Baseline pulmonary assessment and active ILD/pneumonitis surveillance throughout treatment.

    7. Pembrolizumab pembrolizumab, MK-3475, Keytruda, lambrolizumab¶

    Access status: Standard of care Off-label use Clinical trial   Modality: monoclonal_antibody   Verified: 2026-06-01

    If MMR IHC, an MSI assay, or NGS-derived TMB comes back MSI-H/dMMR or TMB-high, pembrolizumab is on-label tumor-agnostic therapy the oncologist can prescribe directly.     2. If MSI-H/dMMR or TMB-high, prescribe on-label; the MSI-H/dMMR path is the more robust full approval.
    3. Enroll in the Merck Access Program for prior authorization and copay support; call 1-800-672-6372 for medical-information questions.
    4. Counsel on immune-mediated adverse events as the characteristic checkpoint-inhibitor risk.

    Unmatched interventions (5)¶

    8. Adagrasib adagrasib, MRTX849, Krazati¶

    Access status: Off-label use Clinical trial   Modality: small_molecule   Verified: 2026-06-01

    Adagrasib is approved for KRAS G12C NSCLC and colorectal cancer but not for biliary cancer, so a biliary patient with a confirmed G12C would access it off-label, supported by the small published KRYSTAL-1 biliary cohort. KRAS G12C is rare in biliary cancer (roughly 1%), and the registry trial under NCT05722327 is a colorectal-only combination that does not admit biliary patients. A clinical trial remains a reasonable route if an open KRAS G12C basket accepting biliary tumors can be located.

    Regulatory
    FDA-approved (accelerated) for KRAS G12C-mutated NSCLC (monotherapy) and for KRAS G12C-mutated colorectal cancer (with cetuximab); no biliary-tract indication
    Guidelines
    No biliary-specific guideline listing; off-label for biliary tract cancer
    Geographic scope
    US; off-label for biliary tract cancer

    Next steps

    1. Confirm KRAS G12C specifically on tumor DNA NGS; other KRAS variants do not qualify for a G12C inhibitor.
    2. For off-label biliary use, expect a prior-authorization step; cite the published KRYSTAL-1 biliary cohort (PMID 37099736) and call BMS Medical Information at 1-800-721-5072.
    3. Search for a currently recruiting KRAS G12C basket that explicitly accepts biliary tract cancer, since NCT05722327 as registered is colorectal-only and closed.
    4. Flag to the trial_screener that the NCT05722327 record does not match the biliary-basket description in the dossier.

    11. Selpercatinib selpercatinib, LOXO-292, Retevmo¶

    Access status: Standard of care Off-label use Clinical trial   Modality: small_molecule   Verified: 2026-06-01

    If RNA-based fusion NGS finds a RET fusion, selpercatinib is on-label tumor-agnostic therapy the oncologist prescribes directly. RET fusions are rare in biliary cancer, so the practical question is whether the fusion panel catches one; the LIBRETTO-001 basket explicitly covered non-lung, non-thyroid tumors.

    Regulatory
    FDA-approved (accelerated, September 2022) tumor-agnostic for RET fusion-positive solid tumors; also approved in RET fusion NSCLC and RET-mutant/fusion thyroid cancer

    Geographic scope

    12. TYRA-200 TYRA-200¶

    Access status: Clinical trial   Modality: small_molecule   Verified: 2026-06-01

    TYRA-200 is a next-generation FGFR2 inhibitor in phase 1, and the only way to get it is enrolling in NCT06160752. It is most relevant if the patient tests FGFR2-positive and later progresses on pemigatinib or futibatinib, since it targets some of the resistance mutations that defeat the approved drugs. No efficacy data have read out yet.

    Regulatory
    Investigational; no regulatory approval anywhere
    Geographic scope
    US sites only (UCSF, MGH, Ohio State, MD Anderson)

    Next steps

    1. Confirm an activating FGFR2 alteration first, since eligibility turns on it.
    2. Email the central contact at TyraClinicalTrials@tyra.bio or call (619) 728-4805 to check slot availability at the nearest of UCSF, MGH, Ohio State, or MD Anderson.
    3. Reserve this option mainly for the post-FGFR-inhibitor resistance setting rather than treatment-naive FGFR2-positive disease, where approved drugs come first.
