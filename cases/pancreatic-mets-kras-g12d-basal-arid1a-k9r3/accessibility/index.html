    Kras G12D interventions (3)¶

    1. daraxonrasib (RMC-6236), pan-RAS(ON) inhibitor daraxonrasib, RMC-6236¶

    Access status: Clinical trial Expanded access program   Modality: small_molecule   Verified: 2026-06-06

    The realistic path right now is a front-line trial: RASolute 303 (NCT07491445) takes treatment-naive metastatic PDAC and accepts the KRAS G12D under its RAS-mutant arm, with RMC-GI-102 (NCT06445062) as a backup that adds combination cohorts. There's also an FDA-authorized expanded access program, but it only opens after a prior line of metastatic therapy, so it isn't a door for him until he's progressed on first-line chemo. The one thing worth nailing down before screening is a tissue NGS G12D result to back up the ctDNA call.

    Regulatory
    investigational; FDA breakthrough therapy designation in PDAC; NDA expected on the strength of RASolute 302 but no approval yet (as of June 2026)
    Guidelines
    not in NCCN as an approved therapy; pivotal RASolute 302 read out at ASCO 2026 (OS 13.2 vs 6.7 mo, HR 0.40), filing pending
    Geographic scope
    US sites; Revolution Medicines runs trials in the US and internationally

    Next steps

    1. Call the Revolution Medicines study line (1-844-2-REVMED) or email medinfo@revmed.com to find the nearest RASolute 303 (NCT07491445) site with an open first-line slot
    2. Ask the treating team to confirm a tissue NGS KRAS G12D result, since the current call is ctDNA-only and tissue confirmation strengthens trial eligibility
    3. If no RASolute 303 site is reachable, ask the same line about RMC-GI-102 (NCT06445062) G12D subprotocols
    4. Keep the daraxonrasib EAP (NCT07573215) on file as the off-trial route for later, once he has had a line of metastatic chemotherapy

    3. INCB161734, oral KRAS G12D-selective inhibitor INCB161734¶

    Access status: Clinical trial   Modality: small_molecule   Verified: 2026-06-06

    4. ceralasertib (AZD6738), oral ATR inhibitor ceralasertib, AZD6738¶

    Access status: Clinical trial   Modality: small_molecule   Verified: 2026-06-06

    Ceralasertib is the cleanest live option on the ARID1A synthetic-lethal axis, and the UCSF basket (NCT03682289) explicitly takes pancreatic cancer and assigns by ARID1A/BAF250a status. Two things stand between the patient and a slot: the trial is active-not-recruiting, so a new opening isn't guaranteed, and entry depends on BAF250a (ARID1A) protein loss by IHC rather than the frameshift call alone. The validation step is ordering ARID1A IHC before pursuing this.

    Regulatory
    investigational; no approval anywhere
    Guidelines
    not in NCCN/ESMO; phase 2 activity in ARID1A-deficient tumors concentrated in gynecologic histologies, weaker non-gynecologic signal
    Geographic scope
    US (UCSF-sponsored)

    Next steps

    1. Have the treating team order ARID1A (BAF250a) IHC on tumor tissue; the frameshift call alone does not confirm eligibility
    2. Contact the NCT03682289 site (UCSF; PI Rahul Aggarwal) through the cancer center to ask whether the ARID1A pancreatic arm has any remaining slot despite the active-not-recruiting status
    3. For drug-level questions, call AstraZeneca medical information at 1-800-236-9933
    4. Keep this secondary to the KRAS axis; pursue in parallel only if ARID1A IHC confirms loss

    6. MRTX1133, KRAS G12D-selective noncovalent inhibitor MRTX1133¶

    Access status: Unavailable   Modality: small_molecule   Verified: 2026-06-06

    This one is a dead end. MRTX1133 was the first clinical KRAS G12D-selective small molecule, but the program was terminated for formulation problems before any efficacy read-out, so there is no trial slot, no expanded access, and no approval. The mechanism it validated lives on in zoldonrasib and INCB161734, which are the agents to pursue instead.
