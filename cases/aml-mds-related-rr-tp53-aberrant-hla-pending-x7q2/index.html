    Research question¶

    In relapsed/refractory MDS-related AML at ~85% marrow blasts, six years after a matched-sibling allo-HCT and now refractory to FLAG-IDA plus venetoclax, what interventions could target the HLA-restricted minor-antigen axis (HA-1/HA-2, WT1), the CD33/CD123 surface antigens, a possible TP53 Y220C handle, and a second-transplant platform, gated on retrieving HLA-A*02:01 typing, genotyping HA-1/HA-2 in the patient and sibling donor, and resolving the TP53 discrepancy?

    Patient profile (scrubbed)¶

    • Woman, 50-59, ECOG 1.
    • AML, myelodysplasia-related, relapsed/refractory; transformed from prior MDS (RCMD, IPSS-R high/very-high, ~2019).
    • Marrow ~85% blasts (June 2026); peripheral blasts rose 58% to 82% in late June 2026.
    • Extramedullary right-thigh myeloid sarcoma on MRI (May 2026), an adverse feature.
    • Late relapse ~6 years after a matched-sibling PBSC allo-HCT (~2020, CR sustained 72 months); pattern reads as graft-versus-leukemia escape, not graft failure.
    • Refractory to FLAG-IDA + venetoclax reinduction (best response PD).
    • TP53: aberrant, pending confirmation, not resolved in either direction. Pathogenic on 2019 diagnostic NGS; reported negative on the 2026 relapse panel, but that panel has a ~2% VAF limit of detection, no copy-number detection, and is not MRD-grade. Working call: TP53-aberrant pending confirmation. Y220C status unknown.
    • HLA-A*02:01: unknown / pending. High-resolution typing was done ~2020 but the allele-level result is only a chart placeholder. A*24:02 also pending.
    • HA-1 / HA-2 minor histocompatibility antigens: unknown for BOTH patient and sibling donor. HLA-identical siblings can still differ here.
    • CD33-positive and CD123-positive blasts by flow (both confirmed, qualitative only; quantitative antigen density requested).
    • Immunophenotype: CD34+, CD13+, CD117+ variably increased, HLA-DR variably decreased, CD38 decreased. FLT3 / IDH1 / IDH2 negative (2019).
    • Cytogenetics: complex, adverse. Shared del(5q)/del(7q) and a TP53 founder across 2019 and 2026 point to evolution of the original clone; 2026 adds MECOM gain and KMT2A amplification (4 copies).
    • No numeric organ-function or cardiac labs supplied; fitness assessment is an explicit open item.

    Preferences¶

    • Efficacy/toxicity weight 0.75 (efficacy-leaning), flagged for revisiting once fitness is known.
    • No toxicity vetoes.
    • No modality constraints; second allo-HCT and cellular/immunotherapy are all acceptable.
    • Curative intent.
    • Clinical trials acceptable, including travel.
    • The explicit reason for the run: HLA-restricted immunotherapy eligibility (HA-1/HA-2 TCR-T, WT1, PRAME), gated on HLA-A*02:01 status and HA-1/HA-2 genotyping of the patient and sibling donor. Surfacing and de-risking that pathway is the priority.

    Target validation paths¶

    The whole plan hinges on three pending gates, none of them resolved. High-resolution HLA class I typing plus HA-1/HA-2 genotyping decide whether donor TCR-T (TSC-100/TSC-101 via NCT05473910) is reachable at all; a variant-level relapse NGS panel decides whether the Y220C-selective reactivator rezatapopt (via PYNNACLE, NCT04585750) is in play; and STR chimerism on sorted CD34+ blasts decides whether the relapse is even recipient-derived and therefore targetable by minor-antigen T cells. If HLA-A*02:01 comes back absent, the HA-1/HA-2 and WT1 TCR-T levers both close, and the next conversation about standard salvage care belongs to the treating team rather than to this report.

    HLA class I typing and HA-1/HA-2 minor antigens¶

    Essential, and the first thing to do. Retrieve the 2020 allele-level HLA-A typing (report A02:01 and A24:02) from the transplant or donor-registry chart, same-day if the record exists and 3-7 days to repeat if it cannot be found. A02:01 is the master switch for the entire restricted axis. The same A02:01 result now unlocks three A02:01-restricted levers rather than two: alongside HA-1/HA-2 TCR-T and A02:01 WT1 TCR-T, a positive type opens CBX-250, the off-the-shelf CG1/HLA-A02:01 T-cell engager in CROSSCHECK-001 (NCT06994676), which needs documented A02:01 alone and no HA-1/HA-2 mismatch. In parallel, genotype HA-1/HA-2 in both the patient and the HLA-identical sibling donor: TSC-100 (HA-1) and TSC-101 (HA-2) via NCT05473910 require a recipient-positive, donor-negative mismatch, and HLA-identical siblings can still differ here. This is a single-SNP PCR genotype, roughly 1-2 weeks, most reliably run through the ALLOHA sponsor central lab because standalone HA-1/HA-2 testing is not a routine catalog assay. Skipping the donor arm leaves eligibility indeterminate even when the patient genotype looks favorable.

    TP53¶

    Held aberrant pending confirmation, not resolved in either direction. A comprehensive relapse myeloid NGS panel with variant-level readout and copy-number detection (1-3 weeks) is exactly what the 2026 relapse panel lacked, given its 2% limit of detection and absence of copy-number calling; it names the specific amino-acid change and gates rezatapopt via NCT04585750, which needs a confirmed Y220C. Read it together with three high-priority orthogonal checks rather than trusting any one alone: TP53 (17p13) FISH for copy loss, p53 IHC (clone DO-7) for an aberrant overexpression or null pattern, and retrieval of the 2019 diagnostic report for the original variant identity and VAF. Do not down-call to wild-type on the strength of the limited relapse panel.

    Second-transplant platform¶

    STR chimerism on FACS-sorted CD34+ blasts (1-2 weeks) is the load-bearing test here. It confirms whether the relapse is recipient-derived evolution of the original clone or a donor-derived leukemia, a distinction the sex-matched sibling donor keeps XY-FISH from settling. Cell sorting is the step that matters, since a bulk-marrow result cannot resolve blast-clone origin, and recipient origin is a prerequisite for any HA-directed TCR-T. Alongside it, a full relapse metaphase karyotype characterizes the adverse MECOM rearrangement and KMT2A amplification for risk stratification and trial eligibility, and right-thigh contrast MRI plus whole-body FDG-PET/CT maps the extramedullary myeloid sarcoma for a directed-radiotherapy decision before or bridging to the transplant.

    CD33¶

    Quantitative CD33 antigen-density flow (antibodies-bound-per-cell, 3-5 days) upgrades the current qualitative-positive call into the metric that predicts gemtuzumab ozogamicin benefit. Request the density readout explicitly; a standard panel reports positivity, not the antigen density that drives ADC selection.

    CD123¶

    Quantitative CD123 antigen-density flow, batched on the same fresh draw to conserve marrow, guides CD123-directed selection (tagraxofusp, pivekimab sunirine) and dual CD33/CD123 cytoreduction ahead of any cellular or transplant consolidation.

    Cross-cutting caveat (read first)¶

    Three pending diagnostics gate the options this case was submitted for, and none of them is resolved. The HLA-restricted immunotherapy the patient came here for is doubly gated: HLA-A02:01 is only a chart placeholder, and HA-directed TCR-T additionally needs a recipient HA-1(H)/HA-2-positive, donor-negative mismatch that has not been genotyped in either the patient or the sibling donor. RNA or qualitative typing does not substitute for allele-level HLA plus a paired mHAg genotype. The newly added CBX-250 rides the same HLA-A02:01 switch, so that one records pull now gates three HLA-restricted levers, not two. The TP53 handle is separately double-gated: the 2019-pathogenic-versus-2026-negative discrepancy sits on a 2% LOD, CNV-blind panel, and even a confirmed aberration only opens rezatapopt if the specific variant is Y220C.

    • The ranking is scoped to the patient's stated targetable features. It carries the confirmatory workup (rank 1), the curative transplant/cytoreduction backbone that targets the allograft platform and the confirmed CD33/CD123 antigens (ranks 2-6, 8-9), and the biomarker-conditional cell therapies and rezatapopt (ranks 7, 10, 11, 12). Standard salvage chemotherapy that does not act on one of these handles is out of scope and is a separate conversation with the treating team.

If TP53 is not Y220C, or a true reversion to wild-type is confirmed, rezatapopt (rank 12) is foreclosed. The curative transplant arc (ranks 2-6, 8) stays within scope regardless of how the immunotherapy and TP53 gates resolve, because it targets the allograft platform and the confirmed CD33/CD123 antigens rather than the pending markers.

    Rank 1. Confirmatory diagnostic gate (HLA typing + HA-1/HA-2 genotyping + relapse TP53/Y220C NGS + sorted-CD34 STR chimerism)¶

    What it resolves: whether the HLA-restricted immunotherapy the case was built around is reachable at all, whether the TP53 handle is Y220C-actionable, and whether the relapse is recipient-derived and therefore targetable by minor-antigen TCR-T.

    Evidence base¶

    This is not a therapeutic; it is the set of tests every downstream option depends on. The HA-directed TCR-T programs (NCT05473910, and the Bleakley precedent PMID 38683966) require HLA-A02:01 plus a recipient HA-1(H)/HA-2-positive, donor-negative mismatch; the off-the-shelf CBX-250 engager (NCT06994676) requires documented HLA-A02:01 alone; the myeloid rezatapopt trial (NCT06616636) requires a confirmed TP53 Y220C. The relapse NGS row that anchors the TP53 gate (PMID 39811143) is a variant-level, low-VAF, copy-number-capable panel, which is exactly what the 2026 assay lacked. Read the NGS alongside p53 IHC, 17p FISH, and the retrieved 2019 variant identity; do not adjudicate the discrepancy from any one of them alone.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Diagnostic certainty rather than a response probability. Each result independently opens or closes a door: HLA-A*02:01 for the whole restricted axis (HA-1/HA-2, WT1, and CBX-250), the HA-1/HA-2 mismatch for donor TCR-T, the Y220C call for rezatapopt, and sorted-CD34 STR chimerism for recipient-origin relapse. The tests are cheap and fast relative to what they decide, which is why all five personas placed their entire ranking behind them.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • None beyond a records request plus assays on blood, marrow, and a paired sibling-donor sample. No procedure-level risk beyond a marrow aspirate the patient is already having for disease assessment.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    Board endorsement was unanimous. There is nothing to dissent from: a non-toxic test that gates everything is the one thing every persona agreed on without qualification.

    Practical considerations¶

    Retrieve the 2020 allele-level HLA typing from the transplant / donor-registry chart first, since that is same-day; repeat typing is 3-7 days if the record cannot be found. HA-1/HA-2 genotyping is not a routine catalog test and is most reliably run through the ALLOHA sponsor central lab or an academic immunogenetics lab, and it needs the sibling donor sampled too. Confirm recipient-origin relapse by STR chimerism on sorted CD34+ blasts (the sex-matched sibling donor rules out XY-FISH). While typing is pending, the transplant team can hold parallel screening conversations with the enrolling programs.

    Rank 2. Second allogeneic HCT (HCT2)¶

    The curative backbone, deliverable only after the marrow is cytoreduced.

    Evidence base¶

    The Christopeit EBMT registry of 179 second transplants (PMID 23918951) puts 2-year OS at ~37% in the matched-related-donor subgroup that fits her, against ~25% pooled. Remission duration after the first transplant and disease stage at the second were the dominant survival drivers, and her ~6-year first remission sits on the favorable side. The FLAMSA-RIC (PMID 16110027) and DLI (PMID 17909197) series fill in how the platform is delivered and consolidated. All three are retrospective or single-arm (ROBINS-I Serious), and the 37% is a fitness-selected subgroup number, not a whole-cohort estimate.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Moderate for her specific profile. The long first remission pushes the odds up; 85% marrow blasts and complex TP53-aberrant cytogenetics pull hard the other way, and the same registry names active high-burden disease at transplant as the single biggest adverse driver. This is why HCT2 earns rank 2 only on the condition that cytoreduction brings the marrow close to a transplantable state first.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • Substantial non-relapse mortality and acute/chronic GVHD in a second allograft, not broken out in the abstract-level registry.
    • Infection and conditioning organ toxicity, which cannot be safely bounded without the missing cardiac and organ-function workup.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    No persona dissented; the critic and concensusite qualified the number (subgroup, retrospective, 2A-by-absence-of-anything-better) but agreed on the rank. The mechanism-level worry is real though: this disease already slipped graft-versus-leukemia once, and a TP53-aberrant complex karyotype is exactly the biology that escapes a second graft too. A durable HCT2 is not assured even with a good first remission.

    Practical considerations¶

    NCCN AML v3.2026 keeps second transplant a category 2A option for fit patients, and ELN 2022 places it at the center of curative-intent management once the marrow is back in remission. Settle donor strategy (same sibling versus an alternative) using the HA-1/HA-2 genotyping already underway, and get a cardiac / organ-function assessment on record before choosing conditioning intensity. The extramedullary thigh sarcoma may need directed radiotherapy separate from the transplant plan.

    Rank 3. Iomab-B (131I-apamistamab) conditioning into HCT2¶

    The RCT-grade way to carry an active, high-blast marrow into the transplant.

    Evidence base¶

    SIERRA (PMID 39298738, NCT02665065) is the only randomized phase 3 in the whole dossier. CD45-targeted radioimmunotherapy conditioning produced durable CR (>=180 days) of 17.1% versus 0% (p<0.0001) and EFS HR 0.23. The number the board weighted most was grade >=3 treatment-related AE parity, 59.7% versus 59.2% on conventional care, meaning the targeted conditioning did not stack toxicity on top of what these patients already face. Three honest limits: ITT OS was flat (HR 0.99, p=0.96), which the authors attribute to 52% crossover rather than a true failure; SIERRA enrolled first-transplant patients, so the durable-CR endpoint is a surrogate read against a population that was not heading to a second transplant; and in August 2024 the FDA judged the SIERRA package insufficient for a BLA and asked Actinium for a fresh non-crossover study, so the drug is neither approved nor guideline-adopted, and its regulatory path is unsettled.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Moderate, and the best-evidenced route to the one thing that makes every downstream cellular play possible: getting an 85%-blast marrow into an allograft. The efficacy caveat is that dCR is a durable-remission surrogate and the survival signal did not separate. It competes with FLAMSA-RIC (rank 8) for the same conditioning job.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • Grade >=3 treatment-related AEs ~60%: cytopenias, febrile neutropenia, infection, mucositis, as expected for myeloablative conditioning.
    • Marrow-directed conditioning may not reach the extramedullary thigh sarcoma, which needs separate directed-RT planning.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    Four personas endorsed the evidence; concensusite left it off their ranked list because NCCN and ELN have not adopted CD45 RIT, and in round 2 both concensusite and the critic sharpened that the FDA declined the SIERRA package as a BLA basis in August 2024 and asked for a fresh non-crossover study, so its guideline standing is trial-route rather than consensus. The risktaker and advocate pushed back that demoting the single highest-tier randomized readout on adoption status optimizes for the wrong variable in a patient who explicitly accepts trials. The critic conceded it is the one tier-1b anchor in the file, with a surrogate/crossover caveat. Conservative endorsed the tolerability parity but held the go/no-go contingent on a fitness assessment.

    Practical considerations¶

    Investigational and not approved; SIERRA is closed and the go-forward non-crossover study (NCT07157514) is not yet recruiting. The load-bearing eligibility question is prior transplant: SIERRA enrolled first-transplant patients, so confirm the new protocol accepts a prior allo-HCT before banking on it. Have the transplant team weigh Iomab-B against FLAMSA-RIC, since both target the same high-burden-disease problem.

    Rank 4. Gemtuzumab ozogamicin (CD33 ADC)¶

    The one agent a treating team can simply write; a modest on-label CD33 debulk.

    Evidence base¶

    The Sievers pooled phase 2 (PMID 11432892, n=142) reported ~30% overall remission (CR+CRp) in CD33-positive AML in first relapse, with grade >=3 hyperbilirubinemia 23% and transaminase elevation 17%. It is on-label and on-mechanism for her confirmed CD33-positive blasts, but the registration population was less pretreated than she is: no antecedent MDS, no prior FLAG-IDA/venetoclax failure, no extramedullary disease. It is a CD33-directed bridge toward HCT2, not definitive therapy.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Low-to-moderate, and probably toward the low end here given how far past first relapse she is. Benefit tracks CD33 antigen density, and only qualitative positivity is on file, so the quantitative flow readout should anchor the go/no-go rather than the density being assumed.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • Grade >=3 hyperbilirubinemia 23%, transaminitis 17%, infection 28%.
    • Veno-occlusive disease is the characteristic calicheamicin class risk and stacks peri-transplant, which argues for an adequate interval and fractionated dosing before HCT2.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    No dissent stood; concensusite actively defended it against the critic's evidence-quality downgrade, noting that a category 2A rating is the panel's own calibration of exactly that single-arm data plus twenty-plus years of VOD-monitoring experience. The mechanism-level catch is timing rather than direction: the hepatotoxicity that makes it a bridge could compromise the transplant it is bridging to if the interval is too short.

    Practical considerations¶

    FDA-approved on-label for CD33-positive relapsed AML; NCCN AML v3.2026 carries single-agent gemtuzumab at category 2A. Available by routine prescription, no trial or expanded-access mechanism needed. Request quantitative CD33 antigen density first, and factor VOD timing into the HCT2 plan.

    Rank 5. Donor lymphocyte infusion (sibling DLI)¶

    A readily available transplant adjunct whose honest expectation is modest in florid disease.

    Evidence base¶

    The Schmid EBMT registry (PMID 17909197, n=399) found 21% versus 9% 2-year OS with versus without DLI (adjusted P=0.04). That is the one comparative signal in the transplant set, and it is confounding-by-indication wearing a p-value: benefit concentrated in the remission or favorable-karyotype group (~56%), while the active-disease or aplasia stratum came in around 15%. Her late relapse (~6 years) predicts DLI benefit, but 85% blasts and adverse cytogenetics put her in the worse stratum.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Low for the stratum she falls in. The registry reads as an argument for cytoreduction before any DLI, not for DLI into a florid marrow, and every persona who touched it read it that way.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    No dissent stood; the critic and concensusite ranked it as an adjunct with the active-disease caveat baked in. Confirm the sibling donor is available and willing to donate lymphocytes again, and pair DLI with the transplant plan (including the FLAMSA-RIC prophylactic-DLI schedule) rather than using it alone.     Rank 6. Pivekimab sunirine (CD123 ADC)¶

    A CD123 debulk that avoids the failed backbone, though its VOD signal works against the transplant it bridges to.

    Evidence base¶

    Pivekimab (PMID 38423051) is a CD123 antibody-drug conjugate with single-agent activity in R/R AML: ORR 21% (95% CI 8-40) and composite CR 17% at the recommended phase 2 dose, matching her confirmed CD123 positivity without re-running the FLAG-IDA scaffold the disease already failed. Preclinically (PMID 29661755) it kills AML at low picomolar potency and reaches the leukemic stem-cell compartment while sparing normal progenitors. There is a recruiting Stanford trial pairing it with low-intensity chemotherapy (NCT06561152).

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Low as a single agent, and density-directed selection is still advisory since quantitative CD123 flow is pending. The 21% ORR comes off a 29-patient RP2D expansion, so the interval around it is wide. It is a cytoreductive bridge, not a curative move by itself, so it earns its rank only as a debulk toward the transplant and cellular options.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • Reversible veno-occlusive disease surfaced as a dose-limiting toxicity (2/68) and carries a boxed warning, the wrong hepatic risk to stack into a planned HCT2.
    • Febrile neutropenia 10% at the RP2D, and one treatment-related death (1%) against a modest 21% ORR.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    The risktaker and advocate ranked pivekimab as a CD123 bridge that debulks without re-using a failed backbone; no persona issued a formal dissent against it this round. The round-2 critiques on it were sequencing caveats: conservative flagged the peri-transplant VOD and would run on-label gemtuzumab first until organ-function data land, and the critic held the 21%-from-29-patients interval honestly. The mechanism-level catch is direct: the VOD that could compromise the very transplant it is bridging to, which is why gemtuzumab, with its mature VOD-monitoring playbook, sits ahead of it for the same debulk.

    Practical considerations¶

    FDA-approved for BPDCN (Decnupaz, May 2026), so a clinician can prescribe off-label for CD123-positive AML, though reimbursement will be a fight this soon after approval, and both AML-relevant trials are closed to new enrollment. Tagraxofusp is the CD123 alternative but adds capillary-leak risk that argues even harder for fitness data first.

    Rank 7. HA-1 / HA-2-directed donor TCR-T after HCT2¶

    Conditional on hla_a0201_ha:positive. Foreclosed if either the HLA-A02:01 type or the HA-1/HA-2 mismatch is negative.*

    Evidence base¶

    This is the handle the case was submitted for and the highest-ceiling cure swing. Bleakley's phase 1 (PMID 38683966, n=9) got 4 of 9 recurrent post-HCT patients into or holding CR, one remission past two years, no dose-limiting toxicity.     Signal-level in the positive branch. A 4/9 uncontrolled CR fraction with durable responders is a live cure signal, not proof; the trial exists precisely to test whether that ceiling is real.     • No dose-limiting toxicities across the n=9 cohort; the characteristic setting risks are lymphodepletion cytopenias and GVHD.

    Rank 10. WT1 HLA-A02:01 TCR-T (with A24:02 siTCR fallback)¶

    Conditional on hla_a0201:positive. Foreclosed if neither A02:01 nor A24:02 is present.

    Evidence base¶

    WT1 keeps the HLA-restricted axis alive if the HA-1/HA-2 pair does not mismatch but the patient is A02:01-positive.     Likelihood of desired effect¶

Best positioned as post-cytoreduction or post-HCT2 consolidation, and foreclosed entirely if neither allele is present.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • Engineered into EBV-specific donor cells to minimize GVHD; no per-term AE breakdown in the abstract.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

Advocate carried it explicitly so an A02:01-positive but HA-non-mismatched result does not foreclose the immunotherapy axis the patient prioritized. It sits just below CBX-250's score on the same A02:01 axis, but its evidence is prophylaxis in a setting she is not in.

    Rank 11. CBX-250 (CG1/HLA-A*02:01 TCR-mimetic T-cell engager)¶

    Conditional on hla_a0201:positive. Foreclosed if the HLA-A02:01 type is negative. Ranked here rather than front-line: the conservative CRS veto is overridden by not placing it over cytoreduction, and it is gated on a documented CRS-mitigation plan.*

    Evidence base¶

    CBX-250 was added to the dossier on this re-run after the initial screen missed it, because CG1/Cathepsin-G was not a nominated feature. It is a TCR-mimetic bispecific that binds the CG1 nonamer presented on HLA-A*02:01 and cross-links CD3 to redirect polyclonal T cells against blasts, so it works off-the-shelf with no donor or autologous manufacturing. The evidence is preclinical and conference-abstract-level (ASH 2024, doi:10.1182/blood-2024-201699): sub-nanomolar T-cell-mediated killing across a range of antigen density, no activity against healthy-donor neutrophils, CD34+ cells, or PBMCs, and tumor control plus a survival benefit in a primary-AML PDX at doses as low as 0.005 mg/kg. CROSSCHECK-001 (NCT06994676) is a recruiting first-in-human phase 1 at 11 US sites with no efficacy or safety readout yet. There is no PMID for the abstract, and the DOI is carried as-in-dossier rather than asserted beyond it.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Preclinical only, even in the positive branch. The mechanism data are genuinely strong for a phase-1 asset, but potency in a PDX is not a response in a patient, and CROSSCHECK-001 has yet to treat and report a single participant. Frame the likelihood as unproven and foreclosed entirely if HLA-A*02:01 comes back negative.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • First-in-human with no established MTD/RP2D and no characterized AE profile in a single treated patient. As a CD3 engager the anticipated class toxicity is cytokine release syndrome, and CRS severity scales with tumor burden, so an 85%-blast marrow is the highest-risk setting to debut an uncharacterized engager.
    • The conservative persona issued a threshold-conditional toxicity veto against ranking it front-line. The veto is liftable to a dissent only on a documented CRS-mitigation plan: cytoreduce the 85%-blast burden below threshold first, use step-up dosing, and keep tocilizumab on hand. None of that is on file yet.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    This is the row the re-deliberation turned on. The risktaker and advocate ranked it first on off-the-shelf speed for a marrow climbing 58% to 82% peripheral blasts in a month; concensusite ranked it fourth as an enrollment-preferred target that carries no consensus category of its own; the critic left it unranked because everything under it is preclinical and ranking it over options with human data inverts the evidence hierarchy; and the conservative issued the threshold-conditional CRS veto against the two rank-1 placements. Handled per the veto rules, the row is retained rather than dropped and is not ranked over the cytoreduction backbone. The veto is overridden by that placement plus the explicit cytoreduction-first / CRS-mitigation gate, and it is left visible in the persona pills and this narrative. The critic's evidence-maturity dissent is not biomarker-contingent, so it persists in the positive branch. Counter-productive-MoA severity is high because the standing veto rests on a mechanism concern: an uncharacterized CRS that scales with the very blast burden the drug is meant to reduce.

    Practical considerations¶

    Investigational, first-in-human, no approval or compassionate-use route; the only access is CROSSCHECK-001 (NCT06994676), recruiting at 11 US sites (City of Hope, Stanford, Moffitt, Northwestern, Dana-Farber, Washington University, MSK, Thomas Jefferson, Sarah Cannon, Vanderbilt, MD Anderson). Enrollment hinges on documented HLA-A02:01, the same pending typing that gates the rest of the axis; prior allo-HCT is permitted once 60 days have elapsed, and at least 4 weeks from any non-conditioned DLI, so the ~2020 transplant is not a barrier. Its honest edge over the TCR-T handles is that it needs neither the HA-1/HA-2 mismatch nor an HCT2 platform under it, so it could move fastest once A02:01 confirms. That speed is exactly why the safety gate matters: pair any move with the transplant team's CRS-mitigation plan and current organ-function assessment.

    Rank 12. Rezatapopt (Y220C-selective p53 reactivator) + azacitidine¶

    Conditional on tp53_y220c:positive. Foreclosed if TP53 is not Y220C or a true reversion is confirmed.

    Evidence base¶

    Rezatapopt binds the surface pocket created by the Y220C substitution and refolds mutant p53 toward a wild-type shape. In Y220C AML models (PMID 40608889) it reactivates the p53 program but drives little apoptosis on its own; adding venetoclax or MDM2/XPO1 inhibition restores real killing. The solid-tumor PYNNACLE data (PMID 41740031) show ORR 20%, and 30% in the KRAS-wild-type subset. Every clinical response so far is in solid tumors; the myeloid trial (NCT06616636, rezatapopt + azacitidine at MD Anderson) has posted nothing.

    Likelihood of desired effect¶

    Cross-tumor only, even in the positive branch, and doubly contingent: confirm a TP53 aberration at all off the 2019-versus-2026 discrepancy, then confirm it is specifically Y220C. A low-positive or unconfirmed biomarker is a weaker predictor of benefit than a tier-matched confirmed one, and the mechanism needs a venetoclax partner she is already refractory to. Treat the likelihood as speculative until both contingencies land.

    Toxicity profile¶

    • Grade >=3 anaemia 16%; predominant low-grade nausea (58%) and vomiting (44%), less frequent when taken with food.
    • Oral, continuous dosing; the toxicity burden is genuinely lower than the transplant and cytoreduction options, which is the one thing it has going for it.

    Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent¶

    Two dissents that persist through biomarker confirmation because they are about the evidence, not the marker. The critic marks it down for solid-tumor-only data plus the venetoclax dependency in a venetoclax-refractory patient, and in this round also caught a citation drift in the risktaker's rationale, correcting the quoted 30% ORR down to PYNNACLE's actual 20% (the 30% is the KRAS-wild-type subset). Concensusite marks it off-consensus, since no AML guideline lists a p53 reactivator and the myeloid trial has no results. Only the risktaker ranked it, as a mechanistic longshot. The counter-productive-MoA is that reactivated p53 barely triggers apoptosis alone and needs a partner drug she has already failed.

    Practical considerations¶

    Investigational, absent from NCCN AML v3.2026 and ELN 2022, and off the HLA-restricted axis that is the stated reason for the run, so the advocate deliberately kept it off the preference-carrying slate. Worth resolving in parallel with the HLA workup, not foregrounding. A non-Y220C aberration or a true reversion closes it; eprenetapopt is the low-yield pan-reactivator alternative and is functionally unavailable in myeloid disease.

    Workup considerations¶

    Beyond the four gating tests in rank 1, several high-priority but non-gating confirmatory steps sit adjacent to the profile and should run off the same specimens:

    • Quantitative CD33 and CD123 antigen-density flow (antibodies-bound-per-cell), batched on one fresh draw, to convert the qualitative-positive calls into the metric that actually predicts ADC benefit for gemtuzumab and pivekimab.
    • p53 protein IHC (clone DO-7) and TP53 (17p13) FISH on the marrow biopsy, plus retrieval of the 2019 diagnostic report, to reconcile the TP53 discrepancy at the protein and copy-number level without down-calling on the CNV-blind panel.
    • A full metaphase karyotype at relapse to characterize the adverse MECOM rearrangement and KMT2A amplification for risk stratification and trial eligibility.
    • Right-thigh contrast MRI plus whole-body FDG-PET/CT to map the extramedullary myeloid sarcoma and decide whether directed radiotherapy is warranted before or bridging to HCT2.

    Classes examined but not ranked¶

    • Tagraxofusp (CD123 immunotoxin): targets the confirmed CD123 handle, but no persona ranked it; pivekimab was preferred as the CD123 lever, and tagraxofusp adds capillary-leak risk that argues for fitness data first. Carried as the CD123 alternative in rank 6.
    • CD123xCD3 bispecifics (flotetuzumab, vibecotamab): on-mechanism for CD123 with real salvage signal for flotetuzumab in chemo-refractory AML, but both programs are discontinued or terminated, so neither is an open route.
    • PRAME-directed TCR-T (BPX-701): the third A*02:01-restricted handle named in the profile, but the only clinical program is terminated with no successor, so there is no way to reach a PRAME-directed TCR-T today.
    • Eprenetapopt (pan-p53 reactivator): targets the TP53 handle, but it is not Y220C-selective, its phase 3 in TP53-mutant MDS missed its primary endpoint, and myeloid development sits under a clinical hold, so it is the low-yield and functionally unavailable p53 option behind rezatapopt.

    How to read the last two columns

    Toxicity burden is patient-level adverse-event severity (tiered Low / Moderate / High from the grade >=3 rates in the cited evidence). Counter-productive MoA is a different axis: the mechanism-level risk that the intervention could blunt its own therapeutic goal, such as graft-versus-leukemia escape, on-target progenitor myeloablation, cytokine release scaling with tumor burden, or a p53 reactivator that needs a partner drug the patient has already failed. The persona pills under each intervention are the at-a-glance board signal; the full per-persona rationale lives on the board page. The veto pill on CBX-250 is the conservative persona's threshold-conditional CRS stop against ranking it front-line; it is overridden here by ranking it mid-list rather than over cytoreduction, and it lifts to a dissent once a documented CRS-mitigation plan exists.

    Caveats¶

    • Evidence-base caveats. The strongest row (Iomab-B / SIERRA) is a randomized phase 3 but open-label with a durable-CR surrogate endpoint and a null ITT OS confounded by 52% crossover, and the FDA declined it as a BLA basis in August 2024, so it is unapproved with an unsettled regulatory path. Everything else is retrospective registry (HCT2, DLI), single-arm phase 1/2 (HA-1 TCR-T n=9, WT1 n=12, pivekimab), cross-tumor only (rezatapopt), xenograft-only (dual CAR-T), or, for CBX-250, a single first-in-human trial-in-progress with no readout backed only by an ASH 2024 conference abstract (no PMID). For the HA-1 TCR-T precedent, the per-grade AE table could not be retrieved from the full text, so its safety profile rests on "no DLTs" across nine patients.
    • Biomarker dependencies. Ranks 7, 10, and 11 assume HLA-A*02:01 confirmation; rank 7 additionally assumes a recipient HA-1(H)/HA-2-positive, donor-negative mismatch; rank 12 assumes TP53 confirmed aberrant and specifically Y220C. Without those, the respective ranks are foreclosed. The transplant backbone (ranks 2-6, 8) does not depend on the pending markers and stays in scope regardless.
    • What would change the ranking. A retrievable HLA-A02:01-positive type plus a confirmed HA-1/HA-2 mismatch would move rank 7 from a conditional longshot toward the top of the therapeutic list, since it is the stated-reason option with the highest ceiling. An HLA-A02:01-positive type alone would activate CBX-250 (rank 11), and a documented CRS-mitigation plan (cytoreduction first, step-up dosing, tocilizumab on hand) would lift the conservative CRS veto to a dissent and could move it up on speed grounds. Documented organ-function and cardiac fitness would firm up the conditioning and transplant ranks and could raise the efficacy weight the file asks to revisit. A confirmed TP53 Y220C would lift rank 12 out of double contingency; a confirmed reversion to wild-type would foreclose it. A donor-derived leukemia on sorted-CD34 STR chimerism would redirect the entire plan away from minor-antigen TCR-T.
    • Re-scoping caveat. If fitness comes back poor, or goals of care shift away from curative intent, the whole arc changes: the transplant backbone and its conditioning would move toward palliative CD33/CD123 cytoreduction, and the efficacy-leaning 0.75 weight would need to be re-set.
