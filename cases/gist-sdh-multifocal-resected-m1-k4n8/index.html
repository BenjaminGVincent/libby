    Research question¶

    In a currently-NED patient with multifocal, R0-resected SDH-deficient gastric GIST (biallelic SDHA, KIT/PDGFRA wild-type) who has also had a single M1 deposit completely resected, what targetable-feature-directed strategy best serves the patient now, and what is the contingency plan at first recurrence, gated on confirming the SDH-deficient phenotype?

    Profile snapshot¶

    • Diagnosis: gastric GIST, SDH-deficient subtype (KIT/PDGFRA wild-type), multifocal; ypT3(m)N0M1, R0 distal gastrectomy + Roux-en-Y, single M1 deposit (ligament of Treitz) completely resected, 0/7 nodes, largest mass 7.5 cm. Currently NED on surveillance. ECOG 1.
    • Driver: biallelic SDHA inactivation (SDHA L306P + Y408N, ~85% combined VAF). Paradox to resolve: SDHB IHC reads intact, which is unusual for biallelic SDHA loss and is the single most load-bearing diagnostic flag in the case.
    • Other calls (all ctDNA-only, unconfirmed in tissue): PIK3CA R93W (non-canonical, weaker-consequence variant), MAP2K1 P124S, SDHA E350fs. Germline POLE R1679C (outside the proofreading hotspots; most likely VUS given TMB <1 and MSS).
    • Diagnostic context: MSS, TMB <1 mut/Mb; CD117/DOG1 positive, Desmin negative.

    Preferences¶

    The patient stated no explicit preferences; documented defaults were used: efficacy/toxicity weight 0.65 (mildly efficacy-leaning), no toxicity vetoes, no modality constraints, and a trial preference (targeted options in this subtype sit predominantly in trial territory). These are defaults, not an elicited patient voice; scan-anxiety tolerance and travel range should be confirmed before any plan hardens.

    Target validation paths¶

    The case turns on two tests. The SDHB IHC re-cut (clone 21A11 or BSB-131) paired with an SDHA IHC (clone 2E3 or D6J9M) resolves the SDHB-intact / biallelic-SDHA paradox, and the germline SDHA panel settles inheritance and family risk. Together they confirm the SDH-deficient driver that gates the belzutifan wt-GIST cohort via NCT04924075 and the temozolomide trials NCT03556384 and NCT05661643, and the germline result triggers cascade testing of first-degree relatives. If SDHA IHC also reads intact after a clean re-stain and the germline panel is unrevealing, the dSDH label collapses: there are no within-scope trial routes, SDHC promoter methylation becomes the next call, and standard KIT/PDGFRA-WT care for first recurrence is the treating team's conversation, not this report's.

    SDH-deficient GIST¶

    Three workups are essential before any feature-targeting therapy can be chosen. The germline SDHA full-gene sequencing panel with del/dup analysis resolves whether SDHA L306P (VAF 44%) is germline or fully somatic; tumor-only NGS cannot distinguish the two. The SDHB IHC re-cut with a positive internal control addresses the most likely explanation for retained SDHB, a technical false-negative from under-fixation or a missing internal-control vessel. The paired SDHA IHC arbitrates the conflict more directly, since SDHA staining is lost only in biallelic-SDHA tumors and retained when an SDHB/SDHC/SDHD or SDHC-methylation driver is at work. Two further essentials are patient-safety floors for an SDHx carrier: baseline paraganglioma surveillance (rapid-sequence whole-body MRI skull base to pelvis, plus Ga-68 DOTATATE PET/CT as the most sensitive functional tracer) and plasma free metanephrines by LC-MS/MS, because an undetected functional paraganglioma going to anesthesia is a hypertensive-crisis hazard. High priority but not gating: tumor-tissue NGS for SDHA E350fs, which maps clonal architecture (biallelic inactivation is already established by L306P plus Y408N), and tumor-informed ctDNA MRD (Signatera) as an imaging adjunct that is informative rather than therapy-triggering. Medium priority as fallbacks: SDHC promoter methylation if the SDHA story collapses (academic referral, 4 to 8 weeks), and MGMT promoter methylation, which predicts temozolomide response without gating enrollment and can wait until that agent enters the discussion.

    PIK3CA R93W¶

    Tumor-tissue NGS confirmation on a comprehensive panel (FoundationOne CDx, Tempus xT, or equivalent) is essential before the PI3Kα pathway can be acted on. R93W sits outside the validated alpelisib companion-diagnostic hotspots (E542K, E545K, H1047R, C420R) and the call is ctDNA-only, so tissue confirmation establishes whether it is a real subclonal alteration or a plasma artifact. The same archival block serves the PIK3CA, MAP2K1, and SDHA E350fs confirmations on one panel.

    MAP2K1 P124S¶

    Tumor-tissue NGS confirmation is essential before MEK-inhibitor consideration. P124 is a recognized MEK1 activating site (P124L/Q/S) with heterogeneous response data, but the variant is ctDNA-only here; tissue confirmation establishes whether it is clonal in the resected tumor or a low-allele-fraction subclonal event. Bundle it on the same block as the PIK3CA and SDHA E350fs confirmation.

    POLE R1679C¶

    The decision-relevant action is a cancer-predisposition genetic-counseling referral, not a therapy. R1679 lies outside the canonical POLE proofreading hotspots (P286R, V411L, S459F), and with TMB under 1 and MSS the polymerase-proofreading polyposis phenotype is not supported. Counseling lands the variant as a likely VUS and folds in the germline SDHA result so the family gets one coherent risk discussion. No specimen is needed; the counselor reviews the existing germline report.

    KIT/PDGFRA wild-type¶

    NF1 assessment is the relevant subtyping step (somatic NF1 sequencing with del/dup on the comprehensive tumor panel; germline NF1 is captured by the hereditary PGL/PCC panel). NF1-related GIST is the other KIT/PDGFRA-WT subtype that keeps SDHB staining intact, so it sits in this case's differential and would give the MAP2K1 P124S call a mechanistic home. It is medium priority because NF1 reads out of the comprehensive tumor panel already being sent for the ctDNA-variant confirmation, so it adds no separate draw; it matters most if the SDHA workup fails to confirm SDH deficiency.

    Workup considerations (the shared first steps)¶

    This is a biomarker-gated case. Three actions are shared first steps to take regardless of what happens later, and the entire systemic-therapy ranking below is conditional on confirming SDH-deficiency (dsdh_ihc:positive) on the re-cut:

    1. Active surveillance (lead action while NED) — see Rank 1.
    2. SDHB + SDHA IHC re-cut with a positive internal control, germline SDHA panel, and tumor-tissue NGS confirmation of the ctDNA-only variants, in one tissue pull — see Rank 2. This resolves the SDHB-intact / biallelic-SDHA paradox and locks the dSDH label that every recurrence trial screens on.
    3. NCI Rare Tumor Natural History Study enrollment + cancer-genetics counseling — see Rank 3.

    Cross-cutting caveat. The ranking is scoped to the patient's targetable features. If the SDHB/SDHA re-cut does not confirm SDH-deficiency, the systemic options (ranks 4–10) foreclose and the next call is SDHC promoter-methylation testing; standard-of-care for the indication then becomes a separate conversation with the treating team. The ctDNA-only PIK3CA R93W and MAP2K1 P124S calls are less-certain predictors than tissue-confirmed alterations and must be confirmed in tissue before the PI3Kα or MEK pathways are acted on.

    Rank 1. Active surveillance — structured imaging + PGL/PCC screening; no adjuvant systemic therapy¶

    Board: endorsed by all five personas (agreement 1.0).

    Every persona lands here as the now-move. Four ranked it first outright (advocate, concensusite, conservative, critic); the risktaker, who led with a systemic agent, states plainly that an NED patient gets no adjuvant therapy. No adjuvant agent bends the recurrence curve in this subset, so committing chronic toxicity now buys nothing and forecloses option space. NCCN and ESMO both place SDH-expression-negative GIST in the avoid-adjuvant column.

    • Likelihood of desired effect: the effect is option-preservation, not tumor control. Cross-society guidelines plus Boikos 2016 (imatinib 1/49 PR) close adjuvant systemic therapy in this subset.
    • Toxicity: none (imaging + biochemical screening; the soft cost is scan anxiety).
    • Why this rank: it is the guideline-aligned lead for NED dSDH-GIST and the unanimous board position.
    • Key risks / open questions: recurrence is the expectation (~71%), so the imaging schedule must actually run on time; multifocal-primary + resected-M1 places this patient past the cleanest localized natural-history cohorts (the ~2.5-yr median EFS is a floor, not a ceiling); an undetected functional paraganglioma in an SDHx carrier going to anesthesia is a hypertensive-crisis hazard, which is why PGL/PCC baseline screening pairs with the imaging plan. Whole-body MRI vs CT backbone and the patient's travel tolerance are unresolved.
    • Evidence: PMID 27011036, PMID 29413424, PMID 30383140.

    Rank 2. SDHB + SDHA IHC re-cut + germline SDHA panel + tumor NGS confirmation (one-tissue-pull diagnostic gate)¶

    Board: endorsed by all five personas (agreement 1.0).

    All five personas demand this. The biallelic SDHA call (~85% combined VAF) should destabilize the complex and abolish SDHB staining, yet the report reads SDHB intact. The critic put it most sharply: every dSDH-GIST trial entry phenotype runs through SDHB-IHC loss, so this is a structural threat to the whole trial portfolio, not a footnote. The germline SDHA result drives cascade testing and the lifetime PGL program; the SDHA IHC arbitrates whether this is SDHA-driven or a non-SDHA SDHx driver (where SDHC methylation is the fallback).

    • Likelihood of desired effect: diagnostic certainty — resolves the SDHB paradox, confirms the driver, and gates trial eligibility.
    • Toxicity: none (germline blood draw, IHC re-cut on archival FFPE, comprehensive tumor NGS).
    • Why this rank: load-bearing for everything downstream; it adds only 2–4 weeks before any recurrence contingency can move.
    • Key risk: if SDHB and SDHA IHC both read intact after a clean re-stain, the dSDH label collapses, the trial-route options foreclose, and SDHC promoter methylation becomes the next fallback (academic referral, 4–8 weeks). Whether PIK3CA R93W / MAP2K1 P124S confirm in tissue determines whether the PI3Kα / MEK pathways survive at all.
    • Evidence: PMID 39927693, PMID 35546442, PMID 23023976.

    Rank 3. NCI Rare Tumor Natural History Study (NCT03739827) + cancer-genetics counseling¶

    Board: endorsed by advocate, concensusite, conservative, risktaker (agreement 0.8).

    The patient is NED, so most contingency trials are blocked by measurable-disease requirements, but the registry is not; it provides NIH wt-GIST clinic access, the SDHC-methylation fallback pathway, biospecimen banking, and family cascade testing. The counseling visit folds the germline SDHA result and the POLE R1679C VUS framing into one coherent family-risk conversation. NCCN mandates genetic-counseling referral for every SDH-deficient GIST.

    • Likelihood of desired effect: high infrastructure yield; no tumor-control claim.
    • Toxicity: none (observational enrollment + a counseling visit; one in-person NIH baseline).
    • Why this rank: the only move that honors the trial preference today at zero toxicity. The open variable is whether the patient's geography supports the baseline visit (OHSU / Texas Children's are alternative operational fits).
    • Evidence: NCT03739827, PMID 21173220, PMID 32424176.

    Ranks 4–10 are recurrence contingencies, each conditional on SDH-deficiency confirmed on the re-cut (If SDH-deficiency is confirmed on SDHB/SDHA IHC re-cut). None is a now-move while the patient is NED.

    Rank 4. Sunitinib at first measurable recurrence (not adjuvant)¶

    Conditional on dSDH confirmation. Board: endorsed by advocate, concensusite, conservative, critic (agreement 0.8).

    The one FDA-approved, guideline-preferred contingency with dSDH-specific data. It is reached at measurable recurrence regardless of the exact WT-GIST subtype.

    • Likelihood of desired effect: moderate-to-low — 7/38 (18%) objective response in dSDH-GIST (Boikos 2016), the best-replicated systemic signal but well below KIT-mutant activity. The critic reads 18% as directional rather than dependable (retrospective, ROBINS-I Moderate).
    • Toxicity: Moderate (fatigue, hand-foot skin reaction, hypertension, diarrhea) — the most predictable toxicity tail on the table.
    • Counter-productive MoA: Low — anti-angiogenic VEGFR blockade can impair wound healing if a future re-resection is on the table.
    • Why this rank: the consensus opening move at recurrence; predictable where the newer agents are not. The advocate's only push (a preference qualification, not a dissent) is that the trial menu should sit alongside sunitinib for a trial-preferring patient.
    • Evidence: PMID 27011036, PMID 17046465.

    Rank 5. Temozolomide (MGMT-methylation-enriched), trial route preferred¶

    Conditional on dSDH confirmation. Status: considered with caveats. Board: endorsed by concensusite, conservative, critic, risktaker (agreement 0.8).

    Doubly gated: it needs both dSDH confirmation and MGMT methylation (~24% prevalence, status unknown here), and the entire clinical signal rests on a 5-patient series.

    • Likelihood of desired effect: moderate only if MGMT-methylated — 2/5 PR (Yebra 2022); low or absent in unmethylated tumors. Order the MGMT test now so the result is ready if recurrence makes this live.
    • Toxicity: Moderate (thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, fatigue, nausea) — not worth incurring in an MGMT-unmethylated tumor.
    • Counter-productive MoA: Low — alkylator exposure without MGMT methylation incurs cytopenias for little effect.
    • Why this rank: what is guideline-aligned is the trial-enrollment route, not temozolomide as a standard agent; n=5 is hypothesis-generating. Enrolling trial NCT05661643 (Asan, Korea) requires measurable disease.
    • Evidence: PMID 34426440, PMID 36198483, NCT05661643.

    Rank 6. Olverembatinib (HQP1351), trial route only¶

    Conditional on dSDH confirmation. Status: considered with caveats. Board: endorsed by conservative, critic, risktaker (agreement 0.6).

    • Likelihood of desired effect: moderate — 6/26 (23.1%) PR, mPFS 25.7 mo (Qiu 2025) in the exact subtype, but a single phase-1b cohort, unreplicated.
    • Toxicity: Low (anemia, transaminitis, hyperuricemia, leukocytosis; all G3+ rates < 20%) — the cleanest safety sheet of the new agents, though figures are pooled across the full 66-patient basket, not the 26-patient GIST cohort.
    • Counter-productive MoA: Moderate — non-canonical mechanism in a KIT-WT tumor; efficacy borrows from VEGFR/FGFR/HIF-CD36 axes, not the labeled KIT/ABL target.
    • Why this rank: two structural caveats, agreed rather than dissented. The "phase 3" NCT06640361 is single-arm with a PFS-rate endpoint and no comparator, so it will not deliver randomized evidence; and its SDHB-IHC-loss entry criterion is one this patient may not currently meet until the re-cut resolves the intact stain. Access outside China (Ascentage) is uncertain.
    • Evidence: PMID 41184234, NCT06640361.

    Rank 7. Rogaratinib (pan-FGFR1–4), trial route only¶

    Conditional on dSDH confirmation. Status: considered with caveats. Board: endorsed by conservative, critic, risktaker; dissent by concensusite (agreement 0.4).

    The most contested row on the board. The risktaker ranked it first as the biggest swing; the concensusite is the clean dissent (a single-arm n=24 cohort closed to accrual appears in no guideline and cannot head the plan).

    • Likelihood of desired effect: highest reported — 10/24 (41.7%) PR, mPFS 31 mo (Merriam 2026) — but single-arm n=24 and unreplicated; the 91.7% DCR may be inflated by dSDH indolence, though the 41.7% PR fraction stands as genuine regression.
    • Toxicity: unresolved — the per-grade AE table is not retrievable from the abstract, the empty ClinicalTrials.gov results tab, or the paywalled full text (class effects: hyperphosphatemia, fatigue, diarrhea, reported without grades). This is the conservative's dissent (on toxicity, not a veto, since nothing is treated today).
    • Counter-productive MoA: Moderate — single-arm DCR may reflect indolence rather than drug effect; FGFR dependence unreplicated.
    • Why this rank: a real, possibly best-in-subtype efficacy signal held to trial-route-only by an access wall (closed to accrual; Bayer has stepped back) and an unverifiable safety table. The recruiting pemigatinib cohort (Rank 9) is the practical way to cash the FGFR bet.
    • Evidence: PMID 42191879, PMID 31666694, NCT04595747.

    Rank 8. Belzutifan (HIF-2α inhibitor), off-label or LITESPARK-015 wt-GIST cohort¶

    Conditional on dSDH confirmation. Status: considered with caveats. Board: endorsed by advocate, risktaker (agreement 0.4).

    • Likelihood of desired effect: uncertain in GIST — no readout; the only efficacy anchor is the cross-indication PPGL cohort (ORR 26%, LITESPARK-015) in sibling SDH-loss biology.
    • Toxicity: Moderate (anemia, fatigue, hypoxia) — anemia compounded here by the Roux-en-Y anatomy (B12/iron malabsorption).
    • Counter-productive MoA: Low — direct driver-axis blockade, but a mechanism-matched agent (guadecitabine) returned 0/9 in this subtype, so target engagement may not translate.
    • Why this rank: what belzutifan has that the FGFR agents lack is access (approved, usable off-label without a trial slot). The HIF-2α pipeline beyond it has thinned (DFF332, NKT-2152 discontinued). Watch the LITESPARK-015 wt-GIST cohort (NCT04924075) for an ASCO 2026 / WCGIC readout.
    • Evidence: NCT04924075, PMID 35324464, PMID 15652751.

    Rank 9. Pemigatinib (FGFR1–3), PEMIGIST trial (NCT07434843, recruiting, DFCI)¶

    Conditional on dSDH confirmation. Status: considered with caveats. Board: endorsed by advocate, risktaker (agreement 0.4).

    • Likelihood of desired effect: unknown — no clinical readout in dSDH-GIST; the rationale rests entirely on the rogaratinib FGFR signal and a shared PDX response. Another 24-patient single-arm design.
    • Toxicity: Moderate (hyperphosphatemia, stomatitis, serous retinal detachment, nail/skin changes) on a days-1–14-of-21 schedule needing phosphate + ophthalmologic monitoring.
    • Counter-productive MoA: Low — shares rogaratinib's unreplicated-pathway uncertainty.
    • Why this rank: for a trial-preferring patient this is the more actionable FGFR option because it is recruiting and US-based, even though rogaratinib carries the higher published number — lead with the door that opens. Suzanne George (DFCI) is the logical referral.
    • Evidence: NCT07434843, PMID 31666694, PMID 42191879.

    Rank 10. Regorafenib — guideline-sequenced 3L TKI after sunitinib¶

    Conditional on dSDH confirmation. Status: considered with caveats. Board: endorsed by concensusite (agreement 0.2).

    • Likelihood of desired effect: low/modest — no dSDH-specific data; GRID HR 0.27 is a KIT-mutant result, dSDH activity extrapolated.
    • Toxicity: Moderate (hand-foot skin reaction G3+ 20%, hypertension G3+ 23%, diarrhea).
    • Counter-productive MoA: Low — anti-angiogenic; dSDH benefit extrapolated, not demonstrated.
    • Why this rank: the established 3L rung once sunitinib is exhausted, with the inference gap stated out loud rather than letting the HR 0.27 carry weight it did not earn in this genotype (NCCN sequencing is category-2A consensus).
    • Evidence: PMID 23177515, PMID 40045030.

    Rank 11. DRUP off-label matched-therapy basket (NCT02925234) — post-board re-screen addition¶

    Post-board addition, not board-voted. A recurrence-contingent access route to matched trametinib (for MAP2K1 P124S) or alpelisib (for PIK3CA R93W), not a variant-matched efficacy readout.

    Status: considered with caveats. Not deliberated by the board — surfaced by a post-board re-screen of the trial registry, so agreement is 0.0 by absence of a vote, not by neutral consensus.

    A registry re-screen after the board adjourned turned up the Drug Rediscovery Protocol, which matches an approved drug to a genomic profile through its own molecular tumor board rather than a fixed gene list. On paper that opens two routes for this patient: trametinib against the ctDNA MAP2K1 P124S call, alpelisib against the ctDNA PIK3CA R93W call. What it does not open is an efficacy readout — DRUP publishes pooled clinical benefit across all its cohorts, never per-arm, so no number attaches to either route here.

    • Likelihood of desired effect: unknown at these variants. The only figure DRUP reports is pooled: 34% clinical benefit (objective response or SD ≥16 weeks), median duration 9 months (95% CI 8–11), across 215 patients spanning many drug-variant pairs (PMID 31570881). Neither the MAP2K1 nor the PIK3CA arm has a published effect size, both calls are ctDNA-only, and R93W is non-canonical.
    • Toxicity: Moderate — pooled treatment-related grade ≥3 AEs ran 18% (rare-cancer subgroup) to 24% (common-cancer subgroup) in the 500-patient DRUP update, with no fatal treatment-related events (PMID 35046062). There is no per-arm AE table; trametinib-arm (rash, diarrhea, retinopathy, cardiomyopathy) and alpelisib-arm (hyperglycemia, rash, diarrhea) class effects come from each drug's label, not from DRUP.
    • Counter-productive MoA: Moderate — the MEK / PI3Kα match rests on ctDNA-only, non-canonical variants; R93W outside the alpelisib hotspots may not confer the pathway dependence the match assumes.
    • Why this rank: it lands low for the same reason the board declined to rank any PI3Kα or MEK option in round 1 — the critic and the concensusite both passed on that axis because R93W and P124S are ctDNA-only and non-canonical, and that objection carries straight over. The same gates that hold the other MAP2K1/PIK3CA baskets (MEGALiT, tersolisib, RLY-2608) at weak fit apply here: tissue confirmation of the variants, R93W outside the alpelisib companion-diagnostic hotspots, a protocol that requires progression on standard therapy with measurable disease, and Dutch-only siting. It is logged so the enrollment door is visible, framed as an access route rather than evidence of benefit — the board did not endorse, dissent from, or veto it, and this row does not manufacture a vote it never received.
    • Evidence: NCT02925234, PMID 31570881, PMID 35046062.

    Rank 12. Adjuvant imatinib — NOT RECOMMENDED¶

    Status: not recommended (veto by advocate; agreement −0.4).

    Logged so the reflex "GIST means imatinib" reach is documented and not re-litigated on the post-op note. KIT is wild-type and the response rate is ~2% (1/49 PR, Boikos 2016); the subset was never represented in the adjuvant RCTs and does not respond. There is no version of the efficacy-toxicity weight that rescues a 2% response against three years of periorbital edema, fatigue, cytopenias, and monitoring burden, which would also spend the screening eligibility for the contingency trials the patient prefers. Kept in the table (not dropped) per the no-silent-veto rule.

    • Evidence: PMID 27011036, PMID 19303137, PMID 30383140.

    Classes examined but not ranked¶

    • PI3Kα inhibitors (alpelisib, inavolisib, RLY-2608) as standalone recs — the only PIK3CA call is R93W, a non-canonical, out-of-hotspot variant detected on ctDNA only; predictive validity is not established at this variant, so none is ranked on its own merits. Alpelisib appears only as one arm of the DRUP access route at Rank 11, contingent on tissue confirmation and molecular-tumor-board sign-off.
    • MEK inhibitors (trametinib, cobimetinib) as standalone recs — rest on the ctDNA-only MAP2K1 P124S call; cross-tumor and unconfirmed. Trametinib appears only as the other arm of the DRUP access route at Rank 11, not as an evidenced pick.
    • Glutaminase inhibition (telaglenastat / CB-839) — a dedicated SDH-deficient GIST trial cohort existed but the program was discontinued, and the metabolic rationale is mechanistically undercut (pyruvate carboxylation, not glutamine, is the dominant anaplerotic route in SDH-loss). Not enrollable.
    • Mechanism-matched agents already closed by negative subtype-specific trials: guadecitabine (0/9), linsitinib / IGF1R (0/20), vandetanib (0/9).
    • Tumor-agnostic ICI (pembrolizumab) for POLE — excluded: POLE R1679C is outside the proofreading hotspots and the tumor is TMB <1 / MSS, so this is a counseling item, not a therapy.
