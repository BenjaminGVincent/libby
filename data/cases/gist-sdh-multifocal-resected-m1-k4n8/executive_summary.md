# Executive summary

**Case:** `gist-sdh-multifocal-resected-m1-k4n8`
**Question:** In a currently-NED patient with multifocal, R0-resected SDH-deficient gastric GIST (biallelic SDHA, KIT/PDGFRA wild-type) plus one completely resected M1 deposit, what targetable-feature-directed strategy fits now, and what is the recurrence plan once SDH-deficiency is confirmed?
**Evidence base:** 22 trials, 28 clinical-evidence rows, 27 preclinical entries, and 13 target-validation paths; the five-persona board ran on this case for the first time (20 cross-critiques), with agreement scores from 1.0 down to −0.4.

## What this report covers

The PI's synthesis of the board into an 11-row ranking: three shared first steps to take while NED, seven recurrence contingencies gated on confirming SDH-deficiency, and one option logged as rejected.

## Top-line findings

- Active surveillance is the now-move. No adjuvant agent bends the recurrence curve in this subtype, so committing chronic toxicity buys nothing. All five personas endorsed it (agreement 1.0).
- The SDHB/SDHA IHC re-cut is the load-bearing gate. It resolves the SDHB-intact / biallelic-SDHA paradox and locks the dSDH label that every recurrence trial screens on (agreement 1.0).
- Systemic agents are recurrence contingencies, not now-moves. The newest positive signals, olverembatinib (23.1% ORR) and rogaratinib (41.7% ORR, but the trial is closed to accrual and its per-grade safety table is unretrievable), stay at single-arm grade. Rogaratinib is the most contested row, with a clean concensusite dissent and a conservative dissent on the unverifiable toxicity.
- Adjuvant imatinib is not recommended (advocate veto, agreement −0.4): KIT is wild-type and the response rate is roughly 2%.
- The ranking is scoped to the patient's targetable features. A re-cut that does not confirm SDH-deficiency forecloses the systemic contingencies, makes SDHC promoter methylation the next call, and turns standard care into a separate care-team conversation this report does not cover.

## Recommendation summary

**Shared first steps (while NED):**

1. Active surveillance + PGL/PCC screening; no adjuvant systemic therapy.
2. SDHB/SDHA IHC re-cut + germline SDHA panel + tumor NGS confirmation.
3. NCI Rare Tumor registry (NCT03739827) + genetics counseling.

**Recurrence contingencies, each conditional on `dsdh_ihc` positive, foreclosed if the re-cut is negative:**

1. **Sunitinib**: the one FDA-approved, dSDH-specific option (18% ORR); modest but predictable.
2. **Temozolomide**: doubly gated on MGMT methylation; the signal rests on n=5.
3. **Olverembatinib**: 23.1% ORR and the cleanest safety sheet, but phase 1b and China-sponsored.
4. **Rogaratinib**: best ORR (41.7%) but n=24, closed to accrual, safety unverified.
5. **Belzutifan**: direct HIF-2α shot, accessible off-label, no GIST readout yet.
6. **Pemigatinib**: the open FGFR door (recruiting), riding the rogaratinib signal.
7. **Regorafenib**: guideline-sequenced 3L; dSDH benefit extrapolated from KIT-mutant data.

## What this report does *not* cover

It does not set dosing, monitoring schedules, line sequencing, or payer and access logistics, and it does not cover standard care should SDH-deficiency fail to confirm. The frame is decision support.

## How to use this report

Each ranked option carries the board's agreement state and one to three anchor citations. The ctDNA-only PIK3CA R93W and MAP2K1 P124S calls are weaker predictors than tissue-confirmed alterations and must be confirmed in tissue before the PI3Kα or MEK pathways are acted on. The full per-trial extraction lives on the live case page; this PDF is the synthesis.
