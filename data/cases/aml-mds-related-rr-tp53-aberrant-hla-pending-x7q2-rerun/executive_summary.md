# Executive summary

**Case:** `aml-mds-related-rr-tp53-aberrant-hla-pending-x7q2-rerun`
**Question:** In relapsed/refractory MDS-related AML at ~85% marrow blasts, six years after a matched-sibling allo-HCT and now refractory to FLAG-IDA plus venetoclax, which interventions reach the patient's HLA-restricted immunotherapy handles (HA-1/HA-2 TCR-T, WT1, PRAME), her CD123- and CD33-positive blasts, a Y220C-contingent TP53 aberration, and a second-transplant platform?
**Evidence base:** 29 screened trials, 15 clinical-evidence rows, 16 preclinical rows, and 13 target-validation rows across five feature axes; board agreement ran from unanimous (1.0) on the workup and the second-transplant backbone down to -0.2 on flotetuzumab, with one conditional veto (the conservative, on pivekimab) that stands because its lifting condition is unmet.

## What this report covers

The PI ranked seven interventions across five personas and two rounds. This PDF packages that ranking, the HLA gate the whole immunotherapy axis hangs on, and the board's dissents and standing veto for a reader who has not seen the live site.

## Top-line findings

- The rank-1 action is a test, not a drug: allele-level HLA-A*02:01/A*24:02 typing plus HA-1/HA-2 genotyping of both the patient and her sibling donor. Every HLA-restricted lever is stranded until it returns, and all five personas placed it first.
- The curative backbone is Iomab-B (131I-apamistamab) conditioning into a second allo-HCT, unanimously endorsed but deliverable only after a fitness and organ-function workup that has not been done. SIERRA's durable-CR signal was earned in first-transplant patients, so it reads indirectly onto a prior-HCT relapse.
- The referral's own priority, HA-1 TCR-T, ranks third: curative intent, but a single-arm n=9 feasibility cohort, and the critic's evidence-quality caution persists even in the positive branch.
- The ranking is scoped to the stated targetable features. A negative HLA-A*02:01 exhausts the within-scope HLA-restricted ranks, and standard salvage care for the leukemia becomes a separate care-team conversation this report does not cover.
- The conservative's veto holds pivekimab out of use on a veno-occlusive-disease signal ahead of transplant; it lifts only once the currently absent hepatic and albumin baselines clear it. TP53 stays aberrant pending confirmation, not collapsed to wild-type on a 2%-LOD panel.

## Recommendation summary

**Shared first step:** Rank 1, the HLA-A*02:01/A*24:02 typing plus HA-1/HA-2 genotyping that opens or forecloses every HLA-restricted option.

1. **Iomab-B conditioning + second allo-HCT.** The one randomized backbone in the dossier; unanimous, gated on an undone fitness workup.
2. **HA-1 TCR-T.** Highest ceiling, thinnest evidence (n=9 feasibility). Conditional on `hla_a0201` positive; foreclosed if the type or the mismatch is negative.
3. **Tagraxofusp.** A labeled CD123 agent, but its headline efficacy is BPDCN cross-tumor and its capillary-leak deaths demand organ-function labs first.
4. **Gemtuzumab ozogamicin.** The on-label CD33 handle whose own tier-1a evidence shows no survival benefit in this adverse karyotype.
5. **Pivekimab sunirine** *(not recommended).* The best own-disease CD123 data, held under the conservative's live veno-occlusive-disease veto.
6. **Flotetuzumab.** A discontinued CD123xCD3 drug carrying a post-hoc TP53 signal, not an enrollable route.

## What this report does *not* cover

Dose adjustments, monitoring schedules, sequencing across lines, and payer or access mechanics are out of scope, as is standard salvage care that does not act on one of these handles.

## How to use this report

Each ranked option carries the board's agreement state and one to three anchor citations. The full per-trial extraction and per-persona rationale live on the case page; this PDF is the synthesis.
