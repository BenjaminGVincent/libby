# Executive summary

**Case:** `pancreatic-mets-kras-g12d-basal-arid1a-k9r3`
**Question:** In newly diagnosed metastatic basal-like pancreatic adenocarcinoma (treatment-naive, large primary plus multiple liver metastases), what can target the KRAS G12D driver or the ARID1A loss-of-function synthetic-lethal axis?
**Evidence base:** 16 trials, 13 clinical-evidence rows (6 ranked anchors), 8 preclinical rows, and 6 target-validation rows; board agreement scores run from -0.2 to 1.0. The top three tie at 1.0, every persona endorsed them, and the fight is about order, not membership. Nobody issued a veto.

## What this report covers

The PI scoped the ranking to two molecular targets, KRAS G12D and ARID1A loss, and ranked seven therapeutic options under a single unbranched list since every biomarker reads as confirmed. This PDF carries that synthesis plus the diagnostic workup that hardens the target calls.

## Top-line findings

- The immunotherapy axis here is a mirage. A lone somatic MLH1 variant sits in an MSS, TMB-8.6, germline-negative tumor that most likely reads as MMR-proficient, so checkpoint monotherapy is low-yield. MMR protein IHC is the gate before any dMMR/MSI-H claim.
- Rank 1, daraxonrasib, is the only ranked option carrying a positive randomized phase 3 (RASolute 302: OS HR 0.40, 95% CI 0.30-0.53, n=500, [PMID 42223072](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42223072)). That result was measured at second line while the patient is treatment-naive, so the first-line benefit rests on the unread RASolute 303, and his G12D estimate is borrowed from the RAS G12 subgroup.
- The board's one persistent down-rank falls on HRS-4642 (rank 5, agreement -0.2): a 90.3% grade 3+ rate with one treatment-related death, and a 63.3% front-line ORR that is confounded with its chemotherapy backbone. Its Nature Medicine report has no PubMed record yet, so it is cited by DOI ([10.1038/s41591-026-04538-9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04538-9)).
- The ranking is targetable-feature-scoped: only KRAS-G12D-directed and ARID1A-directed agents appear. A negative MMR IHC does not change that, and standard first-line care for this indication is a separate conversation with the treating team that this report does not cover.
- The patient is HLA-C*05:01/C*06:02, not C*08:02, so the published anti-KRAS-G12D TCR-T cannot present his epitope. The ASXL1 plasma frameshift may be clonal hematopoiesis and needs paired buffy-coat sequencing before it counts as a tumor target.

## Recommendation summary

1. **daraxonrasib (RMC-6236), pan-RAS(ON)** via RASolute 303. The only ranked option with randomized phase 3 survival data; first-line benefit inferred from an unread trial.
2. **GFH375 / VS-7375, G12D-selective + cetuximab** via NCT07644559. The loudest allele-matched response (ORR 41%) with an open 1L cohort, but China-based, one line early, and the cetuximab doublet is uncharacterized at 79.
3. **zoldonrasib (RMC-9805), G12D-selective** via RASolute 305. Allele-matched and the gentlest agent in the set, but the evidence is a single-arm n=40 abstract with no durability readout.
4. **INCB161734, G12D-selective + chemo** via DAWN-303. Strongest single-agent response number, but the enrollable front-line construct bundles chemotherapy with about 40% grade 3+ neutropenia.
5. **HRS-4642 + gem/nab-paclitaxel** via NCT07232875. The highest front-line ORR on the exact allele, but a 90.3% grade 3+ rate with a treatment-related death, an IV backbone, and a phase 3 not yet recruiting.
6. **Pan-KRAS(ON) first-in-human class** (BBO-11818, BLU-924, PF-07934040, LY3962673). On-axis but pre-data: no efficacy denominator exists, so a bank-for-later class.
7. **ceralasertib (AZD6738), ATR inhibitor** for the ARID1A axis via NCT03682289. A banked second axis. No PDAC responder exists, entry is BAF250a-IHC-gated, and the lead basket is not recruiting.

## What this report does *not* cover

Dose adjustments, monitoring schedules, sequencing across lines, and payer or access logistics all sit outside this synthesis. It is decision support for the targetable-feature question, not a treatment plan.
