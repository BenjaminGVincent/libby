# Executive summary

**Case:** `hgsoc-tuboovarian-stage3c-r0-hrd-pending-idyq`
**Question:** Which maintenance interventions fit an R0-resected stage IIIc high-grade serous tubo-ovarian carcinoma now that tumor NGS confirms a BRCA-wild-type but genomically-unstable tumor, across the homologous-recombination, angiogenesis, endocrine, and on-study IL-12 axes?
**Evidence base:** 14 screened trials, 8 clinical-evidence rows, 11 preclinical rows; six ranked interventions with board-agreement scores from 0.6 down to -0.2, and no single lead the board converged on.

## What this report covers

The PI ranked six maintenance options, each tied to its registration evidence and the board's agreement state. This PDF is that synthesis, for a reader who has not seen the live case page.

## Top-line findings

- Every biomarker has now resulted. The tumor is BRCA-wild-type on both germline and somatic testing but genomically unstable on a qualitative Altera call, which is not the same as a validated companion-diagnostic HRD-positive result.
- Rucaparib (ATHENA-MONO intent-to-treat mPFS 20.2 vs 9.2 months, HR 0.52) takes rank 1: a replicated all-comer phase 3 that qualifies a BRCA-wild-type tumor with no GIS gate. Its grade 3+ anemia (28.7%) presses the patient's myelosuppression veto, so it is an evidence near-tie with niraparib, not a toxicity escape.
- The objections sit at the bottom. Olaparib + bevacizumab (rank 6) holds a double veto: the conservative on stacked VTE/myelosuppression toxicity against the patient's IVC-filter history, the critic on the HR 0.33 being a subgroup, not the ITT (HR 0.59). Niraparib (rank 2) carries a conditional veto from the conservative and a standing advocate dissent, both on myelosuppression.
- One workup remains, gating only rank 6. A validated MyChoice CDx GIS at or above 42 makes the patient PAOLA-1-eligible and reorders olaparib + bevacizumab upward; below 42 she is HRD-negative and that one option is foreclosed. Every other ranked option stands regardless.
- Open question: deliverability of the PARP options at this patient's marrow reserve (baseline WBC 3.5) is unproven until a platelet-based hematology dose plan is documented.

## Recommendation summary

1. **Rucaparib (1L maintenance)**. Replicated all-comer PARP; no veto, no dissent, but grade 3+ anemia sits on the named veto.
2. **Niraparib (1L maintenance)**. Cleanest guideline fit with the only tumor-specific pharmacology; conditional veto and advocate dissent on myelosuppression.
3. **Bevacizumab (continued maintenance)**. The only option clearing both vetoes, on a backbone she already tolerates, but PFS-only with a live VTE/bleeding caution.
4. **GEN-1 / IMNN-001 (IL-12 immunogene)**. On-study continuation with a real OS signal (HR 0.69), but sponsor-topline-immature and intraperitoneal.
5. **Letrozole (endocrine maintenance)**. Lowest-toxicity oral option clearing every veto; evidence is a single ROBINS-I-serious retrospective series.
6. **Olaparib + bevacizumab (PAOLA-1)**. Highest magnitude if HRD-positive, but contingent on a GIS she lacks and double-vetoed.

## What this report does *not* cover

Dose adjustments, cytopenia-monitoring schedules, sequencing across later lines, and payer or access logistics are out of scope. This is decision support for the maintenance question, not a treatment plan.

## How to use this report

Each ranked option carries the board's agreement state by persona and one to three anchor citations. The full per-trial extraction and the per-persona rationale live on the live case page; this PDF is the synthesis.
