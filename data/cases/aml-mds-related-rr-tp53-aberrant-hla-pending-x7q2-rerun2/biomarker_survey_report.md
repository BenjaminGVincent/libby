Twenty biomarkers were surveyed: all nine tumor-agnostic markers and eleven of the panel's 72 protein targets. Not one is measured to decision resolution. Eighteen have no usable result on file, and the two with results cannot support a decision as they stand: CD123 positivity is a qualitative flow call without antigen density, and the HLA class I genotype, typed at high resolution for the 2020 sibling transplant, survives only as a placeholder in the EHR. That genotype is the one essential item, and it is the master switch. Until the 4-digit type is retrieved or repeated, PRAME and MAGE-A1 read as unassessable rather than negative, and the HA-1/HA-2 and WT1 handles, the stated reason this case was submitted, cannot be adjudicated.

Closing all of it takes three orders, none needing a biopsy. A flow panel on the next marrow or blood specimen adds quantitative CD123 and CD33 density plus CLEC12A, CD70, LILRB4, CD37, CD200, B7-H3 and AXL. A comprehensive genomic panel, already indicated for the TP53 question, returns TMB, MSI, BRAF and RNA fusions on the same run. One blood draw covers HLA typing, HA-1/HA-2 genotyping and a germline predisposition panel, for the patient and the sibling donor both. BRAF may cost nothing: a records request for the 2019 and 2026 covered-gene lists would settle whether codon 600 was ever sequenced.

Five of the six tumor-agnostic approvals here are written for solid tumors, checkpoint blockade after an allograft brings a graft-versus-host hazard of its own, and most of these markers will be negative if tested. The argument for testing is the size of the option a positive opens, not any implied likelihood of one. CD33 density, TP53 with the Y220C question, and WT1 with HA-1/HA-2 appear in neither reference panel; the target validation report works them directly from the profile.
