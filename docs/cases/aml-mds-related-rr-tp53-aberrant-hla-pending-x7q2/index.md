## Research question

In relapsed/refractory MDS-related AML at ~85% marrow blasts, six years after a matched-sibling allo-HCT and now refractory to FLAG-IDA plus venetoclax, what interventions could target the HLA-restricted minor-antigen axis (HA-1/HA-2, WT1), the CD33/CD123 surface antigens, a possible TP53 Y220C handle, and a second-transplant platform, gated on retrieving HLA-A*02:01 typing, genotyping HA-1/HA-2 in the patient and sibling donor, and resolving the TP53 discrepancy?

## Patient profile (scrubbed)

- Woman, 50-59, ECOG 1.
- AML, myelodysplasia-related, relapsed/refractory; transformed from prior MDS (RCMD, IPSS-R high/very-high, ~2019).
- Marrow ~85% blasts (June 2026); peripheral blasts rose 58% to 82% in late June 2026.
- Extramedullary right-thigh myeloid sarcoma on MRI (May 2026), an adverse feature.
- Late relapse ~6 years after a matched-sibling PBSC allo-HCT (~2020, CR sustained 72 months); pattern reads as graft-versus-leukemia escape, not graft failure.
- Refractory to FLAG-IDA + venetoclax reinduction (best response PD).
- **TP53: aberrant, pending confirmation, not resolved in either direction.** Pathogenic on 2019 diagnostic NGS; reported negative on the 2026 relapse panel, but that panel has a ~2% VAF limit of detection, no copy-number detection, and is not MRD-grade. Working call: TP53-aberrant pending confirmation. Y220C status unknown.
- **HLA-A*02:01: unknown / pending.** High-resolution typing was done ~2020 but the allele-level result is only a chart placeholder. A*24:02 also pending.
- **HA-1 / HA-2 minor histocompatibility antigens: unknown for BOTH patient and sibling donor.** HLA-identical siblings can still differ here.
- CD33-positive and CD123-positive blasts by flow (both confirmed, qualitative only; quantitative antigen density requested).
- Immunophenotype: CD34+, CD13+, CD117+ variably increased, HLA-DR variably decreased, CD38 decreased. FLT3 / IDH1 / IDH2 negative (2019).
- Cytogenetics: complex, adverse. Shared del(5q)/del(7q) and a TP53 founder across 2019 and 2026 point to evolution of the original clone; 2026 adds MECOM gain and KMT2A amplification (4 copies).
- No numeric organ-function or cardiac labs supplied; fitness assessment is an explicit open item.

## Target validation paths

The whole plan hinges on three pending gates, none of them resolved. High-resolution HLA class I typing plus HA-1/HA-2 genotyping decide whether donor TCR-T (TSC-100/TSC-101 via NCT05473910) is reachable at all; a variant-level relapse NGS panel decides whether the Y220C-selective reactivator rezatapopt (via PYNNACLE, NCT04585750) is in play; and STR chimerism on sorted CD34+ blasts decides whether the relapse is even recipient-derived and therefore targetable by minor-antigen T cells. If HLA-A*02:01 comes back absent, the HA-1/HA-2 and WT1 TCR-T levers both close, and the next conversation about standard salvage care belongs to the treating team rather than to this report.

### HLA class I typing and HA-1/HA-2 minor antigens

Essential, and the first thing to do. Retrieve the 2020 allele-level HLA-A typing (report A*02:01 and A*24:02) from the transplant or donor-registry chart, same-day if the record exists and 3-7 days to repeat if it cannot be found. A*02:01 is the master switch for the entire restricted axis. In parallel, genotype HA-1/HA-2 in both the patient and the HLA-identical sibling donor: TSC-100 (HA-1) and TSC-101 (HA-2) via NCT05473910 require a recipient-positive, donor-negative mismatch, and HLA-identical siblings can still differ here. This is a single-SNP PCR genotype, roughly 1-2 weeks, most reliably run through the ALLOHA sponsor central lab because standalone HA-1/HA-2 testing is not a routine catalog assay. Skipping the donor arm leaves eligibility indeterminate even when the patient genotype looks favorable.

### TP53

Held aberrant pending confirmation, not resolved in either direction. A comprehensive relapse myeloid NGS panel with variant-level readout and copy-number detection (1-3 weeks) is exactly what the 2026 relapse panel lacked, given its 2% limit of detection and absence of copy-number calling; it names the specific amino-acid change and gates rezatapopt via NCT04585750, which needs a confirmed Y220C. Read it together with three high-priority orthogonal checks rather than trusting any one alone: TP53 (17p13) FISH for copy loss, p53 IHC (clone DO-7) for an aberrant overexpression or null pattern, and retrieval of the 2019 diagnostic report for the original variant identity and VAF. Do not down-call to wild-type on the strength of the limited relapse panel.

### Second-transplant platform

STR chimerism on FACS-sorted CD34+ blasts (1-2 weeks) is the load-bearing test here. It confirms whether the relapse is recipient-derived evolution of the original clone or a donor-derived leukemia, a distinction the sex-matched sibling donor keeps XY-FISH from settling. Cell sorting is the step that matters, since a bulk-marrow result cannot resolve blast-clone origin, and recipient origin is a prerequisite for any HA-directed TCR-T. Alongside it, a full relapse metaphase karyotype characterizes the adverse MECOM rearrangement and KMT2A amplification for risk stratification and trial eligibility, and right-thigh contrast MRI plus whole-body FDG-PET/CT maps the extramedullary myeloid sarcoma for a directed-radiotherapy decision before or bridging to the transplant.

### CD33

Quantitative CD33 antigen-density flow (antibodies-bound-per-cell, 3-5 days) upgrades the current qualitative-positive call into the metric that predicts gemtuzumab ozogamicin benefit. Request the density readout explicitly; a standard panel reports positivity, not the antigen density that drives ADC selection.

### CD123

Quantitative CD123 antigen-density flow, batched on the same fresh draw to conserve marrow, guides CD123-directed selection (tagraxofusp, pivekimab sunirine) and dual CD33/CD123 cytoreduction ahead of any cellular or transplant consolidation.

## Scope summary

The search returned 25 screened trial rows, 16 clinical-evidence rows (13 included), and 15 preclinical mechanism rows across six feature axes: HLA-restricted minor-antigen and WT1 TCR-T, CD33- and CD123-directed agents, TP53 Y220C reactivation, and the second-transplant platform. Board agreement ran from unanimous (score 1.0) on the confirmatory workup, on second allo-HCT, and on Iomab-B conditioning, down to -0.2 on rezatapopt and WT1 TCR-T. No formal vetoes were issued; the preferences file carries no toxicity vetoes. The disagreement was almost entirely about order, not membership: all five personas endorsed a curative-intent arc of cytoreduce, carry into a second transplant, then consolidate on the HLA-restricted axis, and split on whether the stated-reason HA-1 TCR-T belongs near the top (risktaker, advocate) or last on evidence maturity (critic), with the conservative and concensusite centering the transplant itself.

## Cross-cutting caveat (read first)

**Three pending diagnostics gate the options this case was submitted for, and none of them is resolved.** The HLA-restricted immunotherapy the patient came here for is doubly gated: HLA-A*02:01 is only a chart placeholder, and HA-directed TCR-T additionally needs a recipient HA-1(H)/HA-2-positive, donor-negative mismatch that has not been genotyped in either the patient or the sibling donor. RNA or qualitative typing does not substitute for allele-level HLA plus a paired mHAg genotype. The TP53 handle is separately double-gated: the 2019-pathogenic-versus-2026-negative discrepancy sits on a 2% LOD, CNV-blind panel, and even a confirmed aberration only opens rezatapopt if the specific variant is Y220C.

- The ranking is scoped to the patient's stated targetable features. It carries the confirmatory workup (rank 1), the curative transplant/cytoreduction backbone that targets the allograft platform and the confirmed CD33/CD123 antigens (ranks 2-5, 7-9), and the biomarker-conditional cell therapies and rezatapopt (ranks 6, 10, 11). Standard salvage chemotherapy that does not act on one of these handles is out of scope and is a separate conversation with the treating team.
- What each negative result forecloses: if HLA-A*02:01 is negative, HA-1/HA-2 TCR-T (rank 6) and the A*02:01 WT1 TCR-T (rank 10) are both foreclosed, and the only remaining HLA-restricted lever is the A*24:02 WT1-siTCR, which has no reachable US site. If A*02:01 is positive but the HA-1/HA-2 pair does not mismatch, rank 6 is foreclosed and WT1 (rank 10) becomes the surviving immunotherapy option. If TP53 is not Y220C, or a true reversion to wild-type is confirmed, rezatapopt (rank 11) is foreclosed. The curative transplant arc (ranks 2-5, 7) stays within scope regardless of how the immunotherapy and TP53 gates resolve, because it targets the allograft platform and the confirmed CD33/CD123 antigens rather than the pending markers.
- Workup logistics: retrieving the 2020 HLA typing is same-day; repeat high-resolution typing is 3-7 days. HA-1/HA-2 genotyping runs 1-2 weeks and usually goes through the trial sponsor's central lab rather than a catalog test, and it needs a paired sibling-donor sample. STR chimerism on FACS-sorted CD34+ blasts (1-2 weeks) confirms recipient-origin relapse, which a bulk-marrow result cannot do; cell sorting is the load-bearing step because a donor-derived leukemia would redirect the whole plan.

## Rank 1. Confirmatory diagnostic gate (HLA typing + HA-1/HA-2 genotyping + relapse TP53/Y220C NGS + sorted-CD34 STR chimerism)

*What it resolves: whether the HLA-restricted immunotherapy the case was built around is reachable at all, whether the TP53 handle is Y220C-actionable, and whether the relapse is recipient-derived and therefore targetable by minor-antigen TCR-T.*

### Evidence base

This is not a therapeutic; it is the set of tests every downstream option depends on. The HA-directed TCR-T programs ([NCT05473910](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05473910), and the Bleakley precedent [PMID 38683966](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38683966)) require HLA-A*02:01 plus a recipient HA-1(H)/HA-2-positive, donor-negative mismatch; the myeloid rezatapopt trial ([NCT06616636](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06616636)) requires a confirmed TP53 Y220C. The relapse NGS row that anchors the TP53 gate ([PMID 39811143](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39811143)) is a variant-level, low-VAF, copy-number-capable panel, which is exactly what the 2026 assay lacked. Read the NGS alongside p53 IHC, 17p FISH, and the retrieved 2019 variant identity; do not adjudicate the discrepancy from any one of them alone.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Diagnostic certainty rather than a response probability. Each result independently opens or closes a door: HLA-A*02:01 for the whole restricted axis, the HA-1/HA-2 mismatch for TCR-T, the Y220C call for rezatapopt, and sorted-CD34 STR chimerism for recipient-origin relapse. The tests are cheap and fast relative to what they decide, which is why all five personas placed their entire ranking behind them.

### Toxicity profile

- None beyond a records request plus assays on blood, marrow, and a paired sibling-donor sample. No procedure-level risk beyond a marrow aspirate the patient is already having for disease assessment.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

Board endorsement was unanimous. There is nothing to dissent from: a non-toxic test that gates everything is the one thing every persona agreed on without qualification.

### Practical considerations

Retrieve the 2020 allele-level HLA typing from the transplant / donor-registry chart first, since that is same-day; repeat typing is 3-7 days if the record cannot be found. HA-1/HA-2 genotyping is not a routine catalog test and is most reliably run through the ALLOHA sponsor central lab or an academic immunogenetics lab, and it needs the sibling donor sampled too. Confirm recipient-origin relapse by STR chimerism on sorted CD34+ blasts (the sex-matched sibling donor rules out XY-FISH). While typing is pending, the transplant team can hold parallel screening conversations with the enrolling programs.

## Rank 2. Second allogeneic HCT (HCT2)

*The curative backbone, deliverable only after the marrow is cytoreduced.*

### Evidence base

The Christopeit EBMT registry of 179 second transplants ([PMID 23918951](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23918951)) puts 2-year OS at ~37% in the matched-related-donor subgroup that fits her, against ~25% pooled. Remission duration after the first transplant and disease stage at the second were the dominant survival drivers, and her ~6-year first remission sits on the favorable side. The FLAMSA-RIC ([PMID 16110027](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16110027)) and DLI ([PMID 17909197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17909197)) series fill in how the platform is delivered and consolidated. All three are retrospective or single-arm (ROBINS-I Serious), and the 37% is a fitness-selected subgroup number, not a whole-cohort estimate.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Moderate for her specific profile. The long first remission pushes the odds up; 85% marrow blasts and complex TP53-aberrant cytogenetics pull hard the other way, and the same registry names active high-burden disease at transplant as the single biggest adverse driver. This is why HCT2 earns rank 2 only on the condition that cytoreduction brings the marrow close to a transplantable state first.

### Toxicity profile

- Substantial non-relapse mortality and acute/chronic GVHD in a second allograft, not broken out in the abstract-level registry.
- Infection and conditioning organ toxicity, which cannot be safely bounded without the missing cardiac and organ-function workup.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

No persona dissented; the critic and concensusite qualified the number (subgroup, retrospective, 2A-by-absence-of-anything-better) but agreed on the rank. The mechanism-level worry is real though: this disease already slipped graft-versus-leukemia once, and a TP53-aberrant complex karyotype is exactly the biology that escapes a second graft too. A durable HCT2 is not assured even with a good first remission.

### Practical considerations

NCCN AML v4.2026 keeps second transplant a category 2A option for fit patients, and ELN 2022 places it at the center of curative-intent management once the marrow is back in remission. Settle donor strategy (same sibling versus an alternative) using the HA-1/HA-2 genotyping already underway, and get a cardiac / organ-function assessment on record before choosing conditioning intensity. The extramedullary thigh sarcoma may need directed radiotherapy separate from the transplant plan.

## Rank 3. Iomab-B (131I-apamistamab) conditioning into HCT2

*The RCT-grade way to carry an active, high-blast marrow into the transplant.*

### Evidence base

SIERRA ([PMID 39298738](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39298738), [NCT02665065](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02665065)) is the only randomized phase 3 in the whole dossier. CD45-targeted radioimmunotherapy conditioning produced durable CR (>=180 days) of 17.1% versus 0% (p<0.0001) and EFS HR 0.23. The number the board weighted most was grade >=3 treatment-related AE parity, 59.7% versus 59.2% on conventional care, meaning the targeted conditioning did not stack toxicity on top of what these patients already face. Two honest limits: ITT OS was flat (HR 0.99, p=0.96), which the authors attribute to 52% crossover rather than a true failure, and SIERRA enrolled first-transplant patients, so the durable-CR endpoint is a surrogate read against a population that was not heading to a second transplant.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Moderate, and the best-evidenced route to the one thing that makes every downstream cellular play possible: getting an 85%-blast marrow into an allograft. The efficacy caveat is that dCR is a durable-remission surrogate and the survival signal did not separate. It competes with FLAMSA-RIC (rank 7) for the same conditioning job.

### Toxicity profile

- Grade >=3 treatment-related AEs ~60%: cytopenias, febrile neutropenia, infection, mucositis, as expected for myeloablative conditioning.
- Marrow-directed conditioning may not reach the extramedullary thigh sarcoma, which needs separate directed-RT planning.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

Every persona endorsed the evidence, including concensusite, who nonetheless ranked it last on their own slate purely because NCCN and ELN have not adopted CD45 RIT yet. The risktaker and advocate pushed back on that directly: demoting the single highest-tier option on guideline-adoption status optimizes for the wrong variable in a patient who explicitly accepts trials. The critic conceded it is the one tier-1b anchor in the file, with a surrogate/crossover caveat. Conservative endorsed the tolerability parity but held the go/no-go contingent on a fitness assessment.

### Practical considerations

Investigational; SIERRA is closed and the go-forward study ([NCT07157514](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07157514)) is not yet recruiting. The load-bearing eligibility question is prior transplant: SIERRA enrolled first-transplant patients, so confirm the new protocol accepts a prior allo-HCT before banking on it. Have the transplant team weigh Iomab-B against FLAMSA-RIC, since both target the same high-burden-disease problem.

## Rank 4. Gemtuzumab ozogamicin (CD33 ADC)

*The one agent a treating team can simply write; a modest on-label CD33 debulk.*

### Evidence base

The Sievers pooled phase 2 ([PMID 11432892](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11432892), n=142) reported ~30% overall remission (CR+CRp) in CD33-positive AML in first relapse, with grade >=3 hyperbilirubinemia 23% and transaminase elevation 17%. It is on-label and on-mechanism for her confirmed CD33-positive blasts, but the registration population was less pretreated than she is: no antecedent MDS, no prior FLAG-IDA/venetoclax failure, no extramedullary disease. It is a CD33-directed bridge toward HCT2, not definitive therapy.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Low-to-moderate, and probably toward the low end here given how far past first relapse she is. Benefit tracks CD33 antigen density, and only qualitative positivity is on file, so the quantitative flow readout should anchor the go/no-go rather than the density being assumed.

### Toxicity profile

- Grade >=3 hyperbilirubinemia 23%, transaminitis 17%, infection 28%.
- Veno-occlusive disease is the characteristic calicheamicin class risk and stacks peri-transplant, which argues for an adequate interval and fractionated dosing before HCT2.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

No dissent stood; concensusite actively defended it against the critic's evidence-quality downgrade, noting that a category 2A rating is the panel's own calibration of exactly that single-arm data plus twenty-plus years of VOD-monitoring experience. The mechanism-level catch is timing rather than direction: the hepatotoxicity that makes it a bridge could compromise the transplant it is bridging to if the interval is too short.

### Practical considerations

FDA-approved on-label for CD33-positive relapsed AML; NCCN AML v4.2026 carries single-agent gemtuzumab at category 2A. Available by routine prescription, no trial or expanded-access mechanism needed. Request quantitative CD33 antigen density first, and factor VOD timing into the HCT2 plan.

## Rank 5. Donor lymphocyte infusion (sibling DLI)

*A readily available transplant adjunct whose honest expectation is modest in florid disease.*

### Evidence base

The Schmid EBMT registry ([PMID 17909197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17909197), n=399) found 21% versus 9% 2-year OS with versus without DLI (adjusted P=0.04). That is the one comparative signal in the transplant set, and it is confounding-by-indication wearing a p-value: benefit concentrated in the remission or favorable-karyotype group (~56%), while the active-disease or aplasia stratum came in around 15%. Her late relapse (~6 years) predicts DLI benefit, but 85% blasts and adverse cytogenetics put her in the worse stratum.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Low for the stratum she falls in. The registry reads as an argument for cytoreduction before any DLI, not for DLI into a florid marrow, and every persona who touched it read it that way.

### Toxicity profile

- Acute and chronic GVHD is the characteristic toxicity and would complicate a subsequent HCT2.
- Marrow aplasia when given into active high-burden disease.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

No dissent stood; the critic and concensusite ranked it as an adjunct with the active-disease caveat baked in. The mechanism-level risk is that DLI into 85% blasts drives GVHD without meaningful graft-versus-leukemia, which is why it belongs after cytoreduction and alongside the transplant rather than as standalone salvage.

### Practical considerations

ASTCT/EBMT-recognized for AML relapse after allo-HCT. Confirm the sibling donor is available and willing to donate lymphocytes again, and pair DLI with the transplant plan (including the FLAMSA-RIC prophylactic-DLI schedule) rather than using it alone. Watch for GVHD if combined with HLA-restricted TCR-T consolidation.

## Rank 6. HA-1 / HA-2-directed donor TCR-T after HCT2

*Conditional on hla_a0201_ha:positive. Foreclosed if either the HLA-A*02:01 type or the HA-1/HA-2 mismatch is negative.*

### Evidence base

This is the handle the case was submitted for and the highest-ceiling cure swing. Bleakley's phase 1 ([PMID 38683966](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38683966), n=9) got 4 of 9 recurrent post-HCT patients into or holding CR, one remission past two years, no dose-limiting toxicity. The mechanism aims donor T cells at recipient HA-1/HA-2, minor antigens presented mainly on blood-lineage cells, so graft-versus-leukemia reaches the leukemia while largely sparing the tissues that drive GVHD. TSC-100/TSC-101 ([NCT05473910](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05473910)) and BSB-1001 ([NCT06704152](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06704152)) run the same mechanism on the back of a fresh matched transplant. This is feasibility-grade evidence: n=9, single-arm, and the per-term grade-level AE table could not be retrieved from the Blood 2024 full text, so "no DLTs" is the only safety datum on file.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Signal-level in the positive branch. A 4/9 uncontrolled CR fraction with durable responders is a live cure signal, not proof; the trial exists precisely to test whether that ceiling is real. And it only exists for this patient if the typing breaks the right way. A negative HLA-A*02:01 or a non-mismatched HA-1/HA-2 pair forecloses it entirely.

### Toxicity profile

- No dose-limiting toxicities across the n=9 cohort; the characteristic setting risks are lymphodepletion cytopenias and GVHD.
- The per-grade AE table is not retrievable from the full text, which is itself the reason two personas held it back in a fitness-unknown patient.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

This is the preference-conflict row. The advocate ranked it first as the axis the run was built around; the risktaker and concensusite endorse it (concensusite through the guideline's enroll-in-a-trial language). Two personas dissent, and their objections are not biomarker-contingent, so they persist in the positive branch: the critic dissents on evidence maturity (n=9 feasibility, not efficacy, above options with a phase 3 or on-label read), and the conservative holds back on the same evidence-maturity grounds plus the undocumented fitness. Because advocate flagged it preference-aligned while two personas dissented, status is considered_with_caveats and the tension is left visible rather than smoothed over. The load-bearing uncertainty is not the HLA type (a records pull) but the HA-1/HA-2 mismatch, a biological coin-flip that genotyping the pair simply closes.

### Practical considerations

Investigational, no consensus category of its own; NCCN AML v4.2026 and ELN 2022 both make trial enrollment the preferred R/R route, which makes this a guideline-consistent enrollment target. Three US programs are recruiting (TScan ALLOHA at 15 sites, Fred Hutch [NCT03326921], BlueSphere BSB-1001 at 6 sites), all delivered on the back of a fresh allo-HCT. It is post-HCT2 consolidation by design and does nothing for the current marrow, so it sequences after the transplant, not instead of it.

## Rank 7. FLAMSA-RIC sequential conditioning + reduced-intensity allo-HCT + prophylactic DLI

*The chemo-based conditioning alternative to Iomab-B for the same transplant.*

Thirty-one of 75 used HLA-identical sibling donors, matching her situation, and the DLI series ([PMID 17909197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17909197)) covers the prophylactic-DLI component from day +120.

Moderate, and the same order of magnitude as the transplant registry, in a design built for exactly the hard subgroup she is in. It is a delivery route to HCT2 rather than an independent modality, so its likelihood is really the transplant's likelihood by a chemotherapy-conditioning path.

- An older regimen whose conditioning intensity depends on the undocumented cardiac / organ-function fitness.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

No persona dissented. ### Evidence base

## Rank 9. Pivekimab sunirine (CD123 ADC)

*A CD123 debulk that avoids the failed backbone, but its VOD signal works against the transplant it bridges to.*

### Evidence base

*Conditional on hla_a0201:positive. Foreclosed if neither A*02:01 nor A*24:02 is present.*

### Evidence base

WT1 keeps the HLA-restricted axis alive if the HA-1/HA-2 pair does not mismatch but the patient is A*02:01-positive. The Chapuis WT1-TCRc4 work ([PMID 31235963](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31235963), n=12) held high-risk AML patients relapse-free post-transplant, reporting 100% RFS at median 44 months versus 54% in a concurrent cohort. The live product is FH-WT1-E50 plus azacitidine ([NCT07645469](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07645469)), written for MRD-positive disease. If A*02:01 is negative, A*24:02 opens the WT1-siTCR lever ([PMID 28860210](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28860210)), which is why both alleles should be typed in parallel.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Setting-mismatched in the positive branch. The 100% RFS figure comes from a prophylaxis product given against relapse, not salvage of a florid 85%-blast marrow, and the 54% comparator is a concurrent non-randomized cohort, so the survival contrast is confounded. Best positioned as post-cytoreduction or post-HCT2 consolidation, and foreclosed entirely if neither allele is present.

### Toxicity profile

- Engineered into EBV-specific donor cells to minimize GVHD; no per-term AE breakdown in the abstract.
- On-target hematopoietic risk is theoretical, since WT1 is a shared self-antigen, though the preclinical work showed normal-progenitor sparing.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

Two dissents, both on the evidence and the disease setting rather than the biomarker, so they persist in the positive branch. The critic dissents because prophylaxis/MRD data cannot rank a lever for florid relapse on a confounded comparator; the risktaker separately noted the WT1 fallback bench is thinner than the ranking implies, being relapse-prevention rather than active-disease evidence. Advocate carried it explicitly so an A*02:01-positive but HA-non-mismatched result does not foreclose the immunotherapy axis the patient prioritized.

### Practical considerations

Investigational, no consensus category; trial enrollment is the guideline-preferred R/R route. The live FH-WT1-E50 product is not yet recruiting and is written for MRD-positive disease, so treat it as a consolidation concept after disease control. The A*24:02 siTCR fallback (TBI-1301) has no reachable US site, so it is more concept than route.

## Rank 11. Rezatapopt (Y220C-selective p53 reactivator) + azacitidine

*Conditional on tp53_y220c:positive. Foreclosed if TP53 is not Y220C or a true reversion is confirmed.*

### Evidence base

Rezatapopt binds the surface pocket created by the Y220C substitution and refolds mutant p53 toward a wild-type shape. In Y220C AML models ([PMID 40608889](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40608889)) it reactivates the p53 program but drives little apoptosis on its own; adding venetoclax or MDM2/XPO1 inhibition restores real killing. The solid-tumor PYNNACLE data ([PMID 41740031](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41740031)) show ORR 20%, and 30% in KRAS-wild-type. Every clinical response so far is in solid tumors; the myeloid trial ([NCT06616636](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06616636), rezatapopt + azacitidine at MD Anderson) has posted nothing.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Cross-tumor only, even in the positive branch, and doubly contingent: confirm a TP53 aberration at all off the 2019-versus-2026 discrepancy, then confirm it is specifically Y220C. A low-positive or unconfirmed biomarker is a weaker predictor of benefit than a tier-matched confirmed one, and the mechanism needs a venetoclax partner she is already refractory to. Treat the likelihood as speculative until both contingencies land.

### Toxicity profile

- Grade >=3 anaemia 16%; predominant low-grade nausea (58%) and vomiting (44%), less frequent when taken with food.
- Oral, continuous dosing; the toxicity burden is genuinely lower than the transplant and cytoreduction options, which is the one thing it has going for it.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

Two dissents that persist through biomarker confirmation because they are about the evidence, not the marker. The critic marks it down for solid-tumor-only data plus the venetoclax dependency in a venetoclax-refractory patient; concensusite marks it off-consensus, since no AML guideline lists a p53 reactivator and the myeloid trial has no results. Only the risktaker ranked it, as a mechanistic longshot. The counter-productive-MoA is that reactivated p53 barely triggers apoptosis alone and needs a partner drug she has already failed.

### Practical considerations

Investigational, absent from NCCN AML v4.2026 and ELN 2022, and off the HLA-restricted axis that is the stated reason for the run, so the advocate deliberately kept it off the preference-carrying slate. Worth resolving in parallel with the HLA workup, not foregrounding. A non-Y220C aberration or a true reversion closes it; eprenetapopt is the low-yield pan-reactivator alternative and is functionally unavailable in myeloid disease.

## Workup considerations

Beyond the four gating tests in rank 1, several high-priority but non-gating confirmatory steps sit adjacent to the profile and should run off the same specimens:

- Quantitative CD33 and CD123 antigen-density flow (antibodies-bound-per-cell), batched on one fresh draw, to convert the qualitative-positive calls into the metric that actually predicts ADC benefit for gemtuzumab and pivekimab.
- p53 protein IHC (clone DO-7) and TP53 (17p13) FISH on the marrow biopsy, plus retrieval of the 2019 diagnostic report, to reconcile the TP53 discrepancy at the protein and copy-number level without down-calling on the CNV-blind panel.
- A full metaphase karyotype at relapse to characterize the adverse MECOM rearrangement and KMT2A amplification for risk stratification and trial eligibility.
- Right-thigh contrast MRI plus whole-body FDG-PET/CT to map the extramedullary myeloid sarcoma and decide whether directed radiotherapy is warranted before or bridging to HCT2.

## Classes examined but not ranked

- **Tagraxofusp (CD123 immunotoxin):** targets the confirmed CD123 handle, but no persona ranked it; pivekimab was preferred as the CD123 lever, and tagraxofusp adds capillary-leak risk that argues for fitness data first. Carried as the CD123 alternative in rank 9.
- **CD123xCD3 bispecifics (flotetuzumab, vibecotamab):** on-mechanism for CD123 with real salvage signal for flotetuzumab in chemo-refractory AML, but both programs are discontinued or terminated, so neither is an open route.
- **PRAME-directed TCR-T (BPX-701):** the third A*02:01-restricted handle named in the profile, but the only clinical program is terminated with no successor, so there is no way to reach a PRAME-directed TCR-T today.
- **Eprenetapopt (pan-p53 reactivator):** targets the TP53 handle, but it is not Y220C-selective, its phase 3 in TP53-mutant MDS missed its primary endpoint, and myeloid development sits under a clinical hold, so it is the low-yield and functionally unavailable p53 option behind rezatapopt.

## Caveats

- **Evidence-base caveats.** The strongest row (Iomab-B / SIERRA) is a randomized phase 3 but open-label with a durable-CR surrogate endpoint and a null ITT OS confounded by 52% crossover. Everything else is retrospective registry (HCT2, DLI), single-arm phase 1/2 (HA-1 TCR-T n=9, WT1 n=12, pivekimab), cross-tumor only (rezatapopt), or xenograft-only (dual CAR-T). For the HA-1 TCR-T precedent, the per-grade AE table could not be retrieved from the full text, so its safety profile rests on "no DLTs" across nine patients.
- **Biomarker dependencies.** Ranks 6 and 10 assume HLA-A*02:01 confirmation; rank 6 additionally assumes a recipient HA-1(H)/HA-2-positive, donor-negative mismatch; rank 11 assumes TP53 confirmed aberrant and specifically Y220C. Without those, the respective ranks are foreclosed. The transplant backbone (ranks 2-5, 7) does not depend on the pending markers and stays in scope regardless.
- **What would change the ranking.** A retrievable HLA-A*02:01-positive type plus a confirmed HA-1/HA-2 mismatch would move rank 6 from a conditional longshot toward the top of the therapeutic list, since it is the stated-reason option with the highest ceiling. Documented organ-function and cardiac fitness would firm up the conditioning and transplant ranks and could raise the efficacy weight the file asks to revisit. A confirmed TP53 Y220C would lift rank 11 out of double contingency; a confirmed reversion to wild-type would foreclose it. A donor-derived leukemia on sorted-CD34 STR chimerism would redirect the entire plan away from minor-antigen TCR-T.
- **Re-scoping caveat.** If fitness comes back poor, or goals of care shift away from curative intent, the whole arc changes: the transplant backbone and its conditioning would move toward palliative CD33/CD123 cytoreduction, and the efficacy-leaning 0.75 weight would need to be re-set.
