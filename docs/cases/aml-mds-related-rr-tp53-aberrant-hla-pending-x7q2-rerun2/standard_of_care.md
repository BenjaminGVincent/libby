_15 standard options assessed: 7 to consider now, 0 behind an open gate, 2 already received._

Fifteen standard options were assessed for this relapsed/refractory AML-MR, and seven can be weighed now. The headline is uncomfortable and worth stating plainly: for TP53-mutated poor-risk AML, NCCN AML v3.2026 lists clinical trial and nothing else, and no approved regimen performs well in this disease state. That makes trial enrollment the guideline-preferred choice, not a consolation. The one on-label drug is gemtuzumab ozogamicin, approved for relapsed/refractory CD33-positive AML, and her blasts are CD33-positive; its own best randomised evidence shows no survival benefit in her adverse karyotype, so it is standard by approval with a modest expectation. Hydroxyurea for a peripheral blast count that moved 58% to 82% in a fortnight, concurrent palliative-care involvement while fitness and goals are still undocumented, high-dose cytarabine salvage as a cytoreductive bridge, an NCCN-listed hypomethylating agent, and radiotherapy to the thigh myeloid sarcoma, which guidelines cover only thinly, complete the near-term list.

Two standard options are already spent: the first sibling allograft, which held remission for about six years, and the FLAG-IDA plus venetoclax reinduction the disease progressed through. That venetoclax exposure also closes several venetoclax-dependent routes elsewhere in the case.

The curative destination, a second allograft with sibling DLI as the other cellular route, carries no NCCN category; the ELN 2022 panel and the EBMT 2024 DLI recommendations are the graded texts, and both sit behind cytoreduction and an organ-function workup that has not been done. Revumenib, ziftomenib, and CPX-351 are assessed and set aside on genotype or line, and the FLT3- and IDH-wild-type results close those inhibitor labels.

This page is additive. It records the standard options and does not narrow, reorder, or filter the experimental options ranked elsewhere on this case page.

## Assessed and set aside

??? note "3 option(s) screened against this patient and set aside"
    Screened against this patient and found not to apply. Listed for the audit trail.

    - **Revumenib (menin inhibitor)**: Revumenib is approved for relapsed/refractory acute leukemia with a KMT2A translocation and for NPM1-mutant disease, and it is named here because her karyotype invites the misread: KMT2A amplification is a copy-number gain of four copies, not the rearrangement the label and its companion diagnostic require, and the disease is NPM1 wild-type. Menin inhibition depends on the dependency a KMT2A fusion or an NPM1 mutation creates, which amplification does not. Assessed and set aside on genotype.
    - **Ziftomenib (menin inhibitor)**: Ziftomenib is the second approved menin inhibitor, but its label is confined to relapsed/refractory AML with a susceptible NPM1 mutation, and this disease is NPM1 wild-type. As with revumenib, the KMT2A amplification here is a copy-number gain rather than the fusion that would create menin-inhibitor sensitivity. The drug is approved but the target is absent, so it is assessed and set aside.
    - **CPX-351 (liposomal daunorubicin/cytarabine)**: CPX-351 is the standard intensive frontline choice for the AML-MR histology she carries, which is why a reader would look for it here. The approval and the NCCN listing are confined to newly diagnosed disease, and she is relapsed and refractory after a transplant, so the histology match does not open the drug at this line. It is assessed and set aside on line, not on target.

## How these sit alongside the targeted options

_The ranked recommendations stay scoped to this case's targetable features, and nothing on this page changes that ranking. What this section adds is the sequencing: where a standard option and a targeted option compete for the same line, and where taking one would close the door on the other._

**Competes for the same line of therapy**

- **Gemtuzumab ozogamicin, single agent (CD33 antibody-drug conjugate)**: Same CD33 payload as the rank-2 CLAG-GO regimen, which stays in the experimental table because the combination is trial-only with no published efficacy in refractory AML. Single-agent GO is the on-label route if the NCT04050280 slot or intensive fitness fails; giving it alone first spends the hepatic and CD33 exposure the rank-2 regimen plans to use. (interacts with: clag-go)
- **Donor lymphocyte infusion (DLI) from the HLA-identical sibling, after cytoreduction**: The same option holds rank 5 in the experimental table as a user-stated feature; the tables are additive, so it appears in both. It sits at the same post-cytoreduction fork as HCT2, shares the sibling donor and collection window the rank-4 HA-1 TCR-T needs, and severe GVHD after it would foreclose both. (interacts with: donor-lymphocyte-infusion, second-allo-hct, ha1-tcr-t)
- **Hypomethylating agent, lower intensity (azacitidine or decitabine)**: An HMA course spends the same weeks the rank-2 cytoreduction would use, so choosing it is a tempo decision against an 85% blast marrow. Adding venetoclax to it would re-expose a drug she has already progressed through, which is the same objection the board logged against venetoclax-containing trial arms. (interacts with: clag-go)
- **Intensive salvage chemotherapy (high-dose cytarabine-based, e.g. MEC or CLAG-M)**: Rank-2 CLAG-GO is the trial-delivered member of this same regimen family; an off-protocol CLAG-M or MEC uses the same window, the same marrow reserve, and the same hepatic budget. The choice between them is a delivery-route question, not a different strategy. (interacts with: clag-go)

**May foreclose a targeted option**

- **FLAG-IDA + venetoclax reinduction, given 2026**: The venetoclax exposure already on record is what narrows several experimental-table routes: the tagraxofusp trial cohort needs a venetoclax washout, the rezatapopt mechanism wanted venetoclax as its killing partner, and the BMS-986497 triplet arm would re-expose it. The foreclosure has already happened; this row records it. (interacts with: tagraxofusp, rezatapopt, bms-986497-orm-6151)

**Comes before the targeted options**

- **Second allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplant (HCT2), cytoreduce first**: The same option holds rank 3 in the experimental table as a user-stated feature; the tables are additive, so it appears in both. It is the platform on which the rank-4 HA-1 TCR-T consolidation would be delivered, it is sequenced after the rank-2 cytoreduction, and it is the alternative to the rank-5 DLI at the same fork. (interacts with: second-allo-hct, ha1-tcr-t, clag-go, donor-lymphocyte-infusion)
- **Hydroxyurea for blast-count control (with leukapheresis if needed)**: Several experimental-table protocols carry count ceilings that hydroxyurea is the usual way to reach: CBX-250 wants white cells under 25,000 and the 211At-BC8-B10 protocol wants circulating blasts under 10,000/mm3. It also buys days ahead of the rank-2 cytoreduction without spending any drug class the plan needs later. (interacts with: clag-go, cg1-a2-tcr-mimetic, at211-bc8-b10)

**Can run alongside the targeted options**

- **Clinical trial enrollment**: Every live option in the experimental ranking reaches her through a trial or a protocolised route, from the rank-1 gating workup through CLAG-GO, the second allograft, HA-1 TCR-T, and DLI. This row is the delivery mechanism for those options, not a competitor to them. (interacts with: pending-diagnostics-workup, clag-go, second-allo-hct, ha1-tcr-t, donor-lymphocyte-infusion)
- **Concurrent specialty palliative care and goals-of-care assessment**: The fitness and goals-of-care assessment this brings is the same open item the rank-1 workup names, and her preferences file asks that the 0.75 efficacy weighting be revisited once fitness is known. It informs the weighting of every option in both tables without changing any option's eligibility. (interacts with: pending-diagnostics-workup)
- **Radiotherapy to the right-thigh extramedullary myeloid sarcoma**: Marrow-directed conditioning does not treat the thigh lesion, a gap the board named against the rank-3 transplant plan, and whether a CD33-directed regimen reaches it is an open question in the rank-2 row (paired series detect CD33 in about 79% of lesions). Radiotherapy to a limb site can run in parallel without spending any systemic option. (interacts with: second-allo-hct, clag-go)

**Independent of the targeted options**

- **Revumenib (menin inhibitor)**: Named to close the loop on the KMT2A-amplification versus KMT2A-rearrangement distinction, a common misread of this karyotype. It does not interact with the ranked options.
- **Ziftomenib (menin inhibitor)**: Listed alongside revumenib so the report is explicit that the approved menin-inhibitor class does not apply to an NPM1-wild-type, KMT2A-amplified genotype. It does not interact with the ranked options.
- **Best supportive care and hospice referral**: The path taken if the curative transplant and cellular pathway closes; it does not compete with any single ranked option.
