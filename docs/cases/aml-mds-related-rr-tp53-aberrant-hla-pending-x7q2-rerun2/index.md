## Research question

In relapsed/refractory MDS-related AML sitting at roughly 85% marrow blasts, six years after a matched-sibling allo-HCT and refractory to FLAG-IDA plus venetoclax, what interventions could target the patient's HLA-restricted immunotherapy handles (HA-1/HA-2 TCR-T, WT1, PRAME), her CD33- and CD123-positive blasts, a Y220C-contingent TP53 aberration, and a second-transplant platform, gated on retrieving the HLA-A*02:01 typing that the whole immunotherapy axis hangs on?

## Patient profile (scrubbed)

- Woman, age band 50-59, ECOG 1.
- AML, myelodysplasia-related (AML-MR), relapsed/refractory; transformed from prior MDS (RCMD, IPSS-R high/very-high, diagnosed ~2019). Extramedullary right-thigh myeloid sarcoma on MRI (May 2026), an adverse feature.
- Marrow ~85% blasts (June 2026); peripheral blasts 58% to 82% over late June 2026. Refractory to FLAG-IDA + venetoclax reinduction.
- Late relapse ~6 years after matched-sibling PBSC allo-HCT (~2020, CR sustained 72 months); pattern consistent with graft-versus-leukaemia escape rather than graft failure.
- **HLA-A\*02:01 — unknown / pending retrieval.** High-resolution typing was performed ~2020 but the allele-level result sits as a placeholder in the EHR. **HLA-A\*24:02 — unknown / pending**, to be pulled from the same report. **HA-1 / HA-2 minor antigens — unknown / pending for BOTH patient and sibling donor**; HLA-identical siblings can still differ at these loci.
- **TP53 — aberrant, pending confirmation, not resolved in either direction.** 2019 diagnostic NGS reported a pathogenic TP53 mutation; the 2026 relapse panel reported negative on a limited assay (LOD ~2% VAF, no copy-number calling, not MRD-grade). **TP53 Y220C — unknown**; rezatapopt is contingent on it.
- CD33 — positive by flow (confirmed, qualitative). CD123 — positive by flow (confirmed, qualitative). Quantitative antigen density requested for both.
- FLT3, IDH1, IDH2 — negative (confirmed, 2019). JAK3 and SMC3 variants of uncertain significance (2019), not actionable.
- Cytogenetics — complex, adverse. 2019: del(5q), del(7q), del(20q12), der(17)t(13;17), monosomy 13. 2026 relapse: gain of 5' MECOM, del(5q), del(7q), KMT2A amplification (4 copies), del(8cen), del(16q22). Shared del(5q)/del(7q) plus the TP53 founder across timepoints points to evolution of the original clone rather than a donor-derived or secondary leukaemia.
- Organ function: no numeric labs supplied. No creatinine clearance, no LFTs, no ejection fraction. Fitness assessment is an explicit open item.

## Target validation paths

Two results decide whether the question this case was submitted to answer has an answer at all. Allele-level HLA-A*02:01 typing gates the Fred Hutch HA-1 TCR-T on NCT03326921, TSC-100/TSC-101 on NCT05473910 and BSB-1001 on NCT06704152, and HA-1/HA-2 genotyping of the patient together with her HLA-identical sibling then decides whether the mismatch those products need actually runs the right way. If both alleles, A*02:01 and the A*24:02 fallback, type negative, this report has no within-scope HLA-restricted workup left to offer, and the next conversation about standard salvage care belongs to the treating team.

### HLA-restricted immunotherapy

High-resolution class I typing of HLA-A, -B and -C is essential and it is mostly a records problem. The typing was run for the 2020 sibling transplant and the allele-level call is sitting in a transplant or donor-registry file rather than in the chart on hand, so ask for that report before drawing anything; a fresh NGS type takes 3 to 7 days with STAT available if it cannot be produced. HA-1/HA-2 genotyping (HMHA1 rs1801284 and MYO1G) is the second essential test and it is the slow one. NCT03326921 wants a recipient carrying rs1801284 A/G or A/A with a G/G donor, NCT06704152 wants HA-1 H/H or H/R with an HA-1-negative donor, and HLA-identical siblings routinely differ at these minor loci, so a patient-only result settles nothing. It runs 1 to 3 weeks as a specialised send-out, needs a fresh donor sample and donor consent, and routing it through the Fred Hutch or ALLOHA screening pathway is often faster than a catalogue order.

One resistance question belongs here too, and a late relapse six years after a matched-sibling graft is exactly the history that raises it. HLA loss of heterozygosity on sorted blasts, 6p LOH by NGS or SNP array paired with a flow read of surface class I, asks whether the relapsed clone still presents the restricting allele. If that clone has deleted the haplotype carrying HLA-A*02:01, every A*02:01-restricted product is aimed at something the leukaemia no longer displays, and the germline typing will still read positive. Reflex it once the typing returns. Two further reads confirm targets without gating anything: standardised WT1 RQ-PCR reported as copies per 10^4 ABL, which also sets an MRD baseline that compares across centres, and a PRAME expression read on blasts, which is worth ordering only after A*02:01 is known to be present.

### TP53 Y220C

The record holds a pathogenic TP53 mutation from 2019 and a negative relapse panel from 2026, and the workup is built to keep both on the table rather than pick the more recent one. Retrieving the 2019 variant table with its HGVS notation and VAF is essential and costs nothing but a phone call, because NCT06616636 screens on a Y220C call at VAF above 2 percent and the specific amino-acid change is what opens or closes that trial. The second essential step is a copy-number-capable myeloid panel at relapse reporting TP53 at the variant level: the 2026 assay ran at a 2 percent limit of detection with no copy-number calling, so a subclonal or copy-loss event would have been reported exactly as it was. Two orthogonal reads corroborate the call off archival material at no new procedure. 17p/TP53 FISH looks for the copy loss a sequence-only panel cannot see, which matters in a leukaemia whose 2019 karyotype carried der(17)t(13;17). p53 IHC scored by pattern, overexpression versus complete null versus wild-type, adds a protein-level read, though it never names the variant.

### CD123

Quantitative CD123 density by flow, reported as antibodies bound per cell or MESF against calibrated beads, is essential and it is a request rather than a default readout. Density across AML spans roughly 200 to 10,000 molecules per cell, so the qualitative positive call on file cannot separate a strong handle from a marginal one, and neither tagraxofusp nor pivekimab sunirine carries an AML label, which puts the weight of the argument on the number. Honest caveat: no validated cutoff predicts benefit from either agent, so the result informs judgement rather than clearing a published threshold.

The right-thigh myeloid sarcoma deserves its own tissue. In a meta-analysis of 85 studies, 56 percent of the 112 patients with paired myeloid sarcoma and marrow sequencing were discordant in mutational profile, and CD33 was detectable in 79 percent of myeloid sarcoma tissue rather than all of it. A core biopsy sent for CD33 and CD123 flow plus targeted myeloid NGS tests whether the marrow speaks for the lesion. The comparison is what carries the information, so sequence it against the marrow work rather than instead of it.

### CD33

Quantitative CD33 density belongs on the same specimen and the same requisition as the CD123 request. The central flow reanalysis of ALFA-0701 tied gemtuzumab benefit to the level of blast CD33 expression rather than to positivity, so a low-density case that still reads positive is a weak handle for a CD33 antibody-drug conjugate. Reported against the laboratory's own AML range, not a published cutoff. The rs12459419 splicing-SNP genotype sits at medium priority and reads as context: the T allele shifts splicing toward an isoform missing the IgV domain gemtuzumab binds, the randomised evidence for that is paediatric, and adult replication is inconsistent enough that a T allele should temper a conversation rather than end one.

### Second-transplant platform

Three reads support the transplant plan and none of them gates enrolment. STR chimerism on flow-sorted CD34+ blasts confirms the relapse is recipient-origin rather than donor-derived, and the sorting is not optional detail: a sex-matched sibling donor rules out XY FISH, and unsorted chimerism gets diluted by normal donor haematopoiesis. A full metaphase karyotype with MECOM and KMT2A FISH puts the 2026 findings into a current cytogenetic picture that trial screens read directly. The germline hereditary myeloid malignancy panel is the one worth starting early, because it bears on re-using the same sibling. A predisposition variant shared with the donor risks donor-derived disease and would redirect donor choice, a germline TP53 finding would change conditioning intensity, and the specimen requirement is the catch: with blasts at 58 to 82 percent, blood and saliva cannot give a clean germline call, so the panel wants cultured skin fibroblasts from a punch biopsy and culture adds 4 to 6 weeks.

### Biomarkers measured, but not to decision resolution

Three markers have something on file that cannot carry a decision as it stands, and each is a different kind of short.

- HLA class I genotype. Typing was performed for the 2020 transplant, so the result exists somewhere; what is missing is the allele-level call in a form anyone can act on. The fix is retrieval first, high-resolution NGS typing of both parties second, and the same pass covers the sibling because HA-1/HA-2 eligibility depends on the donor as much as the patient.
- CD123. Flow called the blasts positive and stopped there. The immunotoxin and ADC literature ties benefit to antigen density, not to a positive-or-negative call, so quantitative ABC or MESF on a current marrow or blood specimen, with the percentage of blasts positive stated alongside it, is what closes the gap.
- PRAME. This one is not under-resolved, it is unmeasured, and two gaps stack. The restricting allele is unknown and expression on these blasts has never been assessed. Order the class I typing first; if A*02:01 is present, reflex PRAME by RT-qPCR on sorted blasts or IHC on the marrow clot section. Reported overexpression in AML swings widely with assay and cutoff, so quantify against the laboratory's own reference rather than assuming the target is there.

## Cross-cutting caveat (read first)

**Two things shape every rank on this page, and neither is a drug.** The first is an allele-level HLA-A*02:01 result that already exists, generated at the ~2020 transplant workup and sitting unretrieved as an EHR placeholder. Every HLA-restricted lever the patient came here for is presented on that allele, and HA-directed TCR-T needs more besides: a recipient HA-1(H)-positive genotype at rs1801284 with an HA-1-negative sibling donor, which nobody has measured in either party. The second is blast burden. At roughly 85% marrow blasts the favourable second-transplant strata do not apply to her, the interferon-gamma DLI protocol caps blasts at 30%, MT-401-OTS caps them at 10%, and the CG1 engager wants white cells under 25,000. Cytoreduction is the gate on nearly everything below, which is why a chemotherapy regimen sits at rank 2 in a case submitted about cellular therapy.

- The ranking is scoped to the patient's stated targetable features, and it is one of two co-equal tables. This Experimental table carries feature-targeting *investigational* options only. Single-agent gemtuzumab ozogamicin is FDA-approved for relapsed/refractory CD33-positive AML, a population that includes her, so it is routed to the Standard-of-care table rather than ranked here; the CLAG-GO regimen at rank 2 stays here because the combination itself is trial-only or off-label and has no published efficacy in refractory AML. Revumenib is approved for KMT2A *translocation* and for NPM1-mutated AML, and she has KMT2A *amplification* with four copies, which is a different lesion, so menin inhibition is target-absent and belongs on the other table if anywhere.

- One item inside that workup carries a lead time nothing else has. HA-1/HA-2 genotyping of the patient *and* the sibling gates a fresh donor collection, and the donor consent conversation has not started. Send it the same day as the ejection fraction. Holding it until a response is documented restarts the manufacturing clock at the moment the window is best.

## Rank 2. CLAG-GO (cladribine, high-dose cytarabine, G-CSF with fractionated gemtuzumab ozogamicin 3 mg/m2 days 1, 4, 7)

*The cytoreduction that makes the rest of this page reachable, on the one strong-fit open trial that treats post-allograft relapse as eligible rather than disqualifying.*

### Evidence base

[NCT04050280](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04050280) is recruiting and has no posted results, so every effect estimate attached to it is imported from elsewhere, and the imports do not travel well. ALFA-0701 gave EFS HR 0.58 (95% CI 0.43-0.78, p=0.0003) in untreated de novo AML aged 50-70, in a trial that specifically excluded secondary and therapy-related disease ([PMID 22482940](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22482940)), which is her diagnosis. MyloFrance-1 gave 26% CR with fractionated gemtuzumab monotherapy in 57 patients in *first relapse* after chemotherapy, not refractory disease after an allograft, so read it as a ceiling ([PMID 17051246](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17051246)). The strongest evidence in the whole dossier is the individual-patient-data meta-analysis of 3,325 randomised patients, and it is null for her: adverse cytogenetics OR 0.99 (95% CI 0.83-1.18, p=0.9), a 2.2% absolute difference at six years ([PMID 25008258](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25008258)). The one published CLAG-GO paper is frontline core-binding-factor disease, a favourable-risk population that says nothing about this one ([PMID 41173347](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41173347)).

### Likelihood of desired effect

Moderate as cytoreduction and honestly uncertain as anything more. The CLAG backbone is what earns the slot; the gemtuzumab component has high-quality randomised evidence pointing away from benefit in her karyotype, and the board did not pretend otherwise. Working against it is cross-resistance: she progressed through FLAG-IDA plus venetoclax, and asking cladribine to succeed where fludarabine failed is the likeliest single reason this does not work. Working for it is that the fractionated schedule produced no grade 3-4 hepatic toxicity and no veno-occlusive disease in 57 patients, seven of whom went to transplant afterwards, so a response here buys the graft instead of costing it.

### Toxicity profile

- Prolonged grade 4 neutropenia and thrombocytopenia; median neutropenia 23 days in the fractionated-gemtuzumab series, and count recovery in a six-year-old graft may be slower still.
- Febrile neutropenia and infection during a protracted aplasia.
- Calicheamicin hepatotoxicity including veno-occlusive disease. Fractionation lowers the risk, it does not zero it, and another conditioning regimen is planned within months. Baseline bilirubin and transaminases before dose one, then weekly.
- Persistent thrombocytopenia ran 16% versus 3% with the control arm in ALFA-0701 (p<0.0001).
- **No creatinine clearance, no LFTs and no ejection fraction on file.** Ranking a cladribine/cytarabine backbone as if organ function were known is exactly what advocate refused to do, and the rank-1 workup is what closes that gap.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

No persona dissented and none vetoed. Advocate came into round 2 at rank 1 on the TCR-T and moved here explicitly, calling the tumour-burden papers arguments about sequencing rather than evidence tier, and attaching one condition: the HLA record pull and HA-1 genotyping run *during* induction. Concensusite qualified on guideline fit, noting that NCCN lists gemtuzumab for CD33-positive R/R AML with no cytogenetic qualifier yet keeps it out of the poor-risk induction options it offers favourable and intermediate risk, and that Hills 2014 is why. The mechanism-level risk is real and separate from the adverse-event list: nucleoside cross-resistance after FLAG-IDA failure, and prolonged aplasia that could end the transplant plan rather than enable it.

### Practical considerations

The trial names post-allograft relapse as eligible at 60 days or more off immunosuppression with GVHD no worse than grade 1, and requires refractoriness to an intensive regimen, which she has. Isolated myeloid sarcoma is excluded but marrow disease is not, so the thigh lesion alongside marrow involvement does not disqualify her. If the Baltimore slot does not open, the regimen can be rebuilt from marketed drugs at her home centre, a fallback almost nothing else on this page has. Entry turns on CD33 on at least 20% of blasts, which is why the density request sits inside rank 1. NCCN AML v3.2026 puts clinical trial first for relapsed/refractory disease and lists both halves of this regimen separately in the same pathway.

### Why this rank

Agreement score 1.0, tied with the transplant platform below it, and it goes first on sequencing rather than on preference. Four of five personas ranked it first or second, and even critic, who ranked the transplant first, wrote that the debulking question is what decides whether any of the registry estimates apply to her at all. The mean board rank is 1.8 here against 2.4 for HCT2.

## Rank 3. Second allogeneic HCT from the HLA-identical sibling, on a cytoreduce-first or sequential-conditioning platform

*The only option in this dossier with a documented cure fraction, and the one whose favourable numbers all require a marrow she does not currently have.*

### Evidence base

Four independent registries say the same thing about relapse after allo-HCT, which is as close to replication as this question gets. Ruutu 2015 covers 2,632 second transplants with 5-year relapse-free survival of 15% ([PMID 26367221](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26367221)). Christopeit 2013 (n=179) puts her stratum, a related-donor first graft, at 2-year OS 37% +/- 6% against 16% +/- 4% after an unrelated first graft ([PMID 23918951](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23918951)). Schmid 2012 (n=263) supplies the prognostic score, where marrow blasts below 27% carry HR 0.53 (95% CI 0.40-0.72, p<0.001) for survival and the worst band sits at 4% +/- 2% at two years ([PMID 22167752](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22167752)). All of it is retrospective, none of it adjusts for the obvious confounder that selection into a second graft is itself a fitness judgement made by the treating centre, and none of the four tabulates non-relapse mortality, so the harm side of every quoted curve is missing.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Moderate if she is cytoreduced, low as she stands. She is on the favourable side of nearly every factor those registries measure: a sibling donor already collected once, a six-year first remission far past the five-month cut that drives the EBMT score, a related-donor first graft. The single exception is the one that decides it, and it is not marginal. At roughly 85% blasts she sits in Schmid's worst prognostic band today, so quoting 37% +/- 6% for her as she is would be an indirectness error rather than a rounding error. About half of those who do reach CR relapse again.

### Toxicity profile

- Non-relapse mortality after a second graft, which no registry row tabulates. The tolerability case rests on survival curves rather than a per-organ risk table.
- Acute and chronic GVHD, prolonged cytopenias, infection.
- Conditioning intensity is unresolved, and there is no cardiac or renal baseline from which to choose it. That decision should not rest on an ECOG score alone.
- The US sequential protocol asks her to repeat a FLAG-Ida backbone she has already progressed through, which is a clinical objection for the investigator rather than an access one.
- Marrow-directed conditioning does not treat the thigh sarcoma, which needs its own local plan.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

No dissent and no veto; all five personas ranked it top-four and critic ranked it first. Concensusite established the guideline gap and it should be read as uncovered rather than contraindicated: NCCN AML v3.2026 tells you to start the donor search at first relapse and ELN 2022 recommends allogeneic HCT once cytoreduction is achieved, but neither body writes a graded recommendation for a *second* allograft after post-transplant relapse, because the pathway assumes a first transplant. Critic's reconciliation is the formulation to carry forward. A missing category is not missing evidence, and evidence quality is not the same axis as clinical value; what backs HCT2 is four registries replicating a prognostic model rather than a treatment effect, with selection unadjusted in every one. The mechanism-level worry conservative raised is separate and unanswered: a second graft from the same sibling does nothing about whatever let this leukaemia escape graft-versus-leukaemia the first time, which is why HLA loss and class I surface expression on sorted blasts belong in the workup.

### Practical considerations

Gate it on a documented remission, or at minimum blasts under 27%, plus a measured ejection fraction and a creatinine clearance. [NCT06928662](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06928662) is the American sequential-conditioning route, cytoreducing into a reduced-intensity graft without waiting for remission, and prior FLAG-Ida triggers a case-by-case review where the patient was sensitive to it. Payer authorisation is likelier to turn on documented cytoreduction than on anything biological, which is another reason rank 2 precedes this. The germline question is open and it bears on donor choice: a predisposition variant shared with the sibling would redirect the plan, and the hereditary panel needs cultured fibroblasts rather than blood while blasts are circulating.

### Why this rank

Tied at 1.0 with the cytoreduction above it and ranked second on sequencing alone. Concensusite put it plainly: read against the guideline text, HCT2 is the consolidation step and not the next thing to do. The gap to the HA-1 TCR-T below (0.8) is the critic's evidence-tier dissent, which does not attach here.

## Rank 4. HA-1 TCR-T, donor-derived CD4+/CD8+ memory T cells (Bleakley/HighPassBio platform)

*Conditional on hla_a0201:positive. Foreclosed if the typing is negative.*

### Evidence base

Krakow 2024 is the entire clinical evidence base and it is nine patients ([PMID 38683966](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38683966), [NCT03326921](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03326921)). Four of nine achieved or maintained CR after lymphodepletion and infusion, one MRD-negative past 27 months, with no dose-limiting toxicity, no CRS and no ICANS. The registered primary endpoint is manufacturing feasibility and dose-limiting toxicity, so 44.4% is a descriptive secondary readout and not a pre-specified efficacy result, and the denominator mixes patients whose remission was induced with patients whose remission was maintained. What separates this from the rest of the HLA-restricted field is that the preclinical work is on the actual clinical construct ([PMID 29051183](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29051183)), with independent replication of the target across a 13-TCR panel and 15 HA-1-positive patient samples ([PMID 36900382](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36900382)). There is no published HA-1 TCR-T data at anything like 85% marrow blasts, and none in extramedullary disease.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Assuming the positive branch, meaning A*02:01 confirmed and the HA-1 mismatch running the right way, the likelihood is genuinely uncertain but the intent is curative: HA-1 sits only on blood-lineage cells, so a durable graft-versus-leukaemia remission is the plausible upside and it arrives with very little collateral. The counterweight the board took seriously is burden. The same class did nothing against active disease in the WT1 TCR-T cohort, where median OS was 242 days across 15 patients with only 2 carrying overt disease ([PMID 40473616](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40473616)), and Medigene read tumour burden as the limiting factor in their own PRAME phase 1 ([PMID 41008812](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41008812)). A negative typing result forecloses this rec outright rather than downgrading it.

### Toxicity profile

- No dose-limiting toxicity, no CRS and no ICANS across nine infused patients. That is the opposite of what a T-cell redirection product usually risks, and conservative called it the best-tolerated product in the dossier.
- One grade 4 infusion reaction in nine.
- Grade 3-5 infections after fludarabine/cyclophosphamide lymphodepletion, including Legionella and S. aureus pneumonia. Lymphodepletion in a heavily pretreated post-transplant marrow is not a low-toxicity step just because the cells are.
- Two mild grade 1-2 acute GVHD cases, attributed to immunosuppression withdrawal before infusion rather than to the TCR-T.
- No patient in the series was infused after a *second* allograft, so the interaction with HCT2 immunosuppression and engraftment is unmeasured rather than reassuring.
- No user toxicity veto applies; the list is empty.

All five personas ranked this and three dissented from putting it first, which is the disagreement that most needed resolving. Advocate ranked it first as the reason the case exists, then conceded the ordering in round 2, and conceded to one argument specifically, risktaker's tumour-burden case, not to the endpoint or guideline objections. That concession is about therapy order, not action order, and the surviving half of advocate's position is load-bearing: the HLA record pull and the HA-1 genotyping of both parties belong in the rank-1 workup running now, during induction, because a fresh donor collection cannot start until the sibling genotype returns and nothing else on this page carries that lead time. Critic's dissent persists at any rank and is not contingent on anything: n=9 on a feasibility-and-DLT primary endpoint limits what may be claimed, and nobody should quote 44.4% as an expected response rate. The mechanism-level risk is that antigen-presentation escape is unmeasured, and a six-year escape from an HLA-identical sibling graft is exactly the history that predicts it.

### Practical considerations

Of every HA-directed programme in the dossier, this is the only one that requires the prior allograft rather than excluding it, with relapse at 5% or more marrow blasts qualifying at any time post-transplant, no blast ceiling and no performance-status floor. That is why TSC-100/101 and BSB-1001 are shut to her and this is not. It is a single site in Seattle; travel is acceptable under her preferences with geography_band null, but travel plus lymphodepletion plus cytopenias needs a local backup plan written before she goes. It ranks fourth rather than first for a reason every persona including its own advocate ended up accepting: a T-cell product that cannot work at 85% blasts serves the stated request while failing the stated goal, which is cure. ## Rank 5. Sibling donor lymphocyte infusion after cytoreduction

*Curative intent without committing her to a second conditioning regimen, and off-guideline at the blast burden she currently has.*

### Evidence base

Schmid 2007 is the only row in this dossier's post-transplant relapse literature carrying a concurrent untreated comparator: 171 patients given DLI against 228 not, 2-year OS 21% +/- 3% versus 9% +/- 2%, p=0.04, n=399 ([PMID 17909197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17909197)). Confounding by indication is the central problem and the design cannot fix it, because the patients who received DLI are the ones their centres judged well enough to receive it. Within the DLI group the split is stark: 56% +/- 10% given in remission or with a favourable karyotype, against 15% +/- 3% given in aplasia or with active disease. Orti 2018 compared DLI with a second graft in 46 patients after debulking and found the univariate survival advantage of HCT2 (p=0.021) did not hold on multivariate analysis ([PMID 29526774](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29526774)); that is the only head-to-head in the set and it is too small to support an equivalence claim.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Low as she stands, moderate after cytoreduction. Her late relapse beyond five months post-HCT puts her on the favourable side of the strongest variable in Schmid's model (p<0.0001). Roughly 85% blasts and an adverse karyotype put her in the 15% +/- 3% band rather than the 56% +/- 10% one, and that is an argument for cytoreducing before the infusion rather than against the infusion. Before committing to anything that depends on donor T cells recognising these blasts, measure HLA class I and II on them.

### Toxicity profile

- GVHD is the toxicity and the mechanism at the same time. Grade III-IV GVHD would close the door on HCT2 and on any post-transplant cellular consolidation.
- Not one registry row grades adverse events, so the harm cannot be quantified for her from this evidence at all. Schmid 2007 discusses GVHD narratively with no per-term table.
- Marrow aplasia after infusion into a graft that has been in place six years.
- Cell dose and escalation schedule are centre-specific, so whoever gives it owns the toxicity, which argues for doing it where her transplant records live.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

Three personas ranked it and none dissented, which is why the score is 0.6. Every one of those endorsements carries the same caveat inside it. - **Consolidated into the ranked transplant approach:** **FLAMSA-RIC sequential conditioning** ([PMID 16110027](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16110027)) is the sequential form of rank 3 rather than a separate option. Amsacrine is not marketed in the US, FLAMSAClax ([NCT05807932](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05807932)) excludes her twice over on prior therapy and a 30% blast cap, and the reachable version is [NCT06928662](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06928662) or an institutional regimen.

Two categories are absent by design. Single-agent gemtuzumab ozogamicin is FDA-approved for relapsed/refractory CD33-positive AML including this patient, so it is routed to the Standard-of-care table rather than surfaced here. The NPM1-directed TCR-T trials ([NCT06424340](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06424340), [NCT07566585](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07566585)) ride her nominated HLA-A*02:01 restriction but target a mutant-NPM1 neoantigen no panel has reported in her, and menin inhibition targets a KMT2A *translocation* she does not have; both are target-absent and excluded rather than surfaced.

## Caveats

- **Evidence-base caveats.** Nothing this patient is actually eligible for gets above evidence tier 2b, and the board said so rather than letting rank order imply otherwise. The lead HLA-restricted lever rests on a single-arm n=9 feasibility cohort whose registered primary endpoint is manufacturing feasibility and dose-limiting toxicity. The transplant platform rests on four retrospective registries that replicate a prognostic model rather than a treatment effect, none of which adjusts for selection into a second graft or tabulates non-relapse mortality. The rank-2 regimen has no posted results and imports its estimates from trials that excluded her disease type. Two ranked rows, the CG1 engager and the CD33 degrader, have no published human efficacy at all. Tagraxofusp's headline number is cross-tumour, and flotetuzumab's TP53 signal is a post-hoc correlative read on 15 patients with no confidence interval, on a discontinued programme.
- **Compartment and biomarker dependencies.** Ranks 4 and 6 assume allele-level HLA-A*02:01 confirmation, and rank 4 additionally assumes a recipient HA-1(H)-positive / donor HA-1-negative mismatch in a sibling nobody has genotyped. Without those, both are foreclosed rather than downgraded. TP53 is unresolved in either direction and must not be down-called to wild-type on a 2%-LOD panel that reads no copy number. CD33 and CD123 are confirmed present qualitatively, and the quantitative density that predicts ADC and immunotoxin benefit is pending for both; the rank-2 trial gates on CD33 at 20% of blasts, which the current result does not establish. HLA loss on the relapsed blasts is unmeasured and would silence every A*02:01-restricted option at once.
- **What would change the ranking.** A negative HLA-A*02:01 call moves ranks 4 and 6 to non-applicable and leaves nothing HLA-restricted within scope. A successful cytoreduction to under 27% blasts moves rank 3 from a gated destination to an actionable step and reopens the DLI trial route, the MRD-setting WT1 TCR-T, and the multi-antigen products; under 10% it reopens MT-401-OTS on disease state, though the age-65 floor still bars her. Documented hepatic and albumin baselines resolve the narrowed condition on rank 8 either way. A confirmed TP53 Y220C reopens rank 11, but with an MDM2 or XPO1 partner rather than the azacitidine backbone the trial specifies, since venetoclax is spent. Confirming that a haploidentical relative exists would make rank 10 a real option rather than a phone call.
- **Re-scoping caveat.** The 0.75 efficacy weight was set before anyone documented organ function, and her own free text asks for it to be revisited once fitness is known. If the cardiac or hepatic assessment comes back poor, ranks 2, 3 and 10 are the ones that move, and a second conditioning regimen may come off the table entirely, which would promote sibling DLI and the lower-intensity CD123 options relative to everything above them.
