_12 standard options assessed: 6 to consider now, 0 behind an open gate, 2 already received._

Twelve standard options were screened for this relapsed/refractory AML-MR, and six are worth weighing now. The curative-intent backbone the guidelines carry for AML that has relapsed after an allograft is a second allogeneic transplant, with donor lymphocyte infusion from the same HLA-identical sibling as the other cellular route at that fork. NCCN and the ELN 2022 panel keep both in play for fit patients, and NCCN makes clinical trial enrollment the preferred strategy in this setting. Gemtuzumab ozogamicin earns a row on its own footing: it is FDA-approved for relapsed/refractory CD33-positive AML and these blasts are CD33-positive, so it is standard here by regulatory maturity, though its own tier-1a evidence shows no survival benefit in the adverse-risk cytogenetics this patient carries. High-dose cytarabine salvage and radiotherapy to the thigh myeloid sarcoma fill out the near-term list as a cytoreductive bridge and local control.

Two standard options are already spent: the first sibling allograft, which held remission for about six years, and the FLAG-IDA plus venetoclax reinduction the disease progressed through.

No standard option sits behind an open biomarker gate, but the transplant backbone is conditioned on a fitness and organ-function workup that has not been done. The two approved menin inhibitors, revumenib and ziftomenib, are set aside on target: this disease carries KMT2A amplification, not the KMT2A translocation the revumenib label requires, and is NPM1 wild-type.

This page is additive. It lists the standard options and does not narrow, reorder, or filter the targeted options ranked elsewhere on the case page.

## Assessed and set aside

??? note "3 option(s) screened against this patient and set aside"
    Screened against this patient and found not to apply. Listed for the audit trail.

    - **Revumenib (menin inhibitor)**: Revumenib is an approved menin inhibitor for R/R acute leukemia with a KMT2A translocation and for R/R NPM1-mutant AML, and it is worth naming precisely because this patient&#x27;s KMT2A finding is easy to misread. The karyotype shows KMT2A amplification, a copy-number gain, not the KMT2A translocation the label and its companion diagnostic require, and the disease is NPM1 wild-type. Neither indication covers this patient, so revumenib is assessed and set aside.
    - **Ziftomenib (menin inhibitor)**: Ziftomenib is an approved menin inhibitor, but its label is confined to relapsed/refractory AML with a susceptible NPM1 mutation, and this disease is NPM1 wild-type. As with revumenib, the KMT2A amplification here is a copy-number gain rather than the fusion that would create menin-inhibitor sensitivity. The drug is approved but the target is absent, so it is assessed and set aside.
    - **CPX-351 (liposomal daunorubicin/cytarabine)**: CPX-351 is the standard first-line intensive choice for the AML-MR histology this patient carries, which is why a reader would look for it. Its approval is confined to newly diagnosed disease, and this patient is relapsed and refractory after a transplant, so the histology match does not open the drug here. It is assessed and set aside on line, not on target.

## How these sit alongside the targeted options

_The ranked recommendations stay scoped to this case's targetable features, and nothing on this page changes that ranking. What this section adds is the sequencing: where a standard option and a targeted option compete for the same line, and where taking one would close the door on the other._

**Competes for the same line of therapy**

- **Donor lymphocyte infusion (DLI) from the HLA-identical sibling donor**: DLI and a second allo-HCT are the two standard cellular routes at this fork, and DLI shares the graft-versus-leukemia mechanism with the ranked HA-1 TCR-T but without the HLA-restricted antigen targeting. Choosing DLI now uses the same donor and the same window the transplant-plus-TCR-T plan depends on. (interacts with: ha1-tcr-t)
- **Gemtuzumab ozogamicin (CD33 antibody-drug conjugate)**: This is the same CD33 agent the feature-targeted ranking carries at rank 5. It appears in both tables because it is FDA-approved for R/R CD33+ AML and it also targets a feature the patient has, so the standard-of-care table logs it by regulatory maturity while the targeted ranking weighs it by mechanism. Spending the salvage slot on it is the trade-off the board flagged against the immunotherapy pathway. (interacts with: gemtuzumab-ozogamicin)

**Comes before the targeted options**

- **Second allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplant (HCT2)**: The HCT2 platform is the base on which the ranked HA-1 TCR-T consolidation would be delivered, and the ranked Iomab-B option is a radioimmunotherapy way to condition for this same transplant. The standard transplant comes first; the targeted cellular consolidation follows on it. (interacts with: iomab-b-apamistamab, ha1-tcr-t)
- **Intensive salvage chemotherapy (high-dose cytarabine-based, e.g. MEC or CLAG-M)**: Intensive salvage functions here as cytoreduction to bring the ~85% blast marrow down toward a state where the ranked transplant and cellular-immunotherapy options become deliverable. It occupies the same debulking role the ranked CD123 agents are also being weighed for. (interacts with: iomab-b-apamistamab, ha1-tcr-t)

**Can run alongside the targeted options**

- **Clinical trial enrollment**: Every ranked targeted option in this case, from the HLA and HA-1 workup through the HA-1 TCR-T and the CD123 agents, is delivered through a trial or expanded-access route, so this standard recommendation is the mechanism by which those options reach the patient rather than a competitor to them. (interacts with: hla-ha-workup, ha1-tcr-t, iomab-b-apamistamab, pivekimab-sunirine)

**Independent of the targeted options**

- **Revumenib (menin inhibitor)**: Named to close the loop on the KMT2A-amplification versus KMT2A-rearrangement distinction, a common point of confusion, since this genotype does not confer menin-inhibitor sensitivity. It does not interact with the ranked options.
- **Ziftomenib (menin inhibitor)**: The second approved menin inhibitor, listed alongside revumenib so the report is explicit that the menin-inhibitor class does not apply to an NPM1-wild-type, KMT2A-amplified genotype. It does not interact with the ranked options.
- **Radiotherapy to the extramedullary (right-thigh) myeloid sarcoma**: Radiotherapy treats the local myeloid sarcoma only and does not interact with the systemic marrow-directed targeted options; it can proceed in parallel for local control or symptom relief.
- **Best supportive care and hospice referral**: Best supportive care is the alternative if the curative transplant and cellular plan cannot be delivered or is not wanted. It does not compete with any single ranked option; it is the path taken if that pathway closes.

!!! note "Reading this report"
    An option earns a row here only if a regulator approved it for a population that includes this patient, or a major society's guideline carries it. **What makes it standard** names the endorsing body and, next to it, whether that endorsement was written for this patient's situation or for a different population. **Fit to this patient** is judged against the case profile alone, so an option the patient has already had and progressed on is marked rather than re-offered.
