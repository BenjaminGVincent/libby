## Rank 1. Comprehensive tumour DNA plus RNA panel on macrodissected dedifferentiated tissue — diagnostic gate

*One order that resolves IDH1/IDH2, TMB, MSI and CDK-pathway status, and decides whether four of the rows below exist at all.*

### Evidence base

This is an assay, so the evidence is prevalence and eligibility rather than an effect size. IDH1/2 mutations occur in 71% of dedifferentiated chondrosarcomas against 36% of conventional tumours, measured on macrodissected material ([PMID 36926116](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36926116)), which makes the pre-test probability the cleanest number in this file. The same paper cautions that the IDH-associated methylation phenotype is partially lost at dedifferentiation. Downstream, the panel is what [NCT04040205](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04040205) screens on for a qualifying CDK-pathway alteration, what [NCT02693535](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02693535) needs to assign a marketed agent, and what would answer the tumour-agnostic TMB and MSI question. Tissue choice is a real variable rather than a formality: mutant IDH1 is recurrently lost during metastatic evolution ([PMID 41299743](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41299743)).

### Likelihood of desired effect

As a gate it either opens doors or closes them, and the honest expectation is mixed. A positive IDH call is likely on prevalence and buys trial currency plus an off-label conversation, not the targeted rescue the word suggests. A qualifying CDK-pathway alteration or a TMB-high result is a much longer shot; chondrosarcoma genomes are often quiet beyond IDH. What makes the order worth ranking first anyway is that a negative panel closes four rows cleanly, and closing options honestly is worth more here than keeping them ambiguous.

### Toxicity profile

- No procedural risk. The panel rides archival diagnostic tissue.
- The only cost is time and tissue. 2-3 weeks for the comprehensive panel, 3-10 days for a targeted IDH hotspot assay, against metastatic medians of 5 to 7 months.
- Tissue is finite and several assays compete for it, which is why the block review and written allocation plan sit alongside this order.
- No user toxicity veto to hit; the veto list is empty.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

Four of five personas ranked this and all four gave it their highest confidence, which happened nowhere else on the board. The risktaker declined to rank it, arguing that the glutaminase route does not wait on an IDH result because cell-line sensitivity did not sort by genotype ([PMID 29576625](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29576625)); concensusite conceded that point directly in round 2. That objection narrows the claim rather than defeating the order. concensusite also pre-committed against the thing a positive result will create pressure for: outside a trial she would not support off-label ivosidenib here, and she preferred to say so before the report lands rather than after.

### Practical considerations

Send it with the diagnostic tissue, not after the first restaging scan. Require macrodissection of the dedifferentiated component and require the report to state which lesion was sampled. If an operative or metastatic specimen becomes available, test that too. concensusite could not read NCCN Bone Cancer v2.2025 ([PMID 40203873](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40203873)) or the ESMO-EURACAN bone sarcoma guideline ([PMID 34500044](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34500044)) beyond their abstracts, and declined to claim either lists IDH testing in the chondrosarcoma workup algorithm. No category is asserted.

### Why this rank

It sits above everything because four rows in this table cannot be scoped without it and because it is the only move on the board with no safety cost at all. Its agreement score of 0.8 is the highest here, and the gap to the next tier at 0.4 is the difference between four personas at high confidence and two personas at low.

## Rank 2. IACS-6274 (glutaminase-1 inhibitor), phase 1 NCT05039801 — make the screening call

*The one open protocol that writes chondrosarcoma into its own treatment-naive population. What is ranked is the phone call and the screening answer, not the drug.*

### Evidence base

The whole clinical base is a registration record. [NCT05039801](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05039801) names chondrosarcoma as its own Part A dose-escalation population with no subtype restriction and no prior-therapy requirement for that group, which is rare enough that it is worth a call on its own. Underneath sits one paper: glutaminase expression rose with tumour grade across 120 cartilage tumours (p=0.001, highest in high-grade disease) and all four tested agents had activity in 10 chondrosarcoma cell lines, but sensitivity did not track IDH1/2 status and there is no in-vivo chondrosarcoma arm ([PMID 29576625](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29576625)). No glutaminase inhibitor has a reported response in any chondrosarcoma patient anywhere. The null half of that paper is doing real work here: because sensitivity did not sort by genotype, a pending IDH result does not gate this protocol, which concensusite conceded explicitly in round 2.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Low, and the advocate said so in her own row rather than leaving it to the critic. Expected benefit from escalation-phase monotherapy is small, and she could be assigned a dose level nobody involved expects to work. The upside is being the escalation patient the drug turns out to work for. What is likelier to matter is the screening answer itself, which costs a phone call and arrives in days.

### Toxicity profile

- No published grade 3+ profile in any chondrosarcoma patient. Adverse-event incidence is the trial's own primary endpoint.
- Entry requires ECOG 0-1 with no deterioration over the preceding fortnight. **This is where the assumed ECOG of 2 bites**: the assumption has to be replaced by a measurement, and the measurement has to come back better, before a screening visit is worth the trip.
- Single-site protocol in Houston. Her travel tolerance was never discussed.
- No user toxicity veto to hit, because the veto list is empty and inferred. The conservative's objection is precisely that an empty list is not consent.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

The risktaker ranked the drug at 2; the advocate ranked the call at 4; concensusite endorsed the advocate's version and said plainly that it was the row she should have written herself. The critic dissented against the risktaker's rank and qualified the advocate's, on the same ground both times: one cell-line paper, no in-vivo chondrosarcoma arm, no clinical result for the class in this disease. The critic's phrasing is worth keeping, that ranking the phone call rather than the drug is the defensible version of this. The conservative's broader objection lands here too: everything below chemotherapy on the risktaker's slate carries zero chondrosarcoma clinical results, and this is one of them. Mechanistically the risk is that glutamine dependence in this tumour is inferred from grade-linked expression in dishes and has never been tested in an animal carrying chondrosarcoma.

The call should happen this week, because the deliverable is a screening answer and first-line anthracycline does not wait for it. concensusite added the operational link that the centre screening her for this protocol is the same referral that opens expert pathology, the periacetabular surgeons and every other trial screen, so the two acts are one booking. It ranks above abemaciclib and pembrolizumab on availability rather than on evidence, which is worth being blunt about. It is the only seat in this screen a treatment-naive chondrosarcoma patient is not automatically outside, and her stated preference is trials-forward. On evidence alone it would rank below both. ## Rank 3. Abemaciclib on NCT04040205, pre-positioned for the post-anthracycline line

*The cleanest trial design in the screen, sitting behind three untested gates.*

### Evidence base

No CDK4/6 inhibitor has a published chondrosarcoma efficacy result. [NCT04040205](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04040205) is a two-step single-arm phase 2, n=44 across a mixed sarcoma population, with dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma named as its own eligible histology and CDKN2A/B loss qualifying as the biomarker. The preclinical case is two papers: CDK4 expression correlated with metastasis and worse prognosis in human samples, and palbociclib cut intraosseous chondrosarcoma burden in vivo with the G1 arrest the mechanism predicts ([PMID 30808351](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30808351)); p16 overexpression and CDK4 knockdown both inhibited growth, with pRb-pathway alterations tracking progression from enchondroma ([PMID 18624751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18624751)). The critic's caveat on the first of those is load-bearing: it never reported CDKN2A status of its lines and carries a 2023 figure correction ([PMID 36803557](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36803557)), so the biomarker inference does not rest on it.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Low to unknown, and the honest framing is the critic's: this ranks on the quality of the process rather than on any signal, and the two are not the same thing. Even a positive readout from a single-arm n=44 will not separate drug effect from having selected an indolent CDK-altered subset. Against that, the target is one of the recurrent alterations in this subtype and the trial gate is applied before enrolment rather than after the fact.

### Toxicity profile

- Class profile: diarrhoea, neutropenia, fatigue and venous thromboembolism. No chondrosarcoma-specific adverse-event table exists anywhere in this dossier.
- ECOG 0-1, stricter than most protocols in this screen. **The assumed 2 excludes her as written.**
- A 14-day post-surgical window with no significant wound-healing problems, more forgiving than the usual 4 weeks if stabilisation goes ahead.
- No user toxicity veto to hit.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

Both the risktaker and the critic ranked it and neither dissented, which for this pair is itself informative: they arrived from opposite directions and landed on the same protocol. The risktaker ranked the biology, the critic ranked the design and explicitly refused the biology. Neither argued for delaying first-line treatment to chase the seat. The mechanism-level risk is that CDK4/6 inhibition is cytostatic rather than cytotoxic, so Rb-null or CDK-independent clones keep cycling despite target engagement, which is also why the protocol's central Rb IHC gate exists.

### Practical considerations

Recruiting. Three gates stand between her and a seat: at least one prior anthracycline for the dedifferentiated group, which first-line treatment would satisfy; a qualifying CDK-pathway alteration plus Rb positivity by central IHC, neither ordered; and ECOG 0-1. The move now is the rank-1 panel plus Rb IHC, because a biomarker gate cannot be cleared retroactively.

### Why this rank

It shares an agreement score of 0.4 with ranks 2, 4 and 5. It sits below the glutaminase call only because that seat exists today and this one requires a completed line of therapy first. It sits above pembrolizumab because the target rationale is coherent and the trial gate is pre-specified and centrally read, where the checkpoint case rests on subgroups of four and five patients.

## Rank 4. Pembrolizumab monotherapy, second line, conditional on TMB-high or MSI-high

*Thin but genuine disease-specific signal with the best characterised safety of anything non-cytotoxic here.*

### Evidence base

Add up the entire prospective checkpoint experience in chondrosarcoma and it comes to five patients. SARC028 treated 5 evaluable chondrosarcoma patients in its bone cohort and 1 responded, inside a cohort whose primary endpoint failed at 2 of 40, and the primary report never subtypes the responder ([PMID 28988646](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28988646)). The retrospective five-centre cohort adds 1 PR among 5 pembrolizumab-treated patients ([PMID 37174084](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37174084)), and there is one case report of a CR sustained 24 months ([PMID 36465334](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36465334)). The on-label route is different evidence entirely: KEYNOTE-158's TMB-high analysis reported ORR 29% (95% CI 21-39) against 6% (95% CI 5-8) in non-TMB-high patients ([PMID 32919526](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32919526)), across ten tumour cohorts, none of them sarcoma. The biomarker context is prevalence and nothing more: PD-L1 positive in 9 of 22 dedifferentiated tumours against 0 of 119 conventional ([PMID 27312065](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27312065)).

### Likelihood of desired effect

Low. The critic put the arithmetic plainly: the 1-of-5 and 1-of-4 numbers everyone cites are subgroups of four and five patients whose confidence intervals run from roughly nothing to roughly everything. The on-label TMB route is a low-prior bet, since TMB at or above 10 mut/Mb appears in about 3.9% of sarcomas and chondrosarcoma is typically TMB-low. It costs nothing extra to test on a panel already going out, and it should not be planned around.

### Toxicity profile

- SARC028 bone cohort, grade 3 or worse: anaemia 14%, lymphocyte count decreased 12%, prolonged aPTT 10%, thrombocytopenia 7%. Serious adverse events in 12% of the bone cohort.
- Immune-related serious events across the trial included adrenal insufficiency, pneumonitis and nephritis.
- No user toxicity veto to hit. The conservative ranked it partly on the strength of a decade of post-marketing irAE algorithms every oncology unit already runs.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

Both personas who ranked it called their confidence low, and the critic refused the framing that usually travels with it: the attribution of the SARC028 responder to dedifferentiated histology is secondhand in later sources and traces back to nobody. The mechanism-level objection is the one that persists regardless of biomarker: macrophages are the dominant immune population in this disease, a high CD68+/CD8+ ratio was independently prognostic at p<0.01 ([PMID 31956474](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31956474)), and depleting T cells accelerated growth in the immunocompetent rat model while depleting CD163+ macrophages slowed it ([PMID 28344871](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28344871)). The advocate qualified the conservative's row on a different axis, objecting that an assumed ECOG was being used to pre-decide monotherapy over the combination on her behalf. concensusite declined to rank any checkpoint route at all, because MSI, TMB and PD-L1 are untested and the tumour-agnostic label is what would carry it.

### Practical considerations

Off-label unless the panel returns TMB-high or MSI-high, in which case it moves onto the tumour-agnostic label for previously treated disease and belongs on the Standard-of-care table rather than in this ranking. MMR IHC is the orthogonal read on the MSI call and runs in 2-5 days off about four unstained slides. PD-L1 should be read on a section containing the dedifferentiated component, with the clone and scoring system recorded explicitly.

### Why this rank

Same agreement score as ranks 2, 3 and 5. It sits below abemaciclib because the CDK protocol has a pre-specified central biomarker gate and a named histology where this has neither, and above TAPUR because it carries an actual, if tiny, disease-specific response signal where TAPUR carries no efficacy estimate at all.

## Rank 5. TAPUR (NCT02693535), pre-positioned for the line after first-line chemotherapy

*An ECOG 0-2 seat she may clear as written, wholly conditional on a panel that chondrosarcoma genomes often decline to fill.*

### Evidence base

There is none specific to her, and that is the point of the row. [NCT02693535](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02693535) is a non-randomised multi-cohort basket registry of marketed targeted agents assigned by genomic match, roughly 4200 patients across cohorts, with objective response or stable disease at 16 weeks as the endpoint. No chondrosarcoma cohort result exists. The critic's qualification should travel downstream with the row: this is an access decision carrying no efficacy estimate at all, and nobody should read the NCT number as though it were data.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Unquantified in the strict sense. Two conditional probabilities stack: that the panel finds an actionable alteration, and that a marketed agent matched outside its approved histology does anything in this disease. Chondrosarcoma genomes are often quiet beyond IDH, so she should hear plainly that this seat may never materialise.

### Toxicity profile

- Whatever the matched marketed drug carries. Known agents with existing management algorithms rather than a novel mechanism, which is the conservative's reason for ranking it ahead of the antigen-directed platforms.
- No user toxicity veto to hit.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

Nobody dissented; the critic qualified. There is no mechanism to argue with until a match exists, so the only counter-productive vector is opportunity cost, spending second-line time on a search that finds nothing. The advocate's practical point cuts the other way: at 181 US sites geography probably does not bite, and in a disease this fast the second-line decision arrives in months, so pre-positioning now is what a trials-forward stance looks like in practice.

### Practical considerations

Recruiting. The fitness bar is what makes this row worth its slot: ECOG 0-2 is among the very few in this entire screen her assumed status clears as written. The line criterion is soft rather than numeric, phrased as no longer benefiting from or having no available standard treatment, which realistically places it after first-line anthracycline. Any matched agent would be used outside its approved histology.

### Why this rank

Last of the four rows sharing an agreement score of 0.4, on the efficacy-weighted tie-break: her preferences file weights efficacy at 0.8, and this is the only row of the four that carries no efficacy estimate whatsoever. It stays ahead of the surfaced rows because it is open, she probably qualifies, and two personas ranked it.

## Rank 6. Sunitinib plus nivolumab, off-label at first progression

*The only checkpoint dataset in which a dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma responded, bought with a treatment-related grade 5 pneumonitis.*

### Evidence base

IMMUNOSARC is a multicentre single-arm phase 2 in advanced progressing bone sarcomas, n=40, of whom 14 had chondrosarcoma and 4 of those were dedifferentiated ([PMID 39540661](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39540661), [NCT03277924](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03277924)). Six-month PFS by central review was 42% (95% CI 27-58) against a >15% threshold, which the trial met. Overall ORR was 5%, median PFS 3.8 months, and one of the two partial responses was a dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma. The registered eligibility names chondrosarcoma and dedifferentiated chondrosarcoma outright, which almost no other checkpoint protocol does. The subgroup is 4 patients and the trial is closed.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Low. The critic's reading is the one to plan against: a 1-of-4 subgroup is not a rank-3 argument, and the 42% six-month figure is largely stable disease in a mixed-histology cohort of four diagnoses. The risktaker's counter is real too, that this is the single dataset in the whole dossier where a patient with her exact histology responded to a checkpoint-containing regimen, and that PD-L1 in this disease is confined to her subtype at 9 of 22 positive against 0 of 119 conventional ([PMID 27312065](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27312065)).

### Toxicity profile

- **One treatment-related grade 5 pneumonitis among 40 patients**, and 17% discontinued for toxicity.
- Grade 3 or worse: neutropenia 15%, hypertension 15%, anaemia 12.5%, ALT/AST elevation 12.5%, pneumonitis 2.5%.
- Her toxicity veto list is empty, and the conservative's dissent is about exactly that. An empty inferred list is not consent to a fatal-toxicity risk she has never been shown, in a woman whose ECOG 2 has never been measured by anyone.
- Off-label combination, so a payer fight should be expected.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

Two personas dissented and a third piled on, which is the widest opposition on the board. The critic dissented on toxicity, noting that the risktaker listed both halves of the ledger and ranked it third regardless. concensusite dissented on guideline fit, called it the clearest off-label move on the board, and made the distinction that marketed means buyable rather than endorsed; her closing position was that if a checkpoint route stays on the table at all, the conservative's biomarker-conditional monotherapy is the version a label could stand behind. The conservative's round-2 critique aimed at the risktaker's slate as a whole and named this row specifically. The mechanism argument against it is the same one that hangs over rank 4 and is stronger here because the combination adds an anti-angiogenic: T-cell depletion accelerated growth in the immunocompetent rat model, so PD-1 release may favour the dominant macrophage compartment rather than the intended effector one.

### Practical considerations

The trial is closed and required both ECOG 0-1 and progression within the preceding 6 months, so this would be off-label prescribing of two marketed drugs rather than a protocol seat. That is what makes it available at first progression without waiting for anything, and it is also what removes every protocol safeguard.

### Why this rank

Its agreement score of -0.2 is the lowest of any row still carrying a narrative, and it sits below TAPUR for that reason. It stays in the ranked section rather than being flagged and set aside because the risktaker's reading of her stated preference is a real reading of a real sentence she said, and the reader is entitled to see what was considered and rejected rather than only what survived.

## Caveats

- **Evidence-base caveats.** There is no randomised comparison in this dossier for any intervention against any endpoint, which the critic put on the record in round 1. Two of the six ranked rows have no clinical result in this disease at all. The prospective checkpoint experience in chondrosarcoma is five patients. The B7-H3 prevalence figure everyone cites comes off a 24-specimen tissue microarray with a Merck-employed author. Where per-term toxicity tables sit behind paywalls, the critic said so rather than assuming their contents; the EURO-B.O.S.S. table ([PMID 33990016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33990016)) is one nobody on this board has read.
- **Two numbers the board refused to settle, and this page will not settle either.** The first-line response rate in her histology brackets 9% (modern five-centre cohort, [PMID 37174084](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37174084)) to 20.5% (44-patient subset treated 1988-2011 with local response assessment, [PMID 24099780](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24099780)), and the p=0.04 attached to the higher figure compares dedifferentiated against conventional histology rather than testing whether chemotherapy helped anybody. The critic and the conservative would plan against the 9%; the advocate holds that she should hear 9 to 20.5 with the reason the ends disagree, because choosing an end on her behalf is a decision she has not been in the room for; concensusite quoted both plus 11.5% for conventional. The second number is EURO-B.O.S.S.'s 29% five-year OS in metastatic patients, which is 23 patients at p=0.12 from an uncontrolled, fitness-selected cohort in which 18 of 57 never completed 6 of 9 planned cycles. The critic's structural point stands: 63% of that cohort reached surgical complete remission and the significant split in the paper is the remission one (49% versus 23%, p=0.004), so the survival tail may track resectability rather than the regimen. Chemotherapy still ranks first with all five personas, and it cannot be sold on the 29%.
- **Compartment and biomarker dependencies.** Ranks 13 through 16 assume an IDH1 or IDH2 mutation and foreclose without one. Rank 3 assumes a qualifying CDK-pathway alteration plus Rb positivity by central IHC. Rank 4 assumes a TMB-high or MSI-high call. Rank 7 assumes a high B7-H3 H-score from a stain that is not a standard clinical order and has no target-validator row. Ranks 21 and 22 assume an allele-level HLA class I type nobody has drawn. Every one of those is unordered today.
- **The fixation question the board could not resolve.** The advocate argued that stabilising the acetabulum may be what raises her *measured* ECOG and reopens the doors the assumption is closing. The critic called that the most useful idea anyone put on the table and also noted that no row in this dossier supports it: nothing measures performance status before and after periacetabular stabilisation, and no cohort here counts surgical morbidity at all. The conservative's counter is that the ECOG can as easily be spent as raised, since recovery runs in weeks against a 5-to-7-month median and a badly picked operative date plus 4-week washouts closes trial doors instead of opening them. concensusite conceded in round 2 that the operation she had ranked was fixation only, not resection, and that her dual label had let resection inherit fixation's justification. On the oncologic half, the SEER interaction test found no survival association for resecting the primary in dedifferentiated, high-grade disease ([PMID 30762691](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30762691)), which withdraws the benefit claim without proving futility; the same paper's all-comers HR of 0.481 is not the estimate that applies to her.
- **What would change the ranking.** A measured ECOG of 0 or 1 would open ranks 2 and 3 outright and is the single highest-leverage measurement in the case. A negative IDH1/IDH2 result forecloses ranks 13 through 16 and leaves the rest of the table standing. A qualifying CDK-pathway alteration with Rb positivity would move abemaciclib above the glutaminase call, since the tie between them broke on availability rather than evidence. A B7-H3 H-score well above the subtype median would move the CAR-T row from surfaced to ranked; a median-range score should kill it, which is what the risktaker said himself.
- **Re-scoping caveat.** The 0.8 efficacy weight, the empty veto list and the trials-forward flag are all inferred defaults built on one sentence she said. If a goals-of-care conversation returns something other than maximal-options, most of this table reorders and some of it comes off entirely.
- **The best-evidenced item on this board is not in this table.** The critic's observation is worth carrying: the ASCO palliative-care recommendation ([PMID 38748941](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38748941)) is the strongest piece of evidence anyone put in front of this board, and it sat at rank 5, below four things built on retrospective cohorts. It is routed to the Standard-of-care table, which is a filing decision and not a judgement about its weight.
