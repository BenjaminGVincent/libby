## Research question

## Target validation paths

One block-release request decides most of what this case can become. Nearly every open question runs on two archival specimens, the September 2024 uterine resection and the 2026 pulmonary core, and the orders should go out together on a single request naming both. The INI1/SMARCB1 stain and the tissue SMARCB1 sequencing decide whether tazemetostat exists as an off-label route or the whole SWI/SNF branch closes; the HRR/HRD panel decides whether olaparib plus temozolomide (the NCI protocol 10250 regimen, NCT03880019) is winnable as an off-label authorisation at progression; the mismatch-repair and TMB reads decide whether tumor-agnostic pembrolizumab or dostarlimab is reachable; the RNA fusion panel decides whether a TRK inhibitor is even conceivable; and the antigen stains behind her confirmed HLA-A\*02:01 decide whether IMA203 (NCT03686124) and its neighbours are worth a screening call. The clinic half, an echocardiogram, baseline labs and a scored ECOG, gates the planned doxorubicin plus trabectedin induction itself. Stains return in 3 to 5 days once a block is in the laboratory and tissue sequencing in 2 to 3 weeks; the slow step is administrative, because the 2024 block sits at an outside institution and nobody clinical currently owns the release request. A fully negative panel would still be a productive result: closed branches, a fitness baseline the planned chemotherapy needs anyway, and a clean record of what was looked for. The conversation about standard care from there belongs to the treating team, not Libby.

### SMARCB1

The plasma G344Afs\*13 frameshift is a hypothesis, not a finding, and the prior against it is steep: 170 of 170 uterine smooth-muscle tumours retained SMARCB1 in the one consecutive IHC series in this histology (PMID 34271067). The decision-grade read is INI1/SMARCB1 IHC (BAF47/SNF5 antibody, clone 25 or MRQ-27) showing complete loss of nuclear staining in tumour cells against an intact internal control; that is the result the tazemetostat basket accepted, with biallelic molecular loss as the alternative where the stain is equivocal (NCT02601950). Order the stain and the tissue SMARCB1 sequencing with copy-number and deletion calling at the same time rather than in sequence, because an equivocal stain with no molecular backup is a two-to-three-week delay this first-line window cannot absorb. Stain both specimens separately: the shedding compartment is the lung, and a metastasis-acquired event would be invisible on the 2024 block. Expectations should stay modest either way, since even a confirmed loss buys a 9 percent response rate in the INI1-negative non-epithelioid basket cohort (PMID 41882006). Germline SMARCB1 sequencing is a reflex, ordered only if tumour loss is confirmed; a reported schwannomatosis-associated uterine leiomyosarcoma (PMID 26001331) makes the question real but not urgent enough to front-load the counselling burden.

### HLA-A\*02:01 and the cancer/testis antigens

The typing is done and it is the expensive half of the gate: allele-level HLA class I typing confirms A\*02:01. What is missing is every antigen result that would pair with it. PRAME is the most plausible positive of the three and both programs behind it, IMA203 (NCT03686124) and brenetafusp (NCT04262466), enrol on the same two-part gate she is halfway through; the operative caution is that IMA203 screens on the sponsor's IMADetect RT-qPCR, not IHC, so a local stain (clone EPR20330) is a one-slide screen whose negative closes the axis cheaply but whose positive still has to be reproduced on the assay that decides entry. MAGE-A4 is scored to a protocol threshold, at least 1+ staining in at least 10 percent of cells in SPEARHEAD-1 (NCT04044768), and the approved product is licensed in synovial sarcoma, not this histology; smooth-muscle sarcomas expressed MAGE-A4 in only a minority in the one multi-subtype series carried here (PMID 40152485). NY-ESO-1 earns one slide on the same block cut and no more, since expression runs low across sarcoma subtypes and the enrolling master protocol (NCT03967223) is currently histology-restricted away from her disease. The resistance check rides along free: ask the sequencing laboratory to report allele-specific HLA copy number, because somatic loss of A\*02:01 would undercut the entire class at once, however strong the antigen stain.

### Unmeasured molecular landscape

Plasma non-detection at unstated tumour fraction settles nothing, and that sentence covers most of this case. One validated comprehensive tissue panel is the single order that answers several questions at once: an MSI call, a TMB in mutations per megabase, the HRR gene set with a genomic-instability score, RNA fusion calling, and whether TP53 Q52\* is in the tumour at all. Four mismatch-repair stains (MLH1, PMS2, MSH2, MSH6) on the block already being cut are the fast orthogonal read, and either route closes or opens the tumor-agnostic PD-1 indications (NCT02628067, NCT02715284). The NTRK question needs RNA rather than DNA, because plasma capture misses breakpoints in the large NTRK introns, and a spindle-cell tumour once signed out as benign is exactly where an NTRK-rearranged mesenchymal neoplasm hides. The HRR/HRD result is the one that opens a branch rather than closing one: uterine leiomyosarcoma has a recognised HR-deficient subset including BRCA2 events (PMID 37143137), and olaparib plus temozolomide produced a 27 percent response rate with median PFS 6.9 months in pretreated disease (PMID 37467452), so this is the panel result an off-label authorisation would lean on. TP53 attribution deserves its own order: paired buffy-coat sequencing or tissue interrogation of the Q52\* position separates tumour DNA from clonal haematopoiesis, which contributed features to 53.2 percent of cfDNA mutations in matched sequencing (PMID 31768066), and the answer calibrates how much weight the rest of the plasma report, the SMARCB1 call included, can carry. The RB1/ATRX/MED12 backbone with its IHC surrogate panel (sequencing concordance kappa 0.941, PMID 36788080) corroborates a diagnosis whose primary was once called benign and supplies the co-alteration context trial screens ask about; the Rb read in that panel is also what the abemaciclib study (NCT06498648, Rb-intact required) and the REC-617 study (NCT07633756, Rb-loss required) will each want to see before screening. A germline multigene hereditary panel runs on the same draw as any other blood work; the slow parts are counselling and insurance, not the assay.

### uLMS histology and fitness

Three orders are essential before cycle 1 and none involves tumour tissue. A transthoracic echocardiogram with LVEF and strain, because the LMS-04 schedule carries doxorubicin into the cardiotoxicity surveillance band and no cardiac reference value exists (PMID 35835135; PMID 27918725; PMID 36017568). A metabolic panel with bilirubin, transaminases and creatine kinase plus a CBC, because trabectedin's dose-limiting hepatotoxicity needs a baseline to be read against, and her count needs an altitude-adjusted haemoglobin reference: at roughly 8,500 feet a sea-level-normed CBC will make real chemotherapy-induced anaemia look normal. And a clinician-scored ECOG with Karnofsky equivalent, because the 1 on file is an intake assumption, and nearly every protocol in scope, from the PRAME TCR-T program (NCT03686124) to olaparib plus temozolomide (NCT03880019), sets a 0-to-1 ceiling at screening. The FNCLCC grading parameters behind the phrase high-grade are a report request rather than a new assay; the reference-laboratory diagnosis itself is settled and needs no second opinion.

### Bilateral pulmonary pattern

Nobody has measured the pulmonary reserve of a lifetime never-smoker living at 8,500 feet with infiltrative bilateral metastases. Baseline pulmonary function tests with DLCO, plus resting and ambulatory oximetry recorded at her own altitude and labelled as such, are the only reference against which on-treatment dyspnea can be attributed to progression, adaptation or treatment; several cell-therapy protocols in scope set saturation floors of 90 percent room air, one at 92, written for sea level. A pre-treatment CT of chest, abdomen and pelvis does double duty: it establishes whether the infiltrative pattern yields RECIST v1.1 measurable target lesions at all, better discovered before a screening slot than during one, and read against anything shot in the 2024-to-2026 interval it bounds the growth kinetics of disease that went untreated for roughly 23 months. PD-L1 with matched clone and scoring, plus CD3 and CD8 infiltrate density on the lung core, informs rather than decides: it is the one way to learn whether the inflammatory radiologic impression corresponds to anything in the tissue.

### ER/PR <1%

The result is confirmed and it forecloses the endocrine branch, but the report on file does not say which specimen produced it. This is a records question first: if the under-1-percent call came from the 2026 lung core, nothing more is needed; if it came from the 2024 primary, a repeat stain on the metastasis costs two slides and makes sure the door is genuinely shut, since hormone-receptor expression can differ between a uterine primary and a distant metastasis.

### The 2024-2026 interval

A 6.0 cm myometrial mass removed as a presumed benign fibroid may have been morcellated, and morcellation of an unrecognised leiomyosarcoma disperses tumour through the peritoneum and worsens outcomes (PMID 28209496; PMID 26646120). The staging picture is currently pulmonary only, and whether peritoneal disease must be actively excluded rather than assumed absent turns on an operative note nobody has yet had reason to re-read. Retrieving the September 2024 operative report and gross description is free, and it decides how wide the staging imaging field needs to be drawn.

### Biomarkers measured, but not to decision resolution

Two results on file are real measurements that cannot carry a decision as they stand, and both are plasma non-detections. The NTRK call is a DNA assay's silence at unstated tumour fraction, which the survey behind this report declined to read as a negative; the assay that closes it is an RNA-based fusion panel on tissue, agnostic to where in the intron a breakpoint sits. The homologous-recombination call is the same problem one step worse: the plasma panel reported no BRCA1/2 alteration but cannot produce a genomic-instability score at all, so HR status is open rather than proficient, and closing it takes tissue NGS across the HRR genes with an HRD score plus a germline panel on blood. The distinction matters: nobody doubts these targets were looked for, the looking was just done with an instrument that cannot rule them out. Six further handoffs, mismatch repair, TMB, MAGE-A4, PRAME, NY-ESO-1 and B7-H3, were forwarded because they were never measured on any assay, not because an existing number fell short; each has a matching workup order above, and no handoff was dropped. Reading either group as negative would be the mistake; the record supports pending, nothing stronger.

## Rank 2. Olaparib + temozolomide (NCI Protocol 10250 regimen in uterine leiomyosarcoma), post-induction, with tissue HRD/HRR testing ordered now

*The strongest lineage-matched targeted signal in the file, one line away, at the cost of a second consecutive 75% grade 3/4 neutropenia.*

### Evidence base

NCI Protocol 10250 was a 22-patient single-arm multicentre phase 2 in advanced uterine leiomyosarcoma after at least one prior line, and it met its prespecified primary endpoint: 5 of 22 responses within six months against a design powered at 93% with alpha 0.06. Overall best objective response rate was 27% (6 of 22; the primary-endpoint figure was 23%), with median PFS 6.9 months (95% CI 5.4-NE) ([PMID 37467452](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37467452), [NCT03880019](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03880019)). This is single-arm with open-label response assessment, rated ROBINS-I moderate, and the randomised confirmation in the same histology is fully accrued and unreported ([NCT05432791](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05432791)). The RAD51 split that everyone quotes — median PFS 11.2 versus 5.4 months, p=0.05 — was exploratory in 18 assayed patients and is not a result. Entry did not require homologous-recombination deficiency.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Moderate, and the most defensible "moderate" on this page, because the response rate was generated in her exact histology rather than borrowed from a neighbouring one. If her tissue returns homologous-recombination deficiency, the exploratory subgroup suggests she sits in the better half; if it returns proficiency, the same subgroup suggests roughly five months of disease control. Neither of those numbers should be quoted to her as a prediction. What is solid is 27% and 6.9 months in 22 women with her disease.

### Toxicity profile

- Grade 3/4 neutropenia 75% and thrombocytopenia 32%, managed with dose modification. No treatment-related deaths reported.
- The sequencing problem the critic named: this marrow will have just absorbed LMS-04's 80% grade 3-4 neutropenia and 47% thrombocytopenia. Two consecutive regimens in that range is not the same proposition as either one alone.
- Polycythaemia at 8,500 ft will confound anaemia assessment throughout, and no altitude-referenced baseline haemoglobin exists.
- No stated toxicity veto is engaged, because the veto list is empty and unasked. That is not consent.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

No dissent and no veto; four of five personas ranked it. The disagreement was about *how* to hold it rather than whether. The critic and the concensusite both rested it on the prespecified endpoint and refused the RAD51 crutch — the critic gave the concensusite explicit credit for being the only persona who did that unprompted. The risktaker took the subgroup bet and labelled it exploratory in the same breath, which the critic conceded is how a subgroup ought to be carried. The mechanism-level risk is mundane rather than exotic: overlapping myelosuppression forcing dose reductions that cost the PARP exposure the regimen depends on.

### Practical considerations

Both trials of the combination are closed to accrual, so the live route is an off-label prescription of two approved drugs, and that prior-authorisation case is much stronger with a tissue HRD or HRR result than with a plasma panel that settles nothing. Guideline carriage is thin: the ESMO clinical practice guideline predates the trial, and the concensusite could not verify NCCN listing this run because NCCN blocks automated access — which is exactly why the guideline chair placed it fourth on its own slate rather than second. Entry required a prior line, so nothing on this branch is reachable in the first-line window except the tissue testing, and the tissue testing is rank 1.

### Why this rank

It ties rank 3 on board agreement at 0.8, and it wins the tie on evidence. Olaparib + temozolomide has a met primary endpoint and a real response rate in her histology; the abemaciclib seat has a blood thymidine kinase time course. What keeps it from being unambiguously first among the therapies is that it cannot be given yet and its toxicity lands on a marrow that will already be depleted.

## Rank 3. Abemaciclib + gemcitabine and docetaxel (NCT06498648, MD Anderson randomised phase 1/2), Rb-intact required

*The only investigational seat that survives LMS-04 instead of competing with it — and the only one whose worst case inside the trial is guideline care.*

### Evidence base

There is no efficacy readout, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. The phase 1 primary endpoint is a blood thymidine kinase activity time course; the phase 2 randomises against the gemcitabine/docetaxel backbone with a PFS endpoint ([NCT06498648](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06498648)). What the design buys is a floor. The control arm is a regimen with a dedicated first-line uterine phase 2 behind it, objective response rate 35.8% (95% CI 23.5-49.6) in 42 chemotherapy-naive women and median PFS 4.4 months ([PMID 18534250](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18534250)), plus a randomised phase 3 keeping that number honest, since GeDDiS found the doublet no better than doxorubicin at 24-week PFS and trending the wrong way ([PMID 28882536](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28882536)). Abemaciclib itself has essentially no efficacy data in leiomyosarcoma.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Unknown for the add-on and moderate for the backbone. Single-arm response rates in uterine leiomyosarcoma have repeatedly outrun their randomised comparisons, so 35.8% should be read as what the control arm might deliver rather than what she should expect. The honest framing is that this seat buys her a randomised chance at an unproven addition, with guideline chemotherapy as the downside — which is a genuinely different bet from every other trial in this dossier.

### Toxicity profile

- Backbone, from the uterine phase 2: grade 3 anaemia 24%, grade 3 fatigue 17%, grade 3-4 neutropenia 17%, grade 3 thrombocytopenia 9.5%, and **one possibly related grade 4 pulmonary event in 42 patients** — a number that reads differently in a never-smoker with infiltrative bilateral lung disease at altitude.
- From GeDDiS: grade 3+ neutropenia 20%, febrile neutropenia 12%, fatigue 14%. No treatment-related deaths.
- Abemaciclib contributes diarrhoea and neutropenia on top, and the combination's tolerability is uncharacterised outside this study's own phase 1 portion. The conservative named that gap specifically.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

No dissent and no veto. Four personas ranked it directly and the concensusite endorsed it inside its own gemcitabine/docetaxel row, calling it the trial-seat answer it could defend from the guideline chair. The critic ranked it fourth while naming the real cost: enrolment forfeits the first-line LMS-04 window, and that trade should be said out loud before consent. There is also a mechanism question nobody has answered — CDK4/6-induced G1 arrest can antagonise S-phase-dependent gemcitabine, and this study's phase 1 portion is the first place that interaction gets characterised in this combination.

### Practical considerations

Single site in Houston, and the advocate's dissent applies squarely: an empty `modality_constraints` list is not the same as a woman at 8,500 ft agreeing to fly to Texas during first-line treatment. Nobody has asked her. The sequencing fact is the one to act on now — the phase 2 admits systemic-treatment-naive patients and the disqualifier is prior gemcitabine, so LMS-04 preserves this seat while an off-protocol gemcitabine/docetaxel prescription destroys it. The conservative pushed back usefully on the purist version of that rule: if the echo or the pulmonary workup rules out full-dose doxorubicin and the Houston screening clock loses the race against symptomatic lung disease, off-protocol gemcitabine/docetaxel delivered at home is the defensible choice, and the rule should say so rather than hold a fallback hostage to a seat.

### Why this rank

Same agreement score as rank 2, placed below it because its own drug has no efficacy data. It sits above rank 4 by a full 0.8 on agreement, and for a concrete reason: this is the one seat where the downside inside the trial is standard care, which is not true of a phase 1 cell therapy.

## Rank 4. IMA203 / IMA203CD8 PRAME-directed TCR-T cells (NCT03686124), sponsor screening started in the first-line window

## Rank 5. In vivo lung perfusion with doxorubicin plus metastasectomy (NCT02811523, Toronto), as post-induction consolidation

*The one option in this dossier aimed at cure rather than control, and the one whose anatomic premise her current imaging may defeat.*

### Evidence base

Two separate evidence bases sit under this row and they should not be blended. The perfusion technique itself is a phase 1 with acute lung injury as its registered primary endpoint, resting on preclinical work ([NCT02811523](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02811523), [PMID 26952295](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26952295)); there is no published human efficacy series. The survival numbers everyone quotes belong to metastasectomy: 55.4% five-year overall survival in the uterine-sarcoma subset of a 319-case Japanese multicentre registry selected on a disease-free interval over 12 months ([PMID 37063124](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37063124)), 36% five-year survival after complete resection in the 5,206-case international registry ([PMID 9011700](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9011700)), and median overall survival 70 months in a leiomyosarcoma subset undergoing repeated resection ([PMID 21867989](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21867989)). All retrospective, all heavily selected.

### Likelihood of desired effect

Genuinely unknown, and the uncertainty is about her rather than about the numbers. Her roughly 23-month interval from primary resection to metastatic diagnosis clears the registry's disease-free-interval selector comfortably. Her current disease distribution does not: bilateral, multifocal, and read as infiltrative. If induction converts that to countable resectable nodules, the registry population becomes hers and this becomes the highest-ceiling option on the page. If it does not, the row evaporates.

### Toxicity profile

- Thoracotomy morbidity, plus acute lung injury — which is the trial's registered primary endpoint, meaning the safety question is what the study is asking rather than what it has answered.
- No published human safety series in this population.
- Unmeasured pulmonary reserve at an 8,500 ft baseline in a lifetime never-smoker with infiltrative bilateral disease. No pulmonary function tests, no DLCO, no oximetry.
- Cumulative doxorubicin above 450 mg is exclusionary. Six full LMS-04 cycles at 60 mg/m² approach that.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

The conservative dissented on toxicity, and the objection has three parts, each of which stands on its own: a phase 1 perfusion protocol with no safety series in this population; a patient with unmeasured pulmonary reserve asked to hold a thoracotomy seat; and a phase 3 regimen having its dose intensity bent around a phase 1 exclusion. The concensusite landed at qualified, right shelf and wrong door, noting that ESMO carries complete excision of resectable lung metastases as standard local treatment in selected patients while the perfusion vehicle has no society carriage anywhere. The sharpest single objection is the conservative's on selection: 55.4% five-year survival belongs to patients who reached *complete* resection of *countable* nodules, so her disease-free interval transfers to her and the survival figure does not. The risktaker's counter also stands, and I think it is underweighted elsewhere in these proceedings: the resectability call belongs to a thoracic surgeon looking at her films, not to a radiation-first default made from a board table. The critic added a related warning that applies to the alternative rather than to this row: zero grade 3+ events across two retrospective, heavily selected SBRT series says something about how retrospective toxicity gets captured, not about a measured safety margin.

Single site in Toronto, so this is an international referral. Two questions decide whether the row is real, and both can be asked this week: whether the study accepts US-resident patients, and how the 450 mg cumulative-doxorubicin exclusion is counted against a planned six-cycle induction. The US counterpart, pulmonary suffusion at a single Buffalo site, carries no prior-line requirement at all and is surfaced separately below. Tied with rank 4 at 0.0 and placed below it because holding this seat is not free: it constrains the anthracycline dose of the regimen the board unanimously supports, while a PRAME screening call constrains nothing. It sits above rank 6 by 0.4 because one persona actively wanted it and one dissented, rather than one wanting it and three refusing.

## Rank 6. Ivonescimab (PD-1 x VEGF bispecific), MSK single-arm phase 2 in leiomyosarcoma (NCT07516925)

*The only seat reachable before cycle 1 by explicit protocol language — and the one four of five personas refused.*

### Evidence base

Zero published sarcoma efficacy for the molecule. The credibility rests entirely on HARMONi-2, where ivonescimab beat pembrolizumab head-to-head on PFS in PD-L1-positive NSCLC: HR 0.51 (95% CI 0.38-0.69), median PFS 11.1 versus 5.8 months, at an interim analysis of a China-only trial with immature overall survival ([PMID 40057343](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40057343)). The MSK study is a 20-patient single-arm phase 2 with an objective-response endpoint ([NCT07516925](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07516925)). Against that sit three trials in or adjacent to her disease: GOG-0250, a randomised double-blind placebo-controlled phase 3 showing bevacizumab added nothing to first-line gemcitabine/docetaxel in chemotherapy-naive metastatic uterine leiomyosarcoma, PFS HR 1.12, p=0.58 ([PMID 25713428](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25713428)); SARC028, where the leiomyosarcoma cohort went 0 for 10 while other histologies carried the signal ([PMID 28988646](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28988646)); and a dedicated uterine leiomyosarcoma nivolumab study stopped at stage 1 for futility, 0 of 12, median PFS 1.8 months ([PMID 28440953](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28440953)).

### Likelihood of desired effect

Low. Both mechanisms in this bispecific have already been tested in this disease and lost, one of them in a randomised trial designed to answer exactly this question. The bispecific-cooperativity argument — that co-targeting produces something neither axis achieves alone — is real preclinical biology and has no clinical support in any sarcoma. HARMONi-2 says the molecule is active in lung cancer. It says nothing about hers.

### Toxicity profile

- Grade 3+ treatment-related adverse events 29% versus 16% for pembrolizumab in NSCLC, the increment mostly VEGF-class effects; grade 3+ immune-related events 7% versus 8%.
- VEGF-class hypertension and bleeding risk, in a patient with bilateral pulmonary lesions.
- Repeated cross-country travel from 8,500 ft during first-line treatment, which is a toxicity of a different kind and is not on any adverse-event table.
- No stated toxicity veto is engaged. The empty list is the problem, not the permission.

### Counter-productive mechanisms / dissent

Three personas dissented and the advocate alone ranked it, which is where hard rule 3 bites: an option the advocate flagged as preference-aligned against three objections stays visible with caveats rather than being dropped. The critic's convergence verdict belongs on this row and cuts both ways. Four of five refused the seat off the same three papers, so that is one evidence base read four times and nobody should bank the unanimity as extra weight — **but the refusal survives the correction**, because GOG-0250, SARC028 and the uterine futility stop are three mutually independent trials and one of them is a randomised null in this exact disease. The risktaker's dissent is the most pointed, because the risktaker is the persona who normally takes thin-evidence bets: thin evidence with a live mechanism is a risk worth taking, and a first-line window spent on two mechanisms this disease has already beaten is not. The concensusite qualified rather than dissented, noting no guideline in any region carries the drug or its class in sarcoma.

### Practical considerations

The eligibility text is the whole reason this row is here: patients who decline standard first-line systemic therapy are eligible, and the ECOG ceiling is 2 rather than 0-1. That makes it reachable now, before LMS-04 starts, by explicit protocol language rather than by inference — the only investigational row in this dossier of which that is true. Seven sites, all in the MSK New York and New Jersey network, six flagged Limited Protocol Activities. Travel is not supplied with the study drug, so ask about patient travel assistance at first contact rather than after the first invoice, and ask which visits could happen at her home institution. Prior PD-1, PD-L1 or VEGF-pathway exposure is exclusionary, which means starting pazopanib first would close this permanently.

Last among the ranked therapies at -0.4, and the distance from rank 5 is the distance between one dissent and three. The advocate did not argue it was better; it argued that the question of whether she wants it has never been put to her, and that the seat closes when LMS-04 starts. Both of those are true, and neither makes it a stronger option than the four above it.

## Caveats

- **Evidence-base caveats.** Rank 2 is a 22-patient single-arm phase 2 with open-label response assessment and an unreported randomised confirmation. Rank 3 has no efficacy data at all for its investigational component; its control arm's 35.8% uterine response rate comes from a 42-patient single-arm study, and the randomised comparison that keeps it honest ran the other way. Rank 4 is a 41-patient phase 1 with no patient of her histology in it. Rank 5's survival numbers come from retrospective registries whose selection criteria her current disease does not meet. Rank 6 has zero sarcoma data. The exploratory RAD51 subgroup on rank 2 (11.2 versus 5.4 months, p=0.05, n=18) is not a finding and should not be quoted as one, and the 4-patient BRCA2 series behind rank 39 is tier 4.
- **Biomarker dependencies.** Ranks 7, 24, 25, 26 and 40 through 42 assume INI1/SMARCB1 loss confirmed by IHC on tumour tissue; a retained stain forecloses all seven, and the prior for a retained stain is high. Rank 3 assumes intact Rb by IHC; rank 8 assumes the opposite. Rank 4 assumes PRAME positivity on the sponsor's own RT-qPCR, not on a local stain. Rank 2's authorisation case assumes a tissue HRD or HRR result. None of these has been measured, and **plasma non-detection at unstated tumour fraction is not a negative for any of them.**
- **The performance-status caveat cuts across everything.** ECOG 1 was assumed at intake and never scored. Every effect estimate on this page comes from a trial that enrolled ECOG 0-1, so an unmeasured score is an applicability defect inside the numbers rather than a screening formality. If the measured score is 2, a substantial fraction of what this board argued over disappears.
- **What would change the ranking.** A confirmed INI1 loss on tissue lifts rank 7 out of the thin-evidence flag and makes the SWI/SNF branch real, though still modest. A tissue homologous-recombination-deficiency result moves rank 2's confidence up materially and strengthens its off-label authorisation. A thoracic surgeon calling the bilateral infiltrative pattern resectable moves rank 5 up sharply, because nothing else here has its ceiling. Publication of the accrued randomised olaparib + temozolomide phase 2/3 would either confirm rank 2 or deflate it. Her answering the trial-and-travel question either way would settle the tension the advocate has flagged on ranks 3, 4 and 6.
- **Re-scoping caveat.** Every value in `preferences.json` is an intake default and the file says so. If she states a toxicity veto, declines travel, or turns out to want gentler therapy than an efficacy weight of 0.7 implies, this ranking changes shape rather than order — and if goals of care turn out to be something other than the maximal-disease-control intent the planned induction implies, several of these rows stop being appropriate to offer at all.
